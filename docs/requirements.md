# Product requirements

## Product goal

Help one adult learner build deep, usable Japanese for everyday life, starting with kana knowledge and no kanji. Organize study around practical venues and conversations, an open and growing set that starts with the restaurant, the shop counter, and meeting people. Make grammar, discourse, and pragmatic choices explicit. Each lesson teaches core ideas and checks understanding: words, short explanations with a few distinct checks, a conversation and a concise mixed review. Repeated retrieval and noun-substitution drills belong mainly in Practice; lessons have no exercise-share quota. Learning happens through constant testing: retrieval, closed-book passage recall, delayed checks, and transfer to new venues, rather than repeated exposure to basics. Every exercise is answered by tapping or dragging; there is no audio and no typing. Listening passages might be added in the future, but they are not planned. Kanji is learned along the way as written words from those venues. Optimize the primary experience for a phone.

Current quantities and prerequisites are generated in the [content inventory](content-inventory.md). The [curriculum audit](curriculum-audit.md) distinguishes outstanding teaching and pacing improvements from implemented behavior.

## Learner profile and design assumptions

- Goal: everyday life in Japan, with sustained conversation and reading.
- Schedule: 20–30 minutes most days; interruption and resumption must be easy.
- Existing knowledge: hiragana and katakana. No assumed kanji or formal grammar terminology.
- Explanations: plain English initially, with Japanese examples and optional Japanese reflection.
- Input: tap or drag only. Multiple choice, choose the particle, fill a gap from choices, put tiles in order, JLPT-style ★ ordering, matching, sorting into groups, true/false, and word-bank dialogue completion. No typed answers or speaking practice. Audio is not implemented and is added only at the owner’s request, to a few selected exercises at most. The app trains reading, recognition, and sentence building toward the goal above; it does not currently test listening or speaking.
- Initial target language: polite everyday speech. Recognize common service expressions (marked "recognize" in a lesson's word list) before drills ask the learner to build with them. Introduce plain forms, casual speech, and register through explicit bridges. Practices may show casual and more natural variants of what a lesson taught. A lesson never relies on them, and teaches a form again itself when it needs it ([learning loop](learning-loop.md)).
- Motivation: understanding and growing independence. No lives, punitive streaks, or speed pressure.
- Pacing target: retention before expansion. Retention-based slowing and arc consolidation weeks are specified, not enforced by the current app; lesson prerequisites and due checkpoints govern access ([curriculum.md](curriculum.md#pacing-model)).

The prototype combines several pages. A separate page is justified only when it serves a distinct learner task.

The sidebar (and the phone bar) holds Learn, Practice, Library (Reading, Dictionary, Flashcard decks, Progress & backup) and Kanji, with the Course guide link below. Kanji took the Editor's place on 2026-10-23; an old `#studio` or `#editor` link, and the old `#practice/kanji-cards`, replace themselves with `#kanji`, leaving no history entry. Library opens on Reading; a bare `#library` link replaces itself with `#library/reading`, leaving no history entry. The course word lists, Core words and Restaurant extra, are filters of Library → Dictionary since 2026-10-23, when the Word lists section went: an old `#library/words` link replaces itself with `#library/dictionary`, leaving no history entry, and the Dictionary opens on Core words. There is no Today page: an old `#today`, `#retention`, `#phrases` or `#readings` link replaces itself with Learn, as `#lessons` does, leaving no history entry. The documented flow is the [learning loop](learning-loop.md). Lessons on Learn come first. Practices (Practice) and books (Library → Reading) build on lessons and on each other, until a branch needs a new lesson. The loop is implemented for Learn, Practice (2a and 2b) and Library → Reading (Phase 3, 2026-10-23: books open on their own prerequisites under their home lessons and are completed by a clean attempt); what remains is content. It covers how practices, books and garden stones are revealed and unlocked, and what completing each one means. It also says that optional content is welcome, for niche and less general situations, as optional practices that can unlock optional books. There are no optional lessons and no elective stones. Until it is built, the rows below describe the app as it is.

### Progressive unlock, no redirects

The app lays out what the learner can do now, in place, and lets them reach completion; finishing a lesson, checkpoint or practice set unlocks the next thing. A topic page’s lesson list is the plan: each row is done (a check), in progress (a ring with its whole-number percent correct), open (an arrow), or locked where it sits, as its greyed title with no reason line (2026-10-29: the order says what comes first). Above the list, under the heading, a small spaced-caps *You will be able to* subheader and the topic’s outcomes as a compact checklist are the page’s only summary: no summary paragraph and no About block (final task, builds on, links). A due checkpoint is a row on the topic page directly above the first lesson it locks. A finish screen reveals what unlocked (*Unlocked: Ordering drinks*, *Checkpoint 2*), or just that the lesson is finished, with one way back to the topic, where the new rows are waiting. Pages never send the learner elsewhere: no “Go to X”, “Next: X”, “Continue the course” or “Start today’s reviews” buttons. The only cross-page controls are back links, the sidebar, the topic arrows and the in-place rows themselves, and a locked row never links to the thing it waits on.

### Reviews are parked

The Today run, Retention page, recovery callouts and review-debt gate remain parked. The small due-lesson list in Practice and due-passage list in Reading (2026-10-25) were removed on 2026-10-29 at the owner's direction; reviews stay parked. A due passage still opens on its delayed recall set on its own page. Completion and access never depend on review results. Reading checks and flashcards remain in place. This heading is retained for older links.

### The Kanji page is the round

Since 2026-10-23 the sidebar's Kanji entry holds one thing: today's round of nine kanji words on paper. A pass draws a circle on the card, a miss fades it, and that is all the page says; it has no counters, labels, pills, subheaders or lists. The owner's reason: the area is there to be used for a few minutes a day, not read, and kanji progress is already visible in the text itself, where each word shows in the stage the learner has reached. What the learner has collected, and how far each word has come, lives in Library → Dictionary with the rest of their words. The six decisions behind the kanji model are in [curriculum.md](curriculum.md#kanji-growth).

## Phone interaction requirements

- Design first for 360–430px widths. Maintain a usable layout at 320px and 200% zoom; allow reflow instead of horizontal scrolling.
- Use large targets, with approximately 44px as the minimum interactive dimension. Node circles remain tappable even with three in a row.
- Keep four primary destinations in thumb-reachable bottom navigation. Put lower-frequency design and data tools inside the Library.
- Selecting a node opens a panel that slides horizontally and fills the available screen. Back restores the field’s scroll position. Previous/Next buttons and horizontal swipes move only between open topics. Reduced motion switches panels immediately. No automatic vertical field scrolling.
- Keep a clear return path. Long lesson sections save responses continuously.
- Lessons and runs are paged, never one long scroll. A lesson shows one screen at a time: the opening page, named in the contents by the lesson's own title (title, goal, scene, track and length, the Focus and any reading check), the words table eight rows a page (“Words 1 of 2”), each point, each drill item under its set's header (title with a “3 of 6” counter, instruction and any kanji introduction; no solved example or Example toggle), the conversation, each まとめ item (items a wrong reason brings back join the end as pages of their own) and the finish page. While items are left, the finish page lists the sets that still have them, each jumping to its first item left. A dock pinned to the panel's bottom edge holds only Back on the left and Next (Start on the opening page) on the right. Next is open on reading pages; on a Words page it waits until every word on the page has been tapped, and the contents cannot skip past it meanwhile (2026-10-29); on an item it waits, greyed out, until that item is solved (right, or answered in a checkpoint), and a swipe waits with it, so the learner moves on only by finishing each item. The toolbar holds the back link, a thin progress track and the position (“15 / 47 ⌄”), which opens the contents (“Contents, page 15 of 47” to screen readers): a list under the toolbar, right-aligned (on a phone, inset 16px from both screen edges) and kept inside the screen, of every section, a practice set with ✓ or “n left”, that jumps to one. Each page names itself in its own header. A horizontal swipe (as on topic pages) goes back or on, never from a control or a drill's drag area. A page change scrolls to the top, moves focus to the page's heading and slides briefly from its side, with no motion under reduced motion. The address does not change per page, so browser Back leaves the lesson; the page is kept for the session, and a fresh open starts on the opening page with nothing answered, after the furthest answered item once it is solved (on it while not), and on the finish once every item is solved. Do the exercises again restarts on the first practice page. A checkpoint run (one question a page, then its finish), the restaurant review and the extra vocabulary round (the words eight to a page, their note, one question a page, then the finish) page the same way. A run's question page has one compact heading, the run's name over “Question 3 of 9”, and its intro line shows on the first question only. On a phone the frame and cards are compact so that most item pages fit above the dock without scrolling; a word-bank conversation scrolls under its word box, which stays just above the dock, while a long point, a long conversation, a long Words page and items with a long scenario or parts line may still scroll a little. A locked lesson, checkpoint or Practice round never renders a pager, so no page reaches its content.
- Exercises use taps and drags only; the on-screen keyboard never opens during practice. Every drag has a tap equivalent: tap a tile, then its place in the line; tap a card, then its group; tap a gap, then a bank word. A drag never starts from a vertical scroll gesture.
- Tiles, choice chips, match items, and ○/× buttons keep the ~44px minimum, wrap onto new lines instead of scrolling sideways, and leave room for furigana. A placed tile can be removed with one tap.
- Japanese text needs generous line spacing for readings. Never cram furigana, or the kanji notes of Kana + kanji above, into a fixed-height line.
- Do not encode state only in color. Pair colors with labels, badges, and descriptions.
- Respect safe areas and reduced-motion preferences. Decorative field texture must not compete with labels.
- Learn’s garden, Practice’s water garden and Reading’s table share one frame over their art: a topline with a kana word, a caps label and a hint (みず PRACTICE · Choose a koi, ほん READING · Choose a book, かんじ KANJI · Choose a card; Learn's にわ LEARN has no hint, at the owner's request); subheaders in small spaced capitals (garden rows, koi categories, shelf topics); a status pill under each item that has states (koi and books, not stones); and a one-line footnote. The stones, koi, book covers and their titles stay as painted. All four scene panels, Kanji’s board too, take Practice’s size: at most `--scene-max` (1100px) wide and centred in the page; Reading’s table may widen past it, up to the screen’s width, only to keep one shelf’s books on a line, and each shelf starts its own line.
- The Kanji board is a sheet of aged paper folded into nine squares, each card's word brushed on it in vertical writing, while the trial and the word collection keep the textbook font.
- The Decks tray on lessons and readings sits on the panel's bottom edge at every scroll position, with no gap of page showing beneath it: directly above the bottom navigation on phones and clear of the safe area. It stays in the page flow, so the final controls are always reachable above it, and the panel keeps its usual bottom padding while the tray is hidden. In a lesson it is part of the dock, one compact row of deck chips above Back and Next, and shows only on the pages with words to drag (the words table, the conversation) and only once the learner has a deck (a word card's Add to deck makes the first); while it is hidden it takes no drag.
- Feedback that a tap opens (a drill's verdict, Answer saved, a reason check, a reading question's or word quiz's verdict, a kanji trial step) slides into view: the panel scrolls smoothly, or at once with reduced motion, just far enough to show it with the Next row after it, clear of the dock and below the sticky top bar, never past the feedback's top. Feedback already on screen, and answers shown again when a page reopens, stay put.
- On phones the top bar (Show all kanji) and a page's sticky toolbar (back link, progress, page count) run the full width of the screen, like the dock, so scrolled content never shows beside them.
- Production requirement: downloads, tapping and dragging tiles, cards, and gaps, and screen-reader reading of ruby must be tested on actual iOS and Android devices.

## Required system states

Distinguish loading, failed content load, a locked lesson with its reason, untouched lesson, saved attempt, unanswered or incorrect quiz items, unlocked expansion, outline-only content, and an invalid imported file. Never silently show fabricated lessons after a failure. A storage failure must explain that work cannot persist and offer export; the app footer shows that warning in place of “Progress is saved in this browser.”

## What a full product must add

Remaining catalogue titles should be checked for an unmet can-do before becoming full lessons; some repeat language already taught in context. All implemented lessons, practices, passages and unseen texts still need Japanese educator review (the Phase 4 content has had only an AI review pass, recorded in the workspace's `content-staging/phase4/batch-*/REVIEW.md`). Five unseen texts and cumulative checkpoints are already built. Full-course arc gates, retention-based pace control and wider transfer coverage remain future work; see the [curriculum audit](curriculum-audit.md).

Speaking and typed output are outside the product. Synthetic-voice dictation, typed answers and readings, typed conversation replies, voice roleplays, and speaking practice are removed. Audio is not implemented but stays open: the owner may decide to add it to a few selected exercises or passages. Until then nothing plans or builds audio. If it is added, it needs recordings with permission and appropriate rights (more than one speaker for listening passages), each exercise is still answered by tap or drag, and its evidence stays separate from reading evidence. Tap and drag results show reading comprehension, recognition, and controlled sentence building. The interface must not present them as evidence of listening, spontaneous speech, or free writing.

Persistent accounts and sync should be added only after the learning loop works. The first prototype intentionally saves on one browser and provides backup. No microphone use, personal-profile collection, or external analytics is implemented.

## Scope boundaries

The first usable release should fully develop foundations and the core stages of the three level-1 venues: At the table, Everyday shopping, and Meeting people (Arcs 0–1). It needs every core lesson authored as a reviewed textbook unit, two transfer scenarios per venue, closed-book passages with delayed recall, and a reviewed capstone. The restaurant is the reference node, built most deeply first and copied by later nodes, but it does not gate the others. Remaining nodes stay in the curriculum catalogue, hidden in the garden until their playable lessons can be reached and are built in the balanced arc order in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md). Avoid promising a complete path from beginner to advanced before that content exists.

No placement claim should equate a custom level or completed node with JLPT, CEFR, or a guarantee of fluency. Align to external descriptors later through an explicit assessment process.
