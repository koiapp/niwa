# Structured content and helper tools

## Files

- `curriculum.json`: profile, level definitions, map rows, topic nodes, references, `kanjiChecks` (check sentences and authored look-alike pairs), and `path`, the Learn path ([Learn path](#learn-path)). Current content quantities are in the [generated inventory](content-inventory.md).
- `lessons.json`: textbook lessons (Words, Points, drills, conversation and mixed review), passages with recall data, unseen transfer texts (`transfer`), vocabulary, `kanjiReadings`, checkpoints and general/optional unit-shaped practices (`practices`). Lessons, passages, unseen texts and practice conversations carry explicit word occurrences. [Current counts, homes and prerequisites](content-inventory.md) are generated from these records; Kanji cards is a separate page, not a practice set.
- `checks.mjs`: authored transfer questions (one per lesson), `transferChecks`. Passage questions live with each passage in `lessons.json`, and practice questions with each practice (`lessons.json` `practices`, authored in `tools/practices/`).
- `exercises.mjs`: shared, dependency-free textbook-unit logic used by the app, the seed, the validator and the tests: Words-table entries, the eight tap and drag formats, counted units, grading, seeded shuffles that never start in answer order, per-unit results, completion and first-try score, closed-book lesson review selection and outcome, and unit checks (`checkUnit`).
- `kanji-words.mjs`: dependency-free kanji word logic used by the app (`jp()`, since Phase 2), `exercises.mjs`, the validator and the tests. It covers the word index, the longest-match resolver of `{written|reading}` tokens, met/points/known state (points are passed Kanji card trials, one a day), the staged display and the strict/lenient state reader ([Kanji words](#kanji-words)), and the daily round of Kanji cards and its saved state, with `recordTrial` for the page's trials ([Kanji cards](#kanji-cards)). Since 2026-10-28 its card-closing rules and mastery threshold take the kanji policy's values, defaulting to today's ([Kanji policy and course](#kanji-policy-and-course)).
- `kanji-cards.mjs`: the Kanji page's rules, pure and dependency-free, used by the app and the tests: the opening at 9 met words, the collection of met words (listed in Library → Dictionary), today's grid of 9, a card's trial, done and replay, and a trial's ledger entry ([Kanji cards](#kanji-cards)). Since 2026-10-28 every rule takes a kanji policy (default `DEFAULT_POLICY`), and the trial's question kinds are a registry, `QUESTION_KINDS` ([Kanji policy and course](#kanji-policy-and-course)).
- `kanji-policy.mjs`: the kanji policy and course list, pure with no imports, used by the app, `kanji-cards.mjs`, the validator and the tests: `DEFAULT_POLICY` (the personal profile's rules), the strict and lenient readers of `lessons.json` `kanji.policy`, `kanji.course` and `kanji.properNouns`, their problem lists, the course rank and the course gates ([Kanji policy and course](#kanji-policy-and-course)).
- `content-rules.mjs`: the content rules, pure with no imports, used by the validator and the tests: `DEFAULT_RULES` (the limits the validator used before, kept by the personal profile), the strict and lenient readers of `curriculum.json` `rules` and their problem list, `rulesProblems` ([Content rules](#content-rules)).
- `recall.mjs`: shared, dependency-free passage recall logic used by the app, the seed, the validator and the tests: the review queue for lessons, passages and harvested phrases (1/3/7/14/30-day intervals), recall attempt records and evidence stages, book completion (`completesPassage`, and `completeBook`, which writes a book's first completion into `loop`), saved-state migration to version 3 (`migrateState`, with the learning loop's record `loop` and its reader `readLoopState`, and the normalisation of the lessons that became practices, `CONVERTED`), evidence spans and highlighting runs, delayed-set ordering, phrase review questions, recall content checks, and saved-state/backup reading.
- `loop.mjs`: the learning loop's runtime rules, with no DOM, clock or imports, used by the app, `tools/loop-check.mjs` and the tests: the practice sets and books in content (`practicesOf`, `booksOf`), references, lesson parts included (`parseRef`, `refMet`, `refStarted`), item states (`itemState`), what opened between two snapshots (`opened`) and what a lesson's newly solved parts opened (`partsOpened`), where an item lives (`homePlace`), the route guard for practice sets and books (`loopRoute`) and a lesson's parts (`lessonParts`) ([Runtime rules](#runtime-rules)). Covered by `tests/loop.test.mjs`.
- `session.mjs`: Today: one capped, interleaved run across every due review (lesson reviews, word cards, kanji readings, passage recall, harvested phrases), the review-debt gate for new lessons, and saved-state/backup reading. Parked since 2026-10-22: no page runs it or consults the gate, but the module, its tests and its state stay ([Today runs](#today-runs)).
- `transfer.mjs`: unseen transfer texts, used by the app, `session.mjs`, the seed, the validator and the tests: the source-language contract checks (`transferProblems`), completion (`completesText`; a text opens on its `requires` through `loop.mjs`), first reading, attempts with deferred feedback and evidence lines, retries, the one delayed re-ask, per-word and per-pattern evidence, and saved-state/backup reading ([Unseen transfer texts](#unseen-transfer-texts), [Unseen texts](#unseen-texts)). The texts are authored in `tools/transfer-passages.mjs` and shipped in `lessons.json` `transfer`.
- `build.json`: the build stamp, `{"profile", "built", "commit"}` (the profile id, the ISO time of the build, the short SHA of niwa HEAD or `null`). `tools/build.mjs` writes it into each profile's `dist/` on every build, so git ignores it (`dist/build.json`, and `course/dist/*`), and the Android shell packs whatever the last build left. - `vocabulary.mjs`: shared, dependency-free vocabulary logic used by the app, the validator and the tests: record and occurrence validation, text segmentation around references, idempotent discovery, dictionary field preferences, search, word sources (lessons, passages and practice sets, Words tables included), per-word reading evidence and furigana fading, reading-check items and reading choices, near-miss naming (`readingSlip`), kanji checks in new sentences (content validation, look-alike index, due selection, interleaved sessions and transfer records), and saved-state/backup reading.
- `flashcards.mjs`: shared, dependency-free deck logic used by the app, the validator and the tests: deck create/rename/delete with undo, membership, contextual collection (source-preserving, deferred during questions) and the hold-and-drag state machine, ordered card faces and presets, kana fallback, deck-independent word review scheduling (prompt identity, due dates, bounded sessions, support and exposure tracking), and saved-state/backup reading.
- `learn.mjs`: dependency-free garden states, teaching-order topic lists, a topic's planned titles (`plannedTitles`), reachable checkpoint placement and strict topic/lesson routes. These use the existing path and progress, with no new saved-state fields. Covered by `tests/learn.test.mjs` and the progression/navigation browser tests.
- `pages.mjs`: dependency-free page model of paged lessons and runs, used by the app and the tests: a unit's pages in order, its sections for the contents list, page labels and keys, sets with items left, and where a fresh open resumes ([Paged lessons and runs](#paged-lessons-and-runs)).

Routes: `#field` (Learn), `#node/<id>`, `#lesson/<id>`, `#checkpoint/<n>`, `#practice` and `#practice/<id>` (each unit practice in `lessons.json` `practices`; a practice of another kind has no page yet and lands on `#practice`), `#library/<section>` (a bare `#library` replaces itself with `#library/reading`, without a history entry), `#passage/<id>`, `#unseen/<id>`, `#deck/<id>`, `#recall/<id>`, `#kanji` (the Kanji page) and `#guide`. - `tools/practices/<id>.mjs`: one file per practice, each exporting `practice`: a unit-shaped set with its prerequisites and home lesson, `newWords` where it adds vocabulary, and, for the six converted from lessons, their conversation. ## Node shape

Required: stable `id`, `title`, `icon`, integer `level`, `prerequisites`, `summary`, `outcomes`, `stages`, `expansions`, `capstone`, `transfer`, and `status`. Each stage has `title`, `focus`, and `lessons` (planned titles). Every prerequisite refers to an existing node. The prerequisite graph must be acyclic.

`status: "outline"` means no full teaching sample is available. `status: "sample"` means a limited number is available, not that the whole node is finished. The view matches full sample titles to their planned titles; keep them identical or replace this prototype convention with explicit lesson IDs in a later schema.

A planned title that a practice has (`lessons.json` `practices`, matched by `title`) is not a planned lesson either: a lesson that became an optional practice keeps its catalogue title in its stage, and the topic page's **Planned lessons** list skips it (`plannedTitles` in `learn.mjs`). So Restaurants keeps *Without onions, please* and Shopping *Sizes and colors* in their outlines without listing them as planned. Dating and relationships is `outline` since 2b: its four teaching samples are practices.

## Learn path

`curriculum.json` `path` orders the playable lessons into one recommended sequence. It is authored with `core()` in `tools/seed-curriculum.mjs`; the audit and the reason for each placement are in [curriculum.md](curriculum.md#the-learn-path). Node prerequisites stay topic-level catalogue data; `path` works with lesson IDs. Since 2b (2026-10-22) the path is version 2:

```json
{"version": 2,
 "core": ["f01", "f02", "…"],
 "requires": {"f01": [], "f02": ["f01"], "f03": ["f01", "f02"]}}
```

- `core` is the sequence of every placed lesson, in order. Every lesson is core: optional content is optional practices, never lessons ([learning-loop.md](learning-loop.md#optional-content), decision 14). It supplies checkpoint lesson order; optional path.checkpoints names the cumulative boundaries.
- `requires` lists each placed lesson's direct prerequisites, an empty list included: earlier lessons in `core` only, never a part, a practice or a book.
- Version 1 also had `electives`, optional topic groups each anchored after a general lesson (r05, s03 and d01–d04). They became optional practices in 2b, and `membership`, `resolvePath` and `electiveSubsets` went with them.

`tools/validate.mjs` fails (through `pathProblems` and `sequenceProblems` in `tools/curriculum-path.mjs`) on a missing path, another version or another field (`electives` included); an empty `core`; an unknown lesson or a lesson without a unit (outline titles cannot be placed); a lesson placed twice; a `requires` entry that is missing, names an unplaced lesson, repeats a prerequisite or names the lesson itself; a lesson that requires a later lesson; and a prerequisite cycle. A playable lesson that is not placed is a warning.

### Course progression

`dist/progression.mjs` holds the Learn path's runtime rules. It is dependency-free: it reads `path`, the lessons, `completed`, a `started` test (`lessonStarted` in `session.mjs`) and `state.progression`. Since 2b there are no electives: the path is one sequence.

- **Lesson access (2026-10-22).** A lesson is open when it is finished, started, or every path prerequisite is finished (`missingPrerequisites` is empty) and no due checkpoint stands in front of it. Otherwise it is locked, by its unfinished prerequisites or the due checkpoint; on its topic page it is the greyed title alone, not a link, with no reason line (2026-10-29: the order of the rows says what comes first). The rule is `lessonLock(path, id, {completed, started, checkpoint})` in `progression.mjs`, fed the due checkpoint from `dueCheckpoint`; `openLessons` lists the open lessons in path order so a finish can compare before and after. Both are unit-tested in `tests/progression.test.mjs`. Topic pages show each lesson's state in place, and a lesson's finish screen reveals what went from locked to open; neither offers a next step. This replaces the review-debt gate (`lessonAccess` in `session.mjs`, now parked) and the earlier rule that every lesson row stays open for browsing.
- `nextStep(path, …)` returns `{kind, lesson, started, position}`. `kind` is `resume` (the lesson in progress), `core` (the first unfinished lesson in path order whose prerequisites are finished) or `review` (nothing new is left). `position` is `{coreDone, coreTotal, coreEnded}`. No page offers its answer as a next step any more; it stays exported and tested. Outline lessons and lessons without a unit are never recommended. The same inputs always give the same answer.
- Completions in any order are kept: nothing is rewound, and each lesson is judged on its own `requires`. `eligibleLessons` lists what could start now (at most one lesson); `missingPrerequisites` names what a lesson still needs; `topicStep` gives a topic's suggestion (the course's own step when it is in that topic, else the topic's earliest ready lesson on the path, else its earliest unfinished one with what it needs); topic pages no longer show a suggestion. A lesson row is a link only while its lesson is open (Lesson access, above).
- `progression` in state is `{version: 1, active, left}`. `active` is the unfinished lesson last opened to learn (not a closed-book review); finishing it clears it. `left` is `{lesson, core}`: the lesson the learner chose to leave and how many lessons on the path were finished then. Version 1 of the path also stored the elective choice here, `electives` and `acknowledged`; they are gone.
- **Saved state and backups.** `readProgressionState` reads the field on load (lenient: malformed values fall back to the defaults) and on restore (strict: a malformed field rejects the backup). In both, `electives` and `acknowledged` are dropped whatever they hold, so saves and backups from before 2b stay valid, and an `active` or `left` lesson that is unknown (or, for `active`, already finished) is cleared. A lesson that became a practice is cleared from both before that, by the state normalisation ([State](#state)). Saves and backups from before this field start empty. Export includes the field as stored.

### Learn garden and topic lists

Learn's garden (`.field`), Practice's water garden (`.practice-river`), Reading's table (`.reading-table`) and the Kanji board (`.kanji-board`) share one size, `--scene-max` (1100px) on `:root` in `styles.css`: each is at most that wide and centred. Reading's table is `max-content` between that size and the panel's width, so it widens only to keep its widest shelf's books on one line.

`stoneState(node, path, lessons, opts)` uses only playable, placed lessons. Any open lesson opens its stone; otherwise, every direct prerequisite of at least one lesson must be started for an unnamed outline. Other stones are hidden. Completed, recorded-started and legacy active lessons retain access. `gardenRows` removes hidden stones and empty rows while retaining authored row order and artwork identity.

The confirmed opening is Basic sentences → Restaurants: `core` begins f01, f02, f03, r01. r02, i01 and s01 require r01, so Meeting people and Shopping cannot open ahead of Restaurants on a fresh save. These are authored entry-order requirements in `path.requires`, not separate stone gates. Dating and relationships has no playable lesson since 2b, so its stone stays hidden.

`topicLessonGroups` orders lessons by `pathOrder` before grouping consecutive stage labels. Unwritten titles are in a closed disclosure, without the titles practices have (`plannedTitles`, [Node shape](#node-shape)). `learnRoute` guards topic and lesson bookmarks before rendering or marking a start; arrows and swipes use the same open-topic set. A flashcard source into a locked lesson is plain text and withholds its sentence.

`checkpointLocation` chooses the first held lesson in an open topic. If none exists, the checkpoint follows its finished boundary lesson, including a final checkpoint. Unlock notices include newly opened stones. Since 2b this Learn implementation uses path version 2 and state version 3.

### Checkpoints

`dist/checkpoints.mjs` adds cumulative closed-book checkpoints at authored boundary lessons. If path.checkpoints is absent, the legacy five-lesson CADENCE applies. The app shows the earliest open checkpoint in the topic list.

- **Boundaries.** Optional path.checkpoints lists unique lesson IDs in core order, ending at the final lesson. Current anchors: i01, h01, r09, ba01, s06, pl02, bc01, tr03; blocks: 5, 6, 5, 5, 5, 5, 5, 5. tr00 belongs to block 2, preserving all previous boundary IDs. Without this field, boundaries fall every five lessons and a short trailing block has none. blockOf and requiredLessons use the actual boundary. All lessons through it must be finished; checkpoints open in order and saved completion remains.
- **Order of the course.** `dueCheckpoint` is the earliest unfinished checkpoint once it opens. While it is due, every lesson after its boundary that is neither started nor finished is locked (a greyed title, no reason line), and the checkpoint is a row (*Checkpoint N*, with Start or Resume) on the topic page that holds the first lesson it locks, directly above that lesson. If no held lesson has an open topic, the checkpoint follows its completed boundary lesson instead, including at the end of a course; finished checkpoints are not listed there. A lesson in progress and completed or started lessons stay open. The checkpoint never waits for reviews. Finishing it, whatever the score, unlocks those lessons: its finish screen reveals them, with one back button to its source or topic and no other way on.
- **Content.** `lessons.json` `checkpoints` is `{version: 1, items}`, emitted by the seed from each unit file's `checkpoint` export with its `lesson`. An item has the unit drill fields of its `format` (`choice`, `blank`, `order`, `star`, `match`, `truefalse` or `cloze`; no sorting) plus the recipe: `target` (`{word}` from the lesson's words table or `{point}` of the lesson) or, for matching and word-bank dialogues, `targets` one per pair or gap; `frame`, `example` and `slot` (the point it recomposes, the example it starts from and the taught word now in the slot, required for a point target); `venue` and `taught` (keys of `VENUES`, different); `uses` (other lessons it needs); `words` (every vocabulary ID shown); `names`; optional `instruction`. IDs start with the lesson ID and never end in `-<number>`. `checkpointProblems` (run by `tools/validate.mjs`) checks targets, frames, slots, venues, the taught pool, unit-line copies, answer keys and coverage ([content-standard.md](content-standard.md#checkpoint-recipes)).
- **Selection.** A run asks `max(9, new lessons)` recent items (`RECENT_MIN`), at least one per newly finished lesson, then `ceil(recent ÷ 3)` items (`EARLIER_SHARE`) from lessons earlier checkpoints covered; the first checkpoint asks no earlier items. Recent lessons are finished lessons placed up to the boundary that no finished checkpoint covered, so a lesson finished ahead of the path waits for its own block's checkpoint. Earlier items round-robin across earlier blocks, preferring items no finished checkpoint asked and patterns not yet in the run. No target appears twice in a run, and an item is eligible only once its lesson and every lesson it `uses` are finished. Everything is seeded (`selectItems`): the same seed, boundary and finished lessons give the same items in the same answer order; no clock or object order affects it. A shortfall is reported (`short`), never filled by repeating an item or borrowing later material.
- **State.** `state.checkpoints` is `{version: 1, seed, runs: {[n]: run}}`. `seed` is drawn once per learner. A run is `{n, after, attempt, seed, revision, started, lessons, earlier, items, answers, opened, done}`: the boundary and its lesson, the attempt number, the run's seed, a hash of its items as shipped, when it started, the new and earlier lessons it covers, the item IDs in answer order, first responses per counted key (`{v, ok, at, s?}`: the value, whether it was right, when, and `s: 1` when the item's lesson had been reopened during the run), when a covered lesson was reopened, and when it finished. A run is frozen at its first launch: reloads and new completions never change its items, and a finished checkpoint never reopens.
- **Answers.** `answerCheckpoint` keeps the first response per key; a repeated tap, a reload or an answer to a finished or stale run changes nothing. During the run the page shows only the questions: no words table, examples, pattern notes or answer feedback, and each answer reads *Answer saved*. Opening a lesson the run covers while it is unfinished is recorded in `opened`; later answers to its items count as supported, never as retrieval.
- **Results.** `finishCheckpoint` records `done` once, when every key is answered, and returns the misses. `runOutcome` gives one row per sampled target: *Retrieved* (right from memory), *Answered after reopening the lesson*, or *Not yet retrieved*. Points a run did not ask are not judged. Each row names the words-table row or point in its lesson that teaches the target, as text with no link into the lesson; a **Pattern note** button opens its dialog in place. Explanations and answers for every question show only now. Library → Progress & backup lists every checkpoint finished or under way (six to a page), so a finished one's results stay reachable.
- **Gap routing.** In the same save as `done`, `missRoutes` and `routeMisses` send each missed target into the existing scheduling. A point whose catalog pattern another started lesson drills becomes a pattern miss with source `checkpoint`, which queues its re-test a day later from the first miss ([Grammar patterns](#grammar-patterns)). Every other miss lapses its lesson's review through `scheduleReview` (back in a day, unless it is already relearning) and adds the unit items that teach the target to `review-focus` in `state.answers[lessonId]`, which that lesson's next review shows first. Ledger entries use venue `checkpoint` and the id `checkpoint:<n>-<attempt>:<lesson>`. Right answers clear nothing, no completion changes and unrelated records stay as they were; later scheduled reviews supply the recovery evidence.
- **Restore.** `readCheckpointState` rejects, in a backup, another version, a malformed seed, an unknown boundary or anchor, unknown lessons (a lesson that became a practice, r05, s03 or d01–d04, is accepted as history in a run's `lessons`, `earlier` and `opened`), extra fields, a finish before the start, a finished run with an unanswered key and an answer whose stored result is not the grade of its stored value; local state drops what it cannot use. An unfinished run whose items changed with a content update is kept and reads as stale: the page says its questions changed and the learner starts again as a new attempt, so its questions never change silently.

  Reordering a checkpoint's existing block is compatible when the former boundary is still in that block and the run's recorded new/earlier lessons cover every lesson in the block. Only `after` changes to the current boundary; the frozen items, answers and completion remain. This preserves checkpoint 1 from the earlier i01-before-r01 order when restoring local saves or backups. Missing coverage or an anchor in a different block is still rejected.

  Lessons inserted before a boundary (2026-10-23, card 0039, owner decision 0043) shift its block. A run is still compatible when its former boundary is on the path and the placed lessons it recorded as new or earlier are exactly the first 5*n* of the path, ending on that former boundary: every other lesson among them was inserted since. As for a reorder, only `after` changes, and the run keeps its items, answers and completion, finished or not. The inserted lessons were never asked, so they are recent lessons of the next unfinished checkpoint, which opens once they are finished. A started run whose block gained one resumes after it is finished. This keeps checkpoints 2–5 finished on a save from the path before h01 and bb01 (old boundaries r04, r07, r08, pl01); h01 and bb01 are asked in checkpoint 6. A run missing one of its lessons, or ending on a lesson it did not cover, is still rejected (`tests/checkpoints.test.mjs`).

## Learning loop

This section is the schema for [learning-loop.md](learning-loop.md): how lessons, practices and books name their prerequisites, how stones follow their lessons, what is stored, and what the validator checks. It was built in phases (1, 2a, 2b, 3 and 4, on 22 and 23 October 2026), and each part records what was built when. [What the loop replaced](#what-the-loop-replaces) lists what it superseded.

The Learn portion is already implemented by `learn.mjs` above. Practices as unit-shaped data, `loop.mjs` reading them, the Practice page and runs, and state version 3 were built in 2a (2026-10-22). ### Items and IDs

The loop has three kinds of item: lessons, practices and books. A book is a passage (`lessons.json` `passages`) or an unseen text (`lessons.json` `transfer`).

- **IDs are unique across all three kinds.** Lessons and books keep their IDs, which are letters followed by digits: `f01`, `r03`, `pl01`, `p01`, `ps01`, `t01`. A practice ID is a lowercase slug of letters, digits and hyphens with **at least one hyphen**, for example `restaurant-review`. That rule means a practice ID can never collide with a lesson ID, a book ID, or the old answer keys `core` and `plus1`.
- **Practice IDs:**
  - general: the first two, `restaurant-review` (*Restaurant review: a full visit*) and `everyday-dishes` (*Everyday dishes*), and since Phase 4 one for every other lesson, from `counter-drinks` (f01) to `late-and-changes` (pl04); `lessons.json` `practices` and the validator's loop map list them all;
  - optional, converted from lessons in 2b: `without-onions` (once r05), `sizes-colors` (once s03), `dating-interests` (d01), `dating-ask` (d02), `dating-confirm` (d03) and `dating-feelings` (d04);
- **Optional items.** A practice or book is general unless it carries `optional: true`. Optional content is for niche, less general situations ([learning-loop.md](learning-loop.md#optional-content)). There are no optional lessons: every placed lesson is core.

### References

A prerequisite is a reference string. These are the only forms:

| Reference | Met when | Started when |
|---|---|---|
| `lesson:<id>` | the lesson is in `completed` | `lessonStarted` is true, or the lesson is in `completed` |
| `lesson:<id>/<part>` | every counted item of that part is solved (`right` or `fixed` in `state.answers[<id>]`, returned items included) | its lesson is started |
| `practice:<id>` | `state.loop.practices[<id>].done` is set | the practice has any result in `state.answers[<id>]`, or is done |
| `book:<id>` | `state.loop.books[<id>].done` is set | for a passage, a recall attempt or draft exists; for an unseen text, a `state.transfer` entry exists |

A part is one teaching part of a lesson's unit. Parts follow the `sections` order:

- `words`: the practice sets before the first point;
- the ID of a `point` section (`r03-p1`): that point, plus the practice sets up to the next point, the dialogue or the まとめ;
- `conversation`: the `dialogue` section, plus the practice sets up to the まとめ;
- `matome`: the `final` practice set.

A part with no counted items cannot be named. With the first Point now immediately after Words, current lessons have an empty words assessment part; their following checks belong to the first Point. Use a nonempty Point reference or the whole lesson. No shipped companion requires an empty part. A reference never names a checkpoint, delayed recall, stone, score or time.

### Where prerequisites are authored

- **Lessons.** *(Built in 2b: [Learn path](#learn-path).)*
  - `curriculum.json` `path` is version 2: `{version: 2, core, requires, checkpoints?}`.
  - `core` orders every placed lesson; optional `checkpoints` names cumulative boundary lesson IDs.
  - `requires` keeps one entry per placed lesson, holding bare lesson IDs only. A lesson never requires a part, a practice or a book.
  - `electives` is removed, and every lesson in `requires` is in `core`. There are no optional lessons.
- **Stones** need no new field. Every stone is core, and its state follows its lessons. A node whose content is all optional, such as Dating and relationships since d01–d04 are practices, has no playable lesson and stays hidden.
- **Practices.** There is one file per practice, `tools/practices/<id>.mjs`, which exports `practice`. A practice is unit-shaped (see [Units](#units)) with these fields:

  ```js
  {id, title, home, requires, revealed?, optional?, kind?, focus, words, newWords?, dialogue?, sections}
  ```

  - **`sections`** may use every unit section kind (`point`, `dialogue`, `practice`, and a `final` まとめ), since a practice can teach wording changes, forms and grammar differences.
  - **`optional: true`** marks an optional practice ([Items and IDs](#items-and-ids)).
  - **`kind`** (default `'unit'`) says how the practice is shown. `'unit'` is the unit-shaped set described here. Another kind would have no authored `words` or `sections` and a view of its own; none is built (Kanji cards, the one kind built in kanji Phase 4, became its own page on 2026-10-23: [Kanji cards](#kanji-cards)). The app does not list a practice of another kind, and its `#practice/<id>` lands on Practice. The seed passes `kind` through only when it is not `'unit'`, `checkUnit` runs only on unit practices, and `tools/loop-check.mjs` leaves another kind's `words` and `sections` alone.
  - **`words`** is its Words table: the words it introduces, in bulk. The 6–12 limit on a lesson's Words table does not apply.
  - **`newWords`** adds new vocabulary records, as a unit file's `newWords` does: the seed merges them into the vocabulary after the units' (an existing ID is accepted only with an identical record).
  - **`dialogue`** holds the lines its `dialogue` section shows, as `{speaker, ja, en}`. The seed ships them with reading support, as a lesson's, and their word references (`refs` in `tools/seed-lessons.mjs`, under the practice ID) as the practice's `occurrences`. A word tapped there records `practice:<id>` as its source.
  - **Seed:** `tools/seed-lessons.mjs` gathers every file into `lessons.json` `practices: {version: 1, items}`, and sets `minutes` from the counted items with `minutesFor`.
  - **Checks:** `checkUnit(unit, {practice: true})` runs on every unit practice. It skips the rules that are for lessons only: the lesson ceiling of 50 counted items, the 6–12 Words-table target, and the lesson skeleton's warnings (2–3 points, the drills after each point, a まとめ). A practice needs at least one counted item instead. Neither lessons nor practices require worked examples. Every other unit rule applies, and a practice may still have points and a まとめ of its own, as the six converted from lessons do. The Phase 4 content plan sets 20–30 counted items for a general practice ([content-plan-phase4.md](content-plan-phase4.md#1-practice-size-decided)); the 28 general sets have 25–30 and the six optional ones 49–50. A practice in [parts](#practice-parts) is larger: the course's part practices run 3–5 parts, 25–45 words and 45–65 counted items, while a flat practice keeps the 20–30 guidance. The validator enforces no size range.
- **Passages.** Each passage record in `tools/seed-lessons.mjs` gains `home`, `requires`, `introduces` and, for an optional book, `optional: true`. `introduces` lists the vocabulary IDs the book itself teaches, each with a glossary pair. `node` stays as the topic tag.
- **Unseen texts.** Each text in `tools/transfer-passages.mjs` gains `home`, `requires`, `introduces` and, if optional, `optional: true`. `sources` stays as the language contract `transferProblems` checks. It no longer decides when a text opens, and `requires` must cover every source lesson and passage within its chain.
- **`home`.** On every practice and book, `home` is one lesson ID. That lesson must be in the item's chain, which is the item's `requires` and, recursively, theirs.
- **`revealed: 'start'`** (optional, practices only) shows an item as revealed before any of its prerequisites is started. Only the first koi, `restaurant-review`, has it (owner, 2026-10-22). The seed rejects any other value.

#### Practice parts

A practice may be written in **parts**, like a lesson: each part introduces its own words and their usage, so one practice ends with a much larger vocabulary (owner, 2026-10-29; [learning-loop.md](learning-loop.md#what-each-kind-is-for)). A practice without `parts` works exactly as before.

**Authoring.** The file has `parts` in place of its own `words` and `sections`:

```js
export const practice={
 id:'countries-and-jobs', title:'Countries, people and jobs', home:'l01p1', requires:['lesson:l01p1'], optional?:true,
 focus:'One or two sentences for the whole practice.', newWords:[...],
 parts:[
  {id:'countries', title:'Countries and languages', focus:'One or two sentences for this part.', words:['roshia',{id:'x',frame:'…'}], sections:[ /* points and practice sets */ ]},
  {id:'jobs', title:'Jobs and workplaces', focus:'…', words:[…], sections:[…]},
 ]
};
```

**Rules.** The seed throws `tools/practices/<file>: …` on a file that breaks one, and the validator fails on a shipped item that does.
- With `parts` the file has no `words` and no `sections` (both forms present is an error), no `dialogue`, and is a unit practice (no other `kind`).
- 2 to 8 parts. A part is `{id, title, focus, words, sections}` and nothing else. `id` matches `^[a-z0-9]+(?:-[a-z0-9]+)*$` and is unique within the practice; `title` and `focus` are non-empty text. The part's Focus follows the unit Focus rules: reading markup is checked, and over two sentences or 45 words is a warning.
- `words` is a list in the unit `words` shape (IDs or `{id, frame?, recognize?}`), possibly empty. A vocabulary ID appears at most once across all parts: list each word in the part that first uses it. The seed's `unknown vocabulary` check covers every part's words.
- `sections` is a non-empty list of unit sections, `point` and `practice` only (no `dialogue` section), each with an `id`, unique across the practice. Each part has at least one counted item (`unitKeys`). A `final: true` set may appear only in the last part, and checkUnit still requires it to be the last practice set.

**Seed.** Both seeds (`tools/seed-lessons.mjs` and the course's hand-kept copy of the block, `flatParts`) join the parts' words and sections in part order into the flat `words` and `sections`, so every existing reader (checkUnit, `pages.mjs`, loop-check's teaching coverage, the app) sees a flat unit. `minutes` is `minutesFor` of the whole practice's count.

**Shipped shape.** `{id, title, home, requires, revealed?, optional?, focus, words, sections, minutes, parts?}`, with `parts: [{id, title, focus, words: [the part's vocabulary IDs, in order], sections: [the part's section IDs, in order]}]`. The parts' lists partition the flat lists in order.

**Helpers** (`dist/loop.mjs`, pure):
- **`practiceParts(practice)`** → `[{id, title, focus, words, sections, from, to, keys}]`, resolved against the flat lists: `words` are the flat list's entries (strings or `{id, frame?, recognize?}`), `sections` the section objects, `from`–`to` the part's section range in the flat list (`to` excluded), `keys` its counted keys as `unitKeys` gives them (the item ID, or `<id>.<n>` per match pair, sort card and cloze gap). `[]` for a practice without parts. It never throws: a part naming a missing section or word gets what resolves.
- **`partProblems(practice)`** → readable problem strings, `[]` when fine or without parts: the rules above, plus the parts' section IDs joined in order are exactly the flat sections' IDs, and their word IDs exactly the flat words'. `tools/validate.mjs` runs it on every unit practice, with the part Focus checks; checkUnit still runs on the flat unit.
- **Checker.** `node tools/check-practice.mjs <file>` checks one practice file without the whole build: the basic shape, the flat item derived from `parts` as the seeds' `flatParts` does (re-implemented there) with `partProblems`, its words against the profile's last-built `lessons.json` plus its `newWords` (a record that already exists is a problem when it differs, a warning when identical), checkUnit's practice rules, item IDs once across parts, words a part lists but no item uses (a warning), and lines that repeat a `kanjiChecks` sentence. It exits 1 on any problem. A file outside a profile (a scratch copy) takes `--profile personal|course`. The build stays the final gate (teaching coverage, shuffles, grading, loop rules).

**The app** (*built 2026-10-29*: `practiceRun`, `partList`, `partRun` and `partUnit` in `app.js`, with `partStates`, `currentPart` and `partRoute` in `loop.mjs`; tests in `tests/practice-parts.test.mjs` and `tests/practice-parts.browser.test.mjs`, the latter against the course build):
- `#practice/<id>` on a practice with parts opens its **part list**: the title, the PRACTICE eyebrow, its home topic and length, the Focus, then one row per part in the topic page's lesson-row style: the part's title and length, and done, a progress ring or an arrow; a part not open yet shows its title in the topic page's locked style (`lockedRow`), with no reason text (the topic page dropped "Opens after …" on 2026-10-29). Back from a part, its row takes focus.
- `#practice/<id>/<part>` runs one part with the unit machinery: an opening page with the part's title and Focus, the part's Words table, its sections, and a finish page.
- Parts open in order: part k opens when every counted key of part k−1 is solved, and a part with any result of its own stays open (so *again* on an earlier part never locks a part under way); a finished practice has every part open. A route to a part that is not open, to an unknown part, or to a part of a practice without parts replaces itself with `#practice/<id>`, leaving no history entry; a part of a practice that is not open goes to Practice, as the practice's own route does.
- A finish on a part other than the last (**Finish part**, once every key of the part is solved) says *Part finished.* and offers *Next: <next part> →*; it writes no loop state. The last part's **Finish practice** is enabled once every counted key of the whole practice is solved, and is the practice's finish: it writes `state.loop.practices[<id>] = {done}` once and names what it opened, as a flat practice's does.
- Results stay in `state.answers[<id>]` under unit keys (item IDs are unique across the practice). The saved page key is `unit-page:<part>` per part (`unitPager` derives it from the route's third segment; lessons and flat practices keep `unit-page`), and a backup keeps it: practice records are read as plain values. "Do the exercises again" on a part resets that part's items only (`resetItems`), keeps the finish and reopens the part's first item page.
- The koi's status pill reads "Part k of n" while the practice is started: k is the first part not done, or n when every part is done but the practice is not finished.
- **Helpers** (`loop.mjs`): `partStates(practice, record, finished)` → per part `{id, title, solved, total, open, done}`; `currentPart(states)` → the 1-based part the learner is on (0 without parts); `partRoute(route, content, progress)`, the route guard's step after `loopRoute`.
- A practice without parts behaves exactly as today.

### Runtime rules

A dependency-free module, `dist/loop.mjs`, holds the runtime rules. It reads the content and a progress snapshot (`completed`, `answers`, `loop`, `recall`, `transfer`). It has no DOM, no clock and no imports, and every function returns the same answer for the same inputs. **Built (2a and 2b, 2026-10-22; Phase 3, 2026-10-23):**
- `loop.mjs` has `practicesOf`, `booksOf` (since Phase 3), `itemState`, `homePlace`, `loopRoute`, `refMet`, `refStarted`, `opened`, `partsOpened` and `lessonParts`, and since 2026-10-29 `practiceParts`, `partProblems`, `partStates`, `currentPart` and `partRoute` ([Practice parts](#practice-parts)).
- Since 2a it reads the practice sets from `content.practices.items` (`lessons.json` `practices`; `restaurant-review` requires r01–r04, `everyday-dishes` requires `restaurant-review`). The progress snapshot is saved state version 3 as it is: results under each item's ID in `answers`, finishes in `loop`.
- *Built in Phase 3 (2026-10-23):* books open on their own `requires`. `booksOf(content)` reads them from `content.passages` and `content.transfer.texts` (`lessons.json`), each with its route (`passage` or `unseen`), and `itemState` judges a book exactly like a practice. The `gate` flag `app.js` used to compute from the topic shelf and the unseen-text gate is gone, with `availableReadings` and `transferAccess`. `content.items` still takes other items, for tests.
- Since 2b `parseRef` reads a part reference as `{kind: 'lesson', id, part}`, and `refMet(ref, progress, content)` and `refStarted(ref, progress, content)` read it from the lesson's unit in `content.lessons`: met once every counted key of the part is `right` or `fixed` in `answers[<lesson>]` (for the まとめ, also each `return:<item>` that a wrong reason check sent there), started once its lesson is. Without `content` a part is never met. `stoneState` stays in `learn.mjs`.
- The rules below are the specification, each noting where it was built. Stones and lessons are not in `loop.mjs`: stone states are `stoneState(node, path, lessons, opts)` in `learn.mjs`, built with the garden on 2026-10-22, and lesson access is `lessonLock` in `progression.mjs`.

- **`lessonParts(unit)`** returns the parts above, each with its section range and counted keys. *Built:* a Map from part name to `{from, to, keys}`, where `from`–`to` is the part's section range (`to` excluded) and `keys` its counted keys as `unitKeys` gives them. `words` is always listed, even when empty. `refMet(ref, progress)` and `refStarted(ref, progress)` apply the reference table.
- **`itemState(id, content, progress)`** gives the state of a practice or book:
  - `finished` once done;
  - `started` once started;
  - `open` once every reference in `requires` is met;
  - `revealed` once every reference has been started;
  - `hidden` otherwise.

  A started or finished item is never lower than `open`, whatever later happens to its prerequisites. An item with `revealed: 'start'` is `revealed` instead of `hidden` from the start. The flag only lifts hidden to revealed: it never opens anything. An item without a `requires` list (malformed content) stays hidden until it is started. *Built for practices (2a) and books (Phase 3).*
- **`stoneState(nodeId, content, progress)`** gives the state of a stone:
  - `open` when any of its lessons is open under `lessonLock`;
  - `outline` when none is open but some lesson has every `requires` entry started;
  - `hidden` otherwise, which includes every node without a playable lesson.
- **Lessons** keep `lessonLock` in `progression.mjs`: `requires` plus a due checkpoint. Checkpoint boundaries and `requiredLessons` count `core` only.
- **`opened(before, after)`** lists the lessons, practices, books and stones that went from locked or hidden to open between two snapshots. The finish screen of a lesson, practice or book names them as text, and so does the note at the end of a lesson part, which compares snapshots taken before and after the part's last item.
  - *Built (2a):* `opened(before, after, content)` returns the practices and books, as `[{id, kind}]` with `kind` `practice` or `book`, practices first, each in content order.
  - `loop.mjs` cannot import the Learn path, so lessons, stones and the due checkpoint are still compared by the app (`openNow` and `unlockedSince` in `app.js`, through `progression.mjs` and `learn.mjs`).
  - A lesson's and a practice's finish screens name the practices it opened as *In Practice: <title>*, and since Phase 3 the books as *In Library → Reading: <title>* (`unlockItems` in `app.js`). A book's first completion names the same under its score ([Completion](#completion)).
  - *Built (2b):* `partsOpened(before, after, content, lessonId)` returns, for each part of the lesson that went from not met to met, the practices and books that opened and name that part in their `requires`: `[{part, items: [{id, kind}]}]`. On the lesson page, after each answer (`learnCtx` wrapped in `lessonView`), the app compares the loop snapshot from before it and, when a part opened something, adds one line under the item just answered: *Unlocked · In Practice: <title>* (or *In Library → Reading*), as text with no links.
- **`homePlace(id)`** gives the place an item lives:
  - a lesson's topic page, `#node/<node>`, or `#field` while that stone is not open;
  - `#practice` for a practice;
  - `#library/reading` for a book.

  A route to an item that is not open (`#lesson/<id>`, `#practice/<id>`, `#passage/<id>` or `#unseen/<id>`) renders nothing. It calls `location.replace` with `homePlace(id)`, so it leaves no history entry. No locked page exists. Practice sets get the route `#practice/<id>`. The old `#core-check` and `#plus` replace themselves with `#practice/restaurant-review` and `#practice/everyday-dishes`, or with `#practice` while those are locked. *Built in Phase 3:* the old address of a lesson that became a practice, `#lesson/r05` (`s03`, `d01`–`d04`), first becomes its practice's route through `CONVERTED` in `recall.mjs` (`convertedRoute` in `app.js`'s `routeGuard`), so it lands on `#practice/<id>` while that set is open, started or finished, else `#practice`.
- **Display.**
  - A revealed practice is a koi silhouette, and a revealed book is a blank cover. Neither has a name, a link, a button or requirements text.
  - A brand-new learner therefore sees one koi silhouette in Practice, the restaurant review (`revealed: 'start'`), and nothing else there.
  - Practice and Library → Reading group items under their home lesson. The groups follow the stone order of the garden, then the `path` order of their home lessons.
  - Within a group, general items come first, then optional ones, each optional item with a small *Optional* label. *(2b)* Within each of those, a set that builds on another set comes after it (its chain depth), else content order: the Dating sets show in their chain order.
  - Hidden items are not in the page at all.
  - *Built for Practice (2a):*
    - The koi are grouped by home lesson. Each group has a small header, the home lesson's title in the style of the koi labels.
    - A group shows its header only once one of its sets is open, so a silhouette never names the lesson it waits on. A new learner's pond is drawn as before.
    - Each koi, open or silhouette, is placed by its index across the whole pond, not within its group (`river.mjs` `pondPlaces`, 2026-10-28): lanes alternate left and right, and its drop (lift) and angle (turn) cycle through short lists, so a lone koi in a group can sit on the right and the pond does not hug one side on a phone.
    - Opened from anywhere but a set or a review (the sidebar, a link, a direct `#practice`), Practice starts at the top, ignoring its saved scroll position, and the first open koi not done takes focus, scrolled into view only when it is not already on screen. With every open koi done, or none open, it stays at the top. Back from a set, that set's koi takes focus as before.

The four Dating sets' category is *Dating and relationships*, though their home lesson is in Making plans.

### Completion

- **A practice is done** when every counted item is solved, as `lessonComplete` works for a lesson. The first time, `state.loop.practices[<id>] = {done: <time>}` is written. **Do the exercises again** clears the practice's results in `state.answers[<id>]` but never clears `done`.
  - *Built (2a):* one view, `practiceRun` in `app.js`, plays every unit practice at `#practice/<id>` with the unit machinery ([Paged lessons and runs](#paged-lessons-and-runs)).
  - Its results are unit keys in `state.answers[<id>]`, and a wrong answer is tried again until it is right, as in a lesson. A practice asks no reason checks.
  - **Finish practice** is enabled once every counted item is solved. The first finish names what it opened (`opened`), as text with one way back to Practice: no links into other items and no Next or Go-to button. Since 2026-10-29 a finished practice never shows the button again: its finish page has the progress line, the status (*Practice finished. Every item is correct.*, or *… · n to go.*) and, while every item is solved, **Do the exercises again**.
  - **Do the exercises again** removes the practice's record from `answers`, except its `seen:<wordId>` keys (2026-10-29), and opens its first item page.
- **A passage is completed** by an immediate-set attempt that is from memory (`closed`, with no look back) and has every first response correct.
  - A learner can start a new immediate attempt at any time, with no limit and no wait. It begins with a fresh supported read, and its choices come in a new seeded order.
  - Attempts are kept as they are now: at most 40, oldest dropped. Completion is stored in `state.loop.books[<id>]`, so it survives that trimming.
  - Delayed-set attempts never complete a book.
  - *Built in Phase 3 (2026-10-23):* `completesPassage(attempt)` in `recall.mjs` is the rule (an immediate attempt, `fromMemory`, every first response correct; the migration's history rule uses it too), and `completeBook(loop, id, now)` writes `{done: now}` once and returns `first`. The passage page applies them when an attempt is finished ([Passage recall](#passage-recall)).
- **An unseen text is completed** by a closed attempt whose recall items and evidence-line picks are all right.
  - Only the `initial` attempt counts as transfer evidence. Further attempts are recorded with phase `retry`, which `transferEvidence` ignores. A retry starts from a fresh read.
  - The one delayed check stays as it is and never completes the text.
  - *Built in Phase 3:* `completesText(attempt)` in `transfer.mjs` (an `initial` or `retry` attempt, `closed`, every recall item and evidence line right), written through `completeBook` ([Unseen texts](#unseen-texts)).
- **Finishing.** When a book is first completed, its result says *Book completed* (*Text completed* for an unseen text) under the score and lists what that completion opened (`opened`, through `unlockedSince` in `app.js`), as text with no links (rule 10). A book not completed yet says instead what completes it and offers **Try again**. *Built in Phase 3.*
- **Replays.** Finishing an item again changes nothing in `state.loop`, and nothing a replay does relocks or reopens anything. Once a book is completed, its result offers **Read again** in place of **Try again**, and adds nothing under the score.

### Validator

`loopProblems(content)`, run by `tools/validate.mjs`, fails on:

- a reference in a form other than those above, or naming an unknown item or part, or a part with no counted items;
- IDs repeated across kinds, or a practice ID without a hyphen;
- a `home` missing, unknown, not a lesson, or outside the item's chain;
- a cycle, or a practice or book whose chain holds no lesson;
- a lesson whose `requires` names anything but lessons;
- a placed lesson missing from `core`;
- a general practice or book whose chain holds an optional practice or book (optional content only leads to optional content);
- an unseen text whose `requires` chain misses a source lesson or source passage;
- **coverage**:
  - a practice or book showing a word that its chain does not teach (lesson Words tables, practice `words`, earlier books' `introduces`) and that its own `words` or `introduces` does not list;
  - a book whose `introduces` makes up more than 5% of its word occurrences;
  - a lesson whose drill uses a word that a practice's `words` or a book's `introduces` lists and that the lesson's chain (its own Words table and those of its `path.requires` ancestors) does not teach (learning-loop.md rule 6). Since 29 October 2026 this fails only when a practice or book that teaches the word is homed at the drilling lesson itself, at a later lesson in the path's teaching order, or at a lesson off the path; when every teacher is homed at an earlier lesson it is a warning (`Loop: lesson …: its drills use … taught earlier by practice … but not by the lesson's chain: …`). Drills are the items the learner answers, in every set including the まとめ, and the lesson's transfer question in `checks.mjs`; returned items are those items again. The conversation, points, Focus, each set's solved example (例), kanji intro lines and feedback are display, where such a word may stay as a glossed context word. A Kanji cards practice teaches no words here either;
- **reachability**: starting from empty progress, a simulated learner finishes every open item, repeatedly, until nothing new opens. Any item still not finished fails, named with what it waits on.

It also fails on any lesson placed on the path that is not the `home` of at least one general practice and at least one general book (a passage or an unseen text). Optional items do not count toward that minimum. Grammar patterns in books are not checked mechanically; whether a book introduces a new pattern needs educator review.

*As built (Phase 4, 2026-10-23):* book coverage, the 5% share and the per-lesson minimum are enforced as problems. Coverage is checked for books (passages and unseen texts); a practice of another kind than `unit` teaches no words to a book's chain. The practice coverage rule in the list above is not yet checked mechanically.

*Lesson drills (2026-10-23):* the lesson rule is enforced as a problem, one per lesson and word, naming the items, for example `lesson r06: its drills use soba (そば), taught by practice choosing-where-to-eat but not by the lesson's chain: r06-C2, r06-E1, r06-E2`. `lessonWordUses(content, curriculum)` in `tools/loop-check.mjs` lists every such use, in drills and in display, and finds words by surface: each word's written form and authored forms, a verb's stem before a conjugation ending (`{入|はい}って`), a kanji い-adjective's stem before く, か or け, and a kanji word's reading written in kana (ごはん, three kana or more), the longest surface first (`そば{屋|や}` is soba-shop, not soba). A gap is read filled with its right answer and tiles in their order; a kanji reading item's choices are readings, not words. A kana-only word of one or two characters that is not a noun (もう, いい, では, と) is not looked for, because its surface also turns up inside other words (いいえ, ではありません, みなと); such words were checked by hand when the rule was first applied.

The validator report prints the loop map from the data (`loop.map`): each lesson in teaching order with the practices and books whose home it is and their `requires`. Stone states are not in it. learning-loop.md keeps the stones table and summarises the map, so the map itself is never maintained by hand.

### Teaching coverage (authoring only)

This metadata is consumed by tools/validate.mjs only. It does not change lessons.json, curriculum.json, item IDs or saved learner state.

`teachingGaps(content, curriculum, {coverage, baseline, sources})` in `tools/loop-check.mjs` checks that every item a learner answers in a unit-shaped lesson or practice tests only what was taught before it ([content-standard.md](content-standard.md#teach-the-assessed-target-first)). Every format is covered, including match, sort and cloze, kanji items and the まとめ. A solved 例 is not assessed. A review or returned copy (`return:<id>`, with `source`) uses its original item's declaration (`declarationFor`). Passage-recall questions, generated checkpoints, transfer checks and kanji-card generators keep their own validators, and this check does not certify their teaching.

**Declarations** live in `tools/teaching-targets.mjs`, which exports `coverage = {version: 1, targets, assessments, exceptions}`. Owner keys are `lesson:<id>` or `practice:<id>`, and item keys are `<owner>/<itemId>` using existing IDs, never array indexes.

- `targets`: semantic target ID (a lowercase slug) → `{label, witnesses}`. A witness is `{owner, section, field, quote, supports}`. `section` is a Point of `owner`. `field` is `pattern`, `explain`, `note`, `examples/<n>/ja` or `examples/<n>/en`. A `pattern` citation also needs an `explain`, `note` or `examples/<n>/en` citation of the same Point. `quote` must appear in that field once ruby markup is reduced to its written form and spacing is collapsed. It anchors a reviewed reading; it does not prove the Point teaches the use. `supports` is `recognition` or `active`: active supports both modes, recognition only recognition. A target may have several witnesses. An empty list is valid, but an assessment of that target always fails.
- `assessments`: item key → `{targets: [{id, mode}], words: [{id, mode}], reason}`, where `mode` is `recognition` or `active`. At least one list is non-empty. `reason` states what determines the answer. A `words` entry recalls a dictionary headword with the meaning its Words row shows. It never covers an inflected form, a secondary sense, a particle combination or a contextual function. A match, sort or cloze item declares the union of its pairs, cards or gaps. Distractors and incidental context are not declared.
- `exceptions`: item key → `{kind: 'nonlanguage', reason, evidence}`, for a task that needs no Japanese distinction. `reason` and `evidence` are non-empty. There is no other kind. The retired pretest has none, so `diagnostic-pretest` is unknown. There are no exceptions for a whole unit. An item is declared or excepted, never both.

**What teaches.** Teaching is a Point field cited as a witness, or a rendered Words row. A row grants its headword and shown meaning, and recognition only when it is `recognize: true`. It grants nothing from its frame, the record's `forms`, senses or definition, or a conjugation. Pages are numbered as `unitPages(unit, {words: rows})` numbers them. A witness in the item's own unit counts only on an earlier page. The Words table comes before every item. Other items, solved examples, feedback, the conversation, Focus and later Points never teach. Across units, an item's prerequisites guarantee the following, read from `path.requires` and `requires` with a visited set:

- a whole lesson: its Points and Words rows, and its `path.requires` ancestors;
- a lesson part (`lesson:<id>/<part>`): the Points in that part's section range from `lessonParts`, and the lesson's ancestors, but no other part of that lesson. The Words rows belong to the `words` part, so `lesson:f01/words` grants no f01 Point and a Point part grants no Words row;
- a practice: its Points and Words rows, and its own requires. An optional practice counts only when the chain actually requires it; a shared `home` grants nothing;
- a book: only its own requires. A book's passage teaches nothing here;
- an unknown or malformed reference grants nothing.

**Baseline.** `tools/teaching-baseline.json` is the frozen legacy inventory: `{version: 1, sourceCommit, entries}`. `entries` maps an item key to `{kind, fingerprint, reason}`, plus `target` and `repairCard` when `kind` is `known-gap`. The other kind is `unreviewed`, whose reason is "Pre-existing assessment awaiting explicit target audit; not certified as covered." The baseline was generated once, by card 0047's Build, from `sourceCommit` b725c29 (read with `git show`). An entry was carried only where its fingerprint still matched the working tree. Nothing regenerates or refreshes it: no command writes it, and an entry is only ever removed. The fingerprint is SHA-256 of key-sorted JSON over:

- the item, its effective format and its set's display (`instruction`, `stem`, `example`, `intro`, `kanjiWords`, `final`);
- the unit's rendered Words rows, with each record's `word`, `reading`, `meaning` and `pos` and the row's frame and recognize flag;
- the unit's Points on earlier pages;
- the item's declaration or exception, with every cited witness Point and every declared word's record, wherever they are.

Other items, later Points and other units' teaching are left out, because declared items are checked against current prerequisites on every run.

**Results.** Each result is `{code, owner, item, page, target, mode, witness, reason, severity, source?}`, sorted by owner, page, target and code. `target` is null for a missing declaration, and is `word:<id>` for a declared word. `witness` is `<owner>/<section>`, `<owner>/words` or null. The codes are `missing-declaration`, `missing-teaching`, `late-teaching`, `recognition-only`, `invalid-metadata`, `stale-baseline` and `legacy-unreviewed`. Problems fail validation:

- an item that is not in the baseline and has no declaration or valid exception (new or changed content);
- a declared target or word without an eligible witness in the right mode;
- invalid metadata, which fails even on a baseline item;
- a stale entry: its fingerprint no longer matches, its item is gone, or its known gap is resolved.

An exact `known-gap` entry turns its target's failure into a warning. An exact `unreviewed` entry is counted, not certified. Plain validation prints each known gap and one summary line: `Teaching coverage: <covered> declared items covered; <excepted> reasoned exceptions; <legacy> unchanged legacy items unreviewed; <gaps> known legacy gaps; <problems> problems. Legacy warnings do not certify teaching coverage.` `node tools/validate.mjs --teaching-report` also prints every result, legacy items included, with the same exit status. There is no warn-only mode, and the check never reads Flow Note cards. `tests/teaching-coverage.test.mjs` keeps the original f01-B3, s05-B1 and i03-B2 failures as pinned fixtures.

### Engine

`dist/kanji-words.mjs` is pure: it touches neither the DOM nor `localStorage`. It imports only `RUBY_PATTERN` from `vocabulary.mjs`.

- **Kanji words.** A kanji word is a vocabulary entry whose `word` contains `{written|reading}` markup.
  - `kanjiWordIndex(vocabulary)` lists every kanji word with its match patterns: the markup of its `word` and of each `forms[].form`, longest first.
  - The index also maps each token (`水|みず`) to the words that contain it.
  - In today's content, 142 entries are kanji words, and 10 tokens belong to more than one word: `物|もの`, for example, is in drink, shopping and food.
- **Resolving text.** `resolveText(text, index)` splits a markup string into plain runs and tokens, and gives each token its word.
  - The longest pattern matching at that place wins, so `{食|た}べ{物|もの}` is food, not eat.
  - A token outside every match takes its only word. When several words share the token, it lists them as `candidates` instead.
  - A token that belongs to no word, such as a name, a place, or a time like `{六時|ろくじ}`, has none.
  - Results are cached per string.
- **State.** `state.kanjiWords` is `{version: 1, words: {[wordId]: {met, points, pointDay, known, missed}}}`. A word with no entry has not been met.
  - `met` is the local day (`YYYY-MM-DD`) the word was met. `known` is the local day it first reached `KNOWN_POINTS`, or `null`.
  - `points` is the word's passed Kanji card trials, a whole number from 0, and `pointDay` is the local day of the last pass, or `null`.
  - `missed` is true while a known word's reading is shown after a wrong answer.
- **Recording (since 2026-10-23).** `markMet(kw, id, now)`, `recordAnswer(kw, id, correct, now)` and `recordTrial(kw, id, passed, now)` return a new state and never change their input. Each also marks the word met.
  - `recordAnswer` is a lesson, practice or book drill answer, or a reading check answer. It changes no points: a right answer clears `missed`, and a wrong one sets `missed` on a known word.
  - `recordTrial` is a closed trial on the Kanji page. A pass adds a point, unless the word already passed one that local day; at `KNOWN_POINTS` it sets `known`, and it clears `missed`. A pass on a known word is a review: it records its day and point and never changes `known`. A failed trial takes nothing away and sets `missed` on a known word.
  - `known` is never cleared.
  - The numbers are exported constants: `KNOWN_POINTS` 3 and `POINTS_PER_DAY` 1. `MISS_COST` is gone.
  - Stored entries keep their shape and both readers are unchanged. Points earned before the trial (when a drill answer was a point and 10 made a word known) count as passes. An entry with points at or above `KNOWN_POINTS` and no `known` day stays as stored, and so stays met (`wordStage` reads `known`), until its next pass makes it known.
- **Stages and display.** `wordStage(kw, id)` is `unmet`, `met` or `known`. `stagedText(text, index, kw, {showAll})` returns the resolved runs, and gives each token a `display` (since 2026-10-29, [Stages on screen](#stages-on-screen)):
  - `kana`: *Show all kanji* is off (every token), or the word is not mastered.
  - `kanji`: *Show all kanji* is on and the word is mastered (`isMastered`); its reading is on tap.
  - `kanji-reading`: *Show all kanji* is on, the word is mastered and `missed` is set.
  - `kana-kanji` is no longer produced for text; it remains a fixed display.
  - `isMastered(kw, id, {written, known})` is the one definition of mastered, provisional until the Kanji section is revised: with no track (`known` null), the word's stage is `known`; with a track (`known` a Set), the word is met or known and every kanji of the token's written form is in `known`.
  - A shared token outside every match takes the most advanced display among its candidates.
  - A token of no word is a composite of its kanji ([Stages on screen](#stages-on-screen)). The index maps each kanji to the words with a token that contains it (`chars`). `compositeWords(written, index)` gives the words of each kanji in a written form, cached on the index, or `null` for a name or place in `PROPER_NOUNS`.
  - `tokenDisplay(kw, ids, {showAll, written, index})` gives one token's display from its word or candidates, as `stagedText` does. A token of no word needs its written form and the index, and is `kana` without them. The app uses it to restage rendered text in place.
  - A fixed display bypasses the stages ([Stages on screen](#stages-on-screen)). Kanji words items use it ([Kanji words drill](#kanji-words-drill-phase-3-unit-format)).
- **Reading saved state.** `readKanjiWords(value, {strict, wordIds})` reads `value.kanjiWords` and returns `{kanjiWords}`, in one of two modes:
  - Strict, for backup restore, rejects an unknown version, an unknown word ID or a malformed entry. It throws *This backup contains invalid kanji word records.*, or *…record for &lt;id&gt;.*
  - Lenient, for local load, drops bad entries and defaults to an empty state.
  - An entry has exactly the five keys and real calendar days. Points or a known day need a `pointDay`, and `missed` needs `known`. Day order is not checked, so a change of time zone cannot break a restore.
  - `wordIds` may be an array, a Set or `index.words`. Without it, any ID in slug form is accepted.

As built, Phase 1 also settles these details:

- `localDay(now)` is exported: the local `YYYY-MM-DD` of a Date or timestamp, or `null` for an invalid time.
- Recording returns the same store object when nothing changes, such as `markMet` on a word already met. The app saves only when `next !== kw`.
- Only `correct === true` (a trial's `passed === true`) counts as right. Points keep counting past `KNOWN_POINTS`, through review passes. A pass clears `missed` even on a day it adds no point, and a fail leaves `pointDay` as it was. `POINTS_PER_DAY` is fixed at 1 by the stored shape, which keeps only the day of the last point. (As first built, every right answer anywhere was a point, 10 made a word known, and a miss cost one; the trial replaced that on 2026-10-23.)
- Two matches of the same length that belong to different words leave a token ambiguous, with `candidates`. In today's content that happens only with identical patterns: `{出口|でぐち}` is both deguchi and exit, and `{焼|や}き{魚|ざかな}` is grilled and also a form of fish.
- Among candidates, display ranks kana, then kanji-reading, then kanji, so a mastered word with no miss outranks one with a miss.
- **Cost:** building the index takes about 1 ms. Resolving all 16,070 distinct content strings takes about 17 ms cold and about 2 ms from cache. The app builds it once per content load, and its cache has no limit and is never cleared. Since the composite rule, staging all 23,010 distinct strings of the lessons, practices and passages takes about 4 ms once they are resolved, composites included.
- **For Phase 3:**
  - About 160 occurrences are ambiguous, because a conjugation or a word without its prefix is not an authored form: `{飲|の}み{物|もの}` without お, `{会|あ}いましたか`, `{食|た}べ（　）`.
  - `{出口|でぐち}` (76 occurrences) is two vocabulary entries.
  - Content work adds the missing forms or merges the duplicates, and the validator reports what is left.

### Stages on screen

Built in Phase 2 (2026-10-23). Since Phase 3a, answers in a kanji words drill record met words (and points, until the Kanji card trial took them over on 2026-10-23).

**Revised 2026-10-29 (owner): Show all kanji shows mastered kanji only.** The stages (met, known) are still recorded and still drive the Kanji page, the dictionary's filters and word cards, but staged text no longer draws them. With *Show all kanji* off (the default), every staged token is kana: lesson text, points, examples, drills, options, tiles, dialogues, practices, books and the Kanji page's sentence question. With it on, a token shows in kanji only when its word is mastered (`kw-kanji`, reading on tap; `kw-kanji-reading` when the word has a miss), and every other token stays kana. Mastered is `isMastered` in `kanji-words.mjs`, one function so the definition can change in one place; for now it is provisional, to be revised with the Kanji section: a known word with no character track, and with a track a met or known word whose kanji are all known on it. Fixed displays (below) are unchanged. The rules further down that describe met tokens in text (`kw-kana-kanji`, *Met style*) are kept as history.

- **State.** `state.kanjiWords` (empty to start) and `state.showAllKanji` (`false`) sit beside `loop`, and are saved and exported with the rest. Local load reads `kanjiWords` leniently and restore strictly (`readKanjiWords`, with the index's word IDs).
- **Migration, with no version bump.** `showAllKanji` replaces `reading`. As with the other added fields, the state version stays 3: a save or backup without `showAllKanji` takes it from `reading` (`readShowAll` in `app.js`), and `reading` is then dropped.
  - `ruby` (Kanji + readings) and `full` (Kanji + all readings) showed every kanji, so they turn it on.
  - `kana`, `kana-kanji` or no `reading` leave it off.
  - Restore still accepts versions 1–3. It refuses a `showAllKanji` that is not `true` or `false`, and a `reading` that names no mode.
- **Tokens of no word (built 2026-10-23).** A token that belongs to no vocabulary word, such as 本屋, 公園 or 六時, is a composite: it takes its stage from the words its kanji belong to.
  - Each kanji takes the most advanced stage among the words with a token that contains it, so 本 counts as known when 本 (book) or 日本 is known.
  - The token shows the least advanced of its kanji. Since 2026-10-29, with *Show all kanji* on, it is kanji once every kanji is in a mastered word, and kana while any is in none or in no word; with the switch off it is kana. 六時 stays in kana even when 時間 is known, because no word holds 六.
  - A missed known word puts the reading back on the composites that hold its kanji, unless another known word holds that kanji too.
  - People's names and places stay in kana whatever is known: `PROPER_NOUNS` in `kanji-words.mjs` lists the 31 names and 15 places in the content. Since 2026-10-28 a profile's `lessons.json` `kanji.properNouns` adds its own, merged over it when the app and the validator build the index ([Kanji policy and course](#kanji-policy-and-course)). Without it, 青木 would turn to kanji once 青 (blue) and 木曜日 are known. Since 2026-10-29 *Show all kanji* leaves them in kana too.
  - In today's content, 30 of the 194 tokens of no word can reach kanji (本屋, 十時, 今日, 切手…); the others wait for words that hold their kanji.
  - **Report.** The validator prints `kanjiTokens`: the distinct tokens of no word in lessons, practices and passages, by kind. Today there are 194: 100 numbers (numerals with any counters, and bare counters such as `{時|じ}`), 31 names, 15 places and 48 others. Each other token warns once, so content work can make it a word (*no vocabulary word covers 公園 (38 occurrences)*). Names are found from speakers, chat labels (`{上野|うえの}：`), a token before さん, さま, くん, ちゃん or 先生, and romanised speakers (Aoki), and one missing from `PROPER_NOUNS` warns. Nothing fails.
- **Rendering.** `jp(s)` is still the one place markup becomes HTML. The index is built once after `lessons.json` loads. `jp` calls `stagedText` on the raw string and escapes each part's text, written form and reading. Each token becomes one element of class `kw` plus its display:
  - `kw-kana`: a `<span>` holding the reading as plain text, with no ruby.
  - `kw-kana-kanji`: ruby with the kana at text size and the kanji small above, the old *Kana + kanji above* look, now per token.
  - **Met style (2026-10-28; no longer affects text since 2026-10-29).** Running text no longer draws the met stage, so `metStyle` changes nothing on screen; the field is still read and validated, and `metDisplay` stays exported, unused by text display. As first built: the kanji policy's `metStyle` ([The policy](#the-policy)) sets how a met token shows. `kanji-above`, the default and the personal profile's, is `kw-kana-kanji`. `furigana`, the course's textbook form, shows a met token as `kw-kanji-reading` (the kanji with its reading small above), wherever text is staged: lesson text, drills, options, tiles, dialogues, practices, books and the Kanji page's sentence question. `stagedText` and `tokenDisplay` take `{metStyle}` (the app passes `kanjiPolicy.metStyle`), and `metDisplay(display, metStyle)` in `kanji-words.mjs` maps the met display. Not-met and known tokens, a missed word's `kanji-reading`, *Show all kanji* and every fixed display are unchanged, so reading targets drawn with `{display: 'kanji', reveal: false}` still hide their reading.
  - `kw-kanji`: a `<span>` holding the kanji and a visually hidden reading (`.kw-reading`), which screen readers read.
  - `kw-kanji-reading`: ordinary ruby.
  - Staged tokens carry `data-kw` (`written|reading`) and `data-kw-words` (the token's word, or its candidates), so they can be restaged in place.
  - Text split before `jp()` (a marked part, the two sides of a gap, a tile) resolves each piece on its own.
- **Reading on tap.** Outside controls, tapping a `kw-kanji` token shows its reading small above the kanji without moving the text, and tapping again hides it. Inside buttons, answer options, tiles, chips and gaps, and in tiles or answers shown as text, a tap keeps its own meaning (choosing, placing), so the reading stays hidden. A tap records nothing.
- **Word references** render their surface through the same `jp()`.
  - A reference whose surface has a `kanji` token shows the ring (`is-faded`). Its first selection reveals the reading and logs a `reveal` in `readingEvidence`, as before, and the next opens the word card.
  - Deck chooser labels follow the stage (`surfaceLabel`): kana for a kana or kana-kanji token, kanji otherwise.
- **Fixed display.** `jp(s, {display})` shows every token of `s` in one display (`kana`, `kana-kanji`, `kanji` or `kanji-reading`), whatever the stage or the switch, and is never restaged. `jp(s, {display, words})` fixes only the tokens of those words (their word, or one of their candidates); the rest stay in their stages.
  - `reveal: false` leaves a `kanji` token's reading out of the markup altogether (`kw-bare`), so no tap, switch or screen reader shows it.
  - Written forms use `{display: 'kanji-reading'}`: the word card headword, deck lists, dictionary and flashcard `written` fields, and the reading check's feedback.
  - A Words table shows its unit's own kanji words (`unitKanjiWords`) that way. Since Phase 3a its other words follow their stages, like the text.
  - The reading check marks its words with `{display: 'kanji', reveal: false}`.
  - Kanji words items use the same option ([Kanji words drill](#kanji-words-drill-phase-3-unit-format)).
- **The switch.** `#kanji-switch` in the top bar is a toggle button (`aria-pressed`) labelled *Show all kanji*, off by default. It saves `showAllKanji` and calls `refreshKanji()`, and nothing else is saved or re-rendered.
- **Live updates.** `refreshKanji()` restages every rendered `data-kw` token in place from `state.kanjiWords`, the track's known characters and `state.showAllKanji` (`tokenDisplay`), then re-renders word references. The page, answers, placed tiles and focus stay. The kanji words drill calls it after it records answers.
- **Removed.** The body classes `kana-first`, `kana-kanji`, `readings-adaptive` and `readings-all`, the four-mode cycle, and fading by reading evidence (`adaptive()`). The reading check, `readingEvidence` and the word card's reading status line stay; Phase 2 only rewords their lines that named the removed mode ([Reading evidence and furigana fading](#reading-evidence-and-furigana-fading)).
- **Tests.** `tests/reading-modes.browser.test.mjs` (5 tests) covers:
  - the four displays in lesson text, a question, an answer option and a tile;

The browser tests that compare text on screen with its written form (bridge, dating, patterns, transfer) seed `showAllKanji: true`.

### Kanji words drill (Phase 3 unit format)

Every lesson has a kanji words drill; f01 shipped it first (Phase 3a). Since 25 October the first Point follows Words and the kanji drill follows initial use of that Point. Practices retain their authored order:

```js
{ kind:'practice', id:'f01-KW', format:'choice', reasons:false, kanjiWords:['water','tea'],
  instruction:'Read each new kanji word, then choose each answer.',
  intro:[ {word:'water', ja:'お{水|みず}、どうぞ。', en:'Here’s some water.'},
          {word:'tea', ja:'はい、お{茶|ちゃ}です。どうぞ。', en:'Here’s your tea.'} ],
  sentences:[ {word:'water', ja:'はい、コーヒーです。＿もどうぞ。', answer:'お{水|みず}', en:'Here’s your coffee. Have some water, too.', slot:'drink'},
              {word:'tea', ja:'…＿…', answer:'お{茶|ちゃ}', en:'…', slot:'drink'} ],
  example:{ id:'f01-KW0', kanjiWord:'tea', ask:'meaning', prompt:'… What does the underlined word mean?',
    ja:'すみません、お{茶|ちゃ}はありません。', mark:'お{茶|ちゃ}', choices:['tea','water','coffee'], correct:0, feedback:[…] },
  items:[
    { id:'f01-KW1', kanjiWord:'water', ask:'meaning', … },
    { id:'f01-KW2', kanjiWord:'tea', ask:'meaning', … },
    { id:'f01-KW3', kanjiWord:'water', ask:'reading', prompt:'… How is the underlined word read?',
      ja:'はい、お{水|みず}です。どうぞ。', mark:'お{水|みず}', choices:['おみず','おちゃ','おすい'], correct:0, feedback:[…] },
    { id:'f01-KW4', kanjiWord:'tea', ask:'reading', … },
  ] }
// In the まとめ (final:true) set:
{ id:'f01-KWR', format:'choice', reasons:false, kanjiWord:'tea', ask:'find', prompt:'… Which one is tea?',
  card:['お{水|みず}','お{茶|ちゃ}','コーヒー'], choices:['お{水|みず}','お{茶|ちゃ}','コーヒー'], correct:1, feedback:[…] }
```

- **`kanjiWords`:** the 1–3 vocabulary IDs the unit introduces, all from its word table (up to `rules.kanjiWordsPerLesson.max`, at most 10, where a profile raises it: [Content rules](#content-rules)).
- **`intro`:** one line per word, in the same order: a sentence from the scene with an English line. The word shows in kanji with its reading. Other words show in the learner's stages. It is not counted.
- **`sentences` (trial sentences, 2026-10-23):** one `{word, ja, answer, en, slot}` per word, in the same order, for the word's Kanji card trial ([Kanji cards](#kanji-cards)). It is not shown in the lesson and not counted.
  - `ja` holds exactly one ＿ (U+FF3F), the blank. `answer` is the markup that fills it, the word as written in the vocabulary or one of its forms (`お{水|みず}`, `{食|た}べます`). `en` is the whole sentence in English.
  - `slot` is what the blank accepts: `drink`, `food`, `thing`, `place`, `time`, `number`, `person`, `language`, `activity`, `state` or `expression` (`TRIAL_SLOTS` in `exercises.mjs`). A trial never shows two sentences of one slot.
  - The blank demands the word's meaning: only this word fits, from words the unit and its prerequisites teach, in a natural register. It is a new sentence, not a line of the unit or a kanjiChecks sentence ([content-standard.md](content-standard.md#kanji-words-in-lessons)).
- **Items:** each word gets one `ask:'meaning'` item and one `ask:'reading'` item, so 2 counted items per word. The 例 is one more of either kind and is not counted.
  - `mark` is the word's markup exactly as it appears in `ja`.
  - Meaning choices are English glosses of words from this unit or its prerequisites.
  - Reading choices are hiragana readings of the whole marked part. The wrong ones are readings of words from this unit or its prerequisites, or a real misreading of the same kanji.
  - There are three choices, with one feedback line each.
- **Optional まとめ item:** `ask:'find'`, with a menu, sign or receipt `card`, and `choices` written in kanji word markup. For example: "On this drinks menu, which is tea?"
- **Display:**
  - A meaning item shows its target in kanji with its reading.
  - A reading item or a find item shows the target in kanji with the reading hidden until it has been answered. *Show all kanji* never reveals it.
  - Feedback lines in the drill, and on any item with a `kanjiWord`, show every token in kanji with its reading, whatever the learner's stage. Otherwise a line that explains a misreading ("だい is how 大 reads in 大学") would turn into kana and stop making sense.
- **Recording:** the first answer in the drill marks every word in `kanjiWords` as met. Each answered item with a `kanjiWord` records a right or wrong answer for that word, which adds no points ([Engine](#engine)).
- **Budget (2026-10-24):** keep each introduced kanji word’s distinct meaning and reading checks. Trim redundant point drills and duplicate finals to keep the whole lesson at most 50 counted items. Repeated retrieval belongs mainly in Practice; no point has a minimum drill quota.

#### As built (Phase 3a)

**Checks.** `checkUnit` runs `kanjiWordProblems(unit, {label, index})` on lessons and practices alike. `index` is the `kanjiWordIndex` of the vocabulary, passed as `checkUnit`'s `kanjiWords` option; `validate.mjs` passes it. Without it, `checkUnit` builds one from vocabulary records, and with bare IDs it skips the rules marked *index*. Each of these is a problem:

- The set: `kanjiWords` holds 1–3 different IDs (1 to `checkUnit`'s `kanjiWordsMax`, default 3, which the validator sets from the content rules), each in the unit's word table and (*index*) a kanji word. It is a `choice` set before the まとめ; it may follow a Point. A unit has at most one. `intro` belongs only on such a set.
- The intro: one `{word, ja, en}` per word, in the order of `kanjiWords`. Each line holds its word in kanji (*index*: a token resolves to it, or lists it among its candidates).
- The trial sentences (2026-10-23): one `{word, ja, answer, en, slot}` per word, in the order of `kanjiWords`. `ja` has exactly one ＿; `answer` is given; `en` is text; `slot` is one of `TRIAL_SLOTS`. *Index:* every token of `answer` resolves to the word (or lists it among its candidates), and so do the tokens at the blank once `ja` is filled with `answer`; `ja` does not hold the word outside the blank. The filled sentence is not a copy of a line of the unit (it equals or sits inside none of the unit's strings, compared bare, as `sourceLines` reads them), and it neither contains nor sits inside a kanjiChecks sentence (`familiar`, which `validate.mjs` passes). Readings on every kanji come from `markupProblems`, which walks the sections. `sentences` belongs only on a kanji words drill.
  - Warnings, not problems: another kanji word of the same drill in `ja` (f03 teaches 円 and 百, and 百's sentence おにぎりは＿{円|えん}ですね needs 円), and an unexpected field.
  - `checkUnit`'s `trialSentences` option is `'required'` by default and in the validator for every lesson and practice. `'optional'` makes a drill without them a warning, for drafts. Until the practice sentences landed (2026-10-23, the same day) the validator used it for the 28 general practices.
- The items: every item (and any retained legacy `example`) must name a `kanjiWord` and ask `meaning` or `reading`. Each word has at least one counted item of each.
- Any item with `kanjiWord` or `ask`: both are present, on a `choice` item. The word is one of the unit's `kanjiWords`. It sits in the drill or the まとめ.
- A meaning or reading item marks the word once in `ja`. Every token of the mark resolves to the item's word in the context of the whole line (*index*).
- A reading item: every choice is kana only, and the right one is the plain reading of the mark (`お{水|みず}` → おみず).
- A find item: only in the まとめ, with a `card`. Its right choice holds one of the word's patterns (its `word` or a form), and every token in it is the word's (*index*). It may run on in kana or katakana, as in r06's `{辛|から}いラーメン`.
- Choices and feedback line up, as for every choice item. `intro` is not counted, so the drill adds 2 items per word. Legacy `example` fields are neither shown nor counted.

The new fields raise no *unexpected field* warning. `unitKanjiWords(unit)`, `drillWords(unit, setId)` and `kanjiDrills(unit)` are exported for the app and tools. Since Phase 3c the validator requires a kanji words drill in every lesson.

**Seed.** Sections and items pass into `lessons.json` unchanged, the drill fields (`sentences` too) included, for units and practices; `tests/exercises.test.mjs` compares every one with its source file. Running the seed twice gives the same file.

**On screen.** The lesson view and the practice run share it (`practiceHTML`, `drillHTML`, `bindPick`):

- The intro sits under the drill's instruction in a compact box (`.kanji-intro`). Each line shows the drill's words with `kanji-reading` and the other words in their stages, then the English. It shows with the drill's first item only (`data-page-first`), so no later page shows a reading next to a reading question.
- A meaning item's mark shows `kanji-reading`, and the rest of its line follows the stages.
- A reading item's mark, and its word anywhere else in the line, show `kanji` with `reveal: false`.
- A find item's choices, and the tokens of their words and of the item's word on its card, show `kanji` with `reveal: false`.
- "Answered" means solved. After a wrong pick the target stays hidden while the learner tries again. Once the item is solved, by the learner or on reopening, its targets re-render with `kanji-reading` (`revealTargets`).
- Fixed tokens carry no `data-kw`, so neither *Show all kanji* nor `refreshKanji()` changes them.
- Feedback on any item with a `kanjiWord` shows every token with `kanji-reading`: the verdict, the answer line and any reason-check lines. Every item in a drill has one.

**Recording.** `kanjiWordsAnswer(unit, item, correct)` in `app.js` runs on every check in the lesson and practice contexts (`learnCtx` `unit` and `pend`, `practiceCtx` `unit`). An item in a kanji words drill marks all of the drill's words met (`markMet`, so only the first answer changes anything). An item with a `kanjiWord` records the answer (`recordAnswer`), a retry after a miss included. The new store is committed with the item's result, or on its own when the result does not change (a second miss). The app then calls `refreshKanji()`, so later text in the lesson restages at once. *Do the exercises again* clears item results only, so kanji progress stays. Since 2026-10-23 these answers add no points ([Engine](#engine)). The editor preview (`memoryCtx`) and closed checkpoints record nothing.

**Words table.** `wordsTableHTML` shows the unit's own `kanjiWords` with `kanji-reading` (class `written-form`). The other words follow the learner's stages and restage with the page. Word card and dictionary headwords stay `kanji-reading`.

**Tests.** `tests/exercises.test.mjs` has 5 tests: a valid fixture, one rejection per rule, one per trial sentence rule (with the sibling warning and the optional mode), the practice path, and f01 with seed passthrough (every lesson and practice drill ships its trial sentences). `tests/kanji-drill.browser.test.mjs` (2 tests) plays f01 as a new learner and a practice run made from f01's drill (the editor preview's step went with the editor).

Its look is the paper theme ([Kanji paper](#kanji-paper)); the owner's background image goes in `--kanji-art` on `.kanji-board`. Nothing on the page links elsewhere. An old `#practice/kanji-cards`, `#studio` or `#editor` replaces itself with `#kanji`, leaving no history entry.
- **Module.** `kanji-cards.mjs` holds the page's rules and builds each card's trial. It is pure (no DOM or clock) and imports only `kanji-words.mjs` and `kanji-policy.mjs`; days come in as local `YYYY-MM-DD`.
  - `ROUND_SIZE` (9) and `ROUND_OPENS_AT` (`ROUND_SIZE`); `roundOpen(met)`, and `roundOpened(before, after)` for the unlock note.
  - `collection(kw, {order, keep})`: the met words as `{id, points, known, missed}` (points: passed trials), by met day, then vocabulary order. The page counts them; the Dictionary's *Kanji words* filter lists them.
  - `needsRound(kc, today)`: a round is drawn when none is saved for today, the saved one is empty, or it has more than `ROUND_SIZE` cards.
  - `todayGrid(kc, met)`: nine cells, `{kind: 'inert', word}` before the round opens, then `{kind: 'card', index, word, state}` (`open`, which includes a trial under way, `right` or `wrong`), filled with `{kind: 'blank'}`. Its `phase` is `waiting`, `none`, `playing`, `replay` or `done`, with `total`, `answered` (closed cards), `right` (passed) and `complete`. `cardState`, `roundTally` and `scoring(kc)` (false once the round is finished) are its parts.
  - The trial: `trialBank`, `trialQuestions`, `trialOf` and `trialOutcome` ([The trial](#the-trial), below). `roundSummary(kc, kw, today)`: `{right, total, known}` once every card is closed.
  - `trialEvent(word, passed, {today, met})`: the ledger entry of a closed, scored trial (below).
- **Before the round opens** (fewer than 9 met words), the grid shows the met words' cards, inert, and blank cells, with nothing written around them. No round is drawn.
- **The round.** `cardRound(kw, index, vocabulary, {now, size, usable})` in `kanji-words.mjs`, called with `size: ROUND_SIZE` and `usable` (whether the word's trial can be built today), gives `{day, cards: [{word}]}` for the local day of `now`. Its own default size, `CARD_ROUND`, is 10.
  - Order: words not yet known first, fewest passed trials (`points`), then the oldest `pointDay` (none counts as oldest), then an order seeded by the day. Known words follow as review: the least recently passed first (oldest `pointDay`), then the day's order. A review pass changes nothing about mastery; a review miss sets `missed`.
  - Two words with the same written form (出口 is `deguchi` and `exit`) give one card, and a word whose trial cannot be built is left out (a word no drill introduces has no intro line or sentence, so `deguchi` never is one), so a round can be short; its empty cell stays blank.
  - The same store, vocabulary and day always give the same round. The page draws it on the day's first visit (`kanjiRound` in `app.js`) and saves it at once, so a reload resumes it. A round from another day, a longer one, or one that no longer fits the content (a word gone, or an open card whose trial can no longer be built) is replaced.
- **A card.** It shows its word with `jp(word, {display: 'kanji', reveal: false})`, so no reading is in the markup, and nothing else: no pill or caption under it, and no round line under the grid while cards are open. A pass shows only as the theme's vermilion circle (`is-right`) and a miss as its faded ink (`is-wrong`). Its `aria-label` carries the word and its state: *お水* while open, *お水, passed today* or *お水, missed today* once closed. Tapping it opens its trial in place of the grid, with no new route (`kanjiTrial` in `app.js`, [The trial](#the-trial)). **Back to the round** returns to the grid with the card marked once closed, and focus on the next open card. A trial left before its last answer keeps its card open, and reopens where it stopped.
- **Done and replay.** With every card closed: *Done today · X of 9 right* (passed trials), *Shown in kanji with Show all kanji on:* with any of the round's words that became known today, **Play the cards again**, and *A replay gives no points. A new round comes tomorrow.* A replay (`replayRound`) clears the answers and keeps the round finished, so its cards reopen and its trials record and log nothing. When they are all closed, the done line shows the replay's score.
- **Collection: Library → Dictionary.** The page has no collection. Once a kanji word is met, the Dictionary (`#library/dictionary`, `dictionarySection` in `app.js`) adds *Kanji words* to the filters in its toolbar's Filter panel, after *Your words* and before the course word lists ([Vocabulary](#vocabulary)). *Kanji words* lists every met word in the order met, tapped or not, as a dictionary entry with fixed fields: the written form (kanji with its reading) and the meaning, then a small pill, *n of 3 trials* or *Mastered* (`kanjiStatus`, from `KNOWN_POINTS`), with **Details** and **Add to deck**. Its count line reads *12 kanji words met · 1 mastered.*, and the search applies to it too. In *Your words*, a met kanji word's entry shows the same pill in place of its reading-evidence line. The word card's kanji line gives the same status ([Word card](#word-card)).
- **Unlock note.** A lesson or practice meets its words in its drill, before it is finished. Its first finish therefore compares against a snapshot without the kanji words it introduces (`withoutUnitWords` in `app.js`), leaving out any that a unit finished earlier also introduces. The finish whose words take the met words to 9 or more names *In Kanji: your first round* among what unlocked (`unlockedSince`, `roundOpened`), as text with no link.
- **Not a practice.** There is no practice file and no `lessons.json` `practices` entry, `loop.mjs` has no hook for it, and `tools/loop-check.mjs` has no exemption: a practice without a home or `requires` fails, whatever its kind. Practice has no *Kanji words* group.
- **State.** `state.kanjiCards` is `{version: 2, day, cards, answers, finished}`: the day the round was drawn, its cards (`{word}`), each card's answers so far (`answers`, keyed by card position: one `true` or `false` per question answered, in order) and whether it was finished. A card is closed at its first `false` (missed) or after three `true`s (passed); fewer `true`s are a trial under way. The functions are `startRound`, `answerCard(kc, i, right)`, `cardAnswers`, `cardClosed`, `cardPassed`, `roundResult` (`{total, answered, right, complete}`), `finishRound`, `replayRound` and `roundDone(kc, now)`, in `kanji-words.mjs`, with `TRIAL_QUESTIONS` (3).
  - `readKanjiCards(value, {strict, wordIds})`, like `readKanjiWords`, reads a missing field as empty, so old saves and backups load and restore without it. A version 1 round (one question a card, `{word, ask, options}`, before 2026-10-23) also reads as empty, in both modes, so it is redrawn, and so does a round holding a `{char}` card (the first course build's character trial, retired 2026-10-29), so that build's backups restore.
  - Strict, for restore, refuses another version, missing or extra fields, a day that is not real, a card that is not exactly `{word}` with a kanji word ID, an answer list outside its card, empty, longer than three, not all `true`/`false`, or going on after a `false`, and a `finished` that is not true or false. It throws *This backup contains an invalid Kanji cards round.*
  - Lenient, for local load, drops a round it cannot use, and a new one is drawn.
  - No state version bump: the state version stays 3.
- **Ledger.** Each closed, scored trial is one entry in the outcome ledger (`schedule.mjs`): type `kanji` (in `TYPES`; `normalizeOutcome('kanji', {correct})`; `SOURCE_UNITS.kanji` is `answer`), `ref` the word ID, venue `kanji`, grade `good` for a pass or `again` for a miss, effect `recorded` (evidence only, no schedule), and `eligible` only when the word was met before today (otherwise reason `not-delayed`). Its id is `kanji:<word>:<day>`, so a word logs once a day. `readLedger` accepts it strictly and leniently. It is never a lapse, so it opens no recovery episode, and it is not a Today unit (`session.mjs` `TYPES` leaves it out), so no due count or forecast includes it. A replay logs nothing, and a trial under way logs nothing until it closes.

#### The trial

The owner's decision of 2026-10-23: a card is a trial of three questions, and a word is mastered after three passed trials on three different days. It replaced the one-question card (meaning or reading) and the 10 points that any right answer gave.

- **Bank.** `trialBank(content, index)` reads `lessons.json` once per content load into a Map of each kanji word's `{id, gloss, reading, written, pos, home, node, slot, intro, sentence}`: its vocabulary gloss and whole kana reading (`cardText`; katakana counts, so パン屋 reads パンや), its markup and part of speech, the lesson or practice whose drill introduces it (`home`, the first in content order) and that lesson's topic (`node`; a practice's through its home lesson), and two sentences:
  - `intro`: the drill's intro line, with the word's span (the longest of its patterns there whose tokens resolve to it, `blankWord`) replaced by ＿. `null` if no pattern resolves there, or the line still holds the word.
  - `sentence`: the drill's trial sentence (`sentences`), with `filled` (`ja` with `answer` in the blank). `slot` comes from it; a word without one has no slot.
- **Questions.** `trialQuestions(id, {bank, kw, day, index})` gives three questions, in this order, or `null` when one cannot be built (the word is then left out of the round). Each is `{ask, options, answer}`, `answer` the right option's index. The other met words are the store's words in the bank, apart from the word and any word of its written form.
  1. `meaning` (*What does this word mean?*): four `{word, text}` options, the word's gloss and three other met words' glosses, distinct as text.
  2. `reading` (*How is this word read?*): the same with whole kana readings; readings of the same kana shape (the same hiragana and katakana runs, then the same length, then one kana off) come first.
  3. `sentence` (*Which sentence does it fit?*): three `{word, ja, filled, en, slot}` options, each `ja` with its blank. The right one is the word's intro line on its first trial (`points` 0) and its authored sentence once it has passed one, each falling back to the other.
- **Wrong sentences.** Two other met words' authored sentences, chosen by the word's passes:
  - Tier 0 (no passes): words of another part of speech (vocabulary `pos`).
  - Tier 1 (one pass): the same part of speech, from another home lesson.
  - Tier 2 (two or more): as tier 1, from the same topic (`node`).
  - A trial fills from its own tier, then each lower tier in turn, then any met word. Every chosen sentence's slot differs from the word's and from the other's, its text differs, and it does not hold the word. A word with no authored sentence has no slot, so it is never a wrong sentence; as the target its intro line is used every time, and its wrong sentences only differ from each other.
- **Seeded.** Every draw and every option order is seeded by the word, the local day and the question, so the same store and day always give the same trial.
- **Answering.** `trialOf(kc, i, ctx)` is an open card's trial, `{index, word, questions, step, scoring}`, `step` the next question (the card's answers so far, all right); `null` for a closed card. `trialOutcome(trial, pick)` is `{index, word, step, ask, pick, correct, closes, passed, scoring}`: a wrong answer closes the trial, and so does the third right one (`passed`). The app saves `answerCard(kc, i, correct)` at once, finishes the round once every card is closed, and when the trial closes on the first pass records `recordTrial(kw, word, passed)` and logs `trialEvent`.
- **On screen** (`kanjiTrial` in `app.js`). One `.kanji-trial` slip holds an `<ol>` of blocks, one per question reached: the question, the word (`.kanji-trial-word`), the options (`.choices` of `.choice`) and the feedback (`.answer-feedback.correct` or `.incorrect`). Answered blocks stay above the next, so the paper theme's rule for any element holding `.choices` stacks them. The eyebrow reads *CARD 3 OF 9 · QUESTION 2 OF 3* (*· REPLAY* in a replay).
  - The word shows in kanji with no reading in the markup until the reading question is answered or the trial closes; then every block shows it with its reading.
  - Sentence options render through `jp()` in the learner's stages, the ＿ kept. Once answered, the right one shows filled, the word in kanji with its reading.
  - Feedback: the meaning answer shows the word and its gloss; the reading answer, the word with its reading, *is read* and the kana; the sentence answer, the sentence's English. A miss also shows the word with its reading and meaning, then *Closed for today, with nothing taken away · 1 of 3 trials passed*. A pass ends on *Passed · 2 of 3 trials* (from `KNOWN_POINTS`), *Passed · 3 of 3 trials. Known: shown in kanji when Show all kanji is on*, *Passed · a review of a known word*, or *A replay gives no points.*
  - After a close, focus moves to **Back to the round**; before it, to the next question.
- **Tests.** `tests/kanji-cards.test.mjs` (22 tests) covers the removal from content and the loop, the opening at 9, the collection, the grid and its phases (a trial under way stays open), `needsRound`, the bank (intro blanking, sentences, home and topic), the three questions (from met words, distinct, one right, kana readings, three slots, no sentence holding the word), determinism by day, the right sentence by passes and its fallbacks, the tiers 0, 1 and 2 on a made-up bank and their relaxation (tier by tier, then any word; slots never repeat; a slotless word is never a wrong sentence), `trialOf` and `trialOutcome` (the first miss closes, three right pass, a trial resumes), three passes on three days through closed trials, the done line, `trialEvent`, `cardRound` (determinism, words not yet known first, known words after by last pass, one card per written form, `usable`), the saved round, and the strict and lenient readers (a version 1 round reads as empty). `tests/schedule.test.mjs` covers the `kanji` ledger type. `tests/kanji-cards.browser.test.mjs` covers the frame (one subheader, no collection, no pills, no round line while cards are open, each card's `aria-label`), the waiting grid at 0 and 4 met words, the Dictionary's *Kanji words* filter (absent before a word is met; the met words in order with written form, meaning and pill; its count line and search; *Mastered* once known) and the word card's kanji line (*Met today*, *0 of 3 trials passed*, *Mastered*), the unlock note when f01 takes the met words to 9, a full pass (blocks added one by one, no reading before the reading question, sentence options in the learner's stages with their blank, the right one filled, a point, a ledger entry, focus), a miss on question 2 (closed, nothing taken away), a reload part-way through a trial, *Done today*, a replay with no points and no entries, no Kanji words group in Practice, three passes on three days (the page's clock moved a day at a time) making お水 known and then kanji in f01, a composite token restaged in place, backups (with and without a round, with an old editor draft, with a version 1 round, a bad round refused), a damaged local round, and 360 px.

### Kanji policy and course

Built 2026-10-28 (kanji Phase 6). The owner is splitting Niwa into two content profiles ([builds.md](builds.md)): `personal`, their own, and `course` (the classroom-support build), which will follow their textbook's kanji order (JLPT N5 onwards) with its own trial rules ([curriculum.md](curriculum.md#two-editions)). So the Kanji page's rules are a kanji policy in the content, the order of its cards may come from a course list, and a profile may add names and places that stay in kana, all read from that profile's `lessons.json` `kanji` block. The engine files are identical in both builds and never read the profile: everything that differs is in the `kanji` block. The engine is unchanged in shape: the policy only supplies the numbers and kinds it used to hold as constants. `DEFAULT_POLICY` reproduces the personal profile's rules as the owner decided them on 2026-10-23 ([Kanji cards](#kanji-cards), [The trial](#the-trial)), and the personal profile carries no `kanji` block, so it plays exactly as before.

- **Module.** `dist/kanji-policy.mjs` is pure, with no imports. It exports `DEFAULT_POLICY`, `QUESTION_KIND_NAMES` (`meaning`, `reading`, `sentence`), `MET_STYLES` (`kanji-above`, `furigana`), `readKanjiPolicy`, `policyProblems`, `rulesOf`, `readKanjiCourse`, `courseProblems`, `courseRank`, `eligibleWords`, `readProperNouns` and `properNounProblems`.

#### The policy

`content.kanji.policy` is merged over `DEFAULT_POLICY`, field by field. `DEFAULT_POLICY` is frozen: `{version: 1, roundSize: 9, opensAt: 9, questions: ['meaning', 'reading', 'sentence'], options: {meaning: 4, reading: 4, sentence: 3}, closeOnMiss: true, passAt: 3, knownAt: 3, order: 'met', reviewKnown: true, metStyle: 'kanji-above'}`. The character fields the first course build added (`characterQuestions`, the `kanjiMeaning`, `readingHere` and `chooseKanji` option counts, `unmetDistractors`) were removed with the set model, 2026-10-29 ([Kanji sets](#kanji-characters)); a policy naming one fails as an unknown field. The course's policy is `{version: 1, metStyle: 'furigana', opensAt: 0}`.

| Field | Default | What it changes | Valid values |
|---|---|---|---|
| `version` | 1 | The policy's format. | 1 |
| `roundSize` | 9 | The cards in a day's round (was `ROUND_SIZE`), and the grid's cells. | Integer 1–25 |
| `opensAt` | 9 | The met words before the round opens (was `ROUND_OPENS_AT`), and the unlock note's threshold. | Integer 0–100 |
| `questions` | `['meaning', 'reading', 'sentence']` | The trial's question kinds, in the order asked. | A non-empty list of distinct names from `QUESTION_KIND_NAMES` |
| `options` | `{meaning: 4, reading: 4, sentence: 3}` | The options shown per kind (was `TRIAL_OPTIONS`). | Integer 2–6 for each kind used by `questions`; a key that is not a name in `QUESTION_KIND_NAMES` fails |
| `closeOnMiss` | `true` | Whether the first wrong answer closes the card for the day. With `false`, every question is asked. | `true` or `false` |
| `passAt` | 3 | The right answers that pass a trial. With `closeOnMiss` it must equal the number of questions: all right. | Integer 1 to the number of questions; exactly that number when `closeOnMiss` |
| `knownAt` | 3 | The passed trials, on different days, that make a word known (was `KNOWN_POINTS`). | Integer 1–20 |
| `order` | `met` | How the round ranks the met words. `met` is the order of [Kanji cards](#kanji-cards): words not yet known first, by fewest passes, then the oldest pass, then a day-seeded order, and known words after as review. `course` keeps words not yet known before known ones, and within each group puts the course list's words first, in course order, then the rest in the `met` order. | `met` or `course` |
| `reviewKnown` | `true` | Whether known words come back in the round as review. With `false`, a round holds only words not yet known, and may be short. | `true` or `false` |
| `metStyle` | `kanji-above` | Unused by text display since 2026-10-29 (text no longer draws the met stage; still read and validated). Was: how a met word (introduced, not yet known) is printed everywhere text is staged ([Stages on screen](#stages-on-screen)). `kanji-above` is the kana with the kanji small above; `furigana` is the kanji with its reading small above, as a textbook prints it. Not-met and known words are unchanged. Added 2026-10-28 for the course. | `kanji-above` or `furigana` (`MET_STYLES`) |

- **Reading.** `readKanjiPolicy(content, {strict})` returns the merged policy, frozen; an absent `kanji` or `kanji.policy` gives `DEFAULT_POLICY` itself.
  - Strict, for the validator, throws on the first problem, an unknown key included.
  - Lenient, for the app at load, ignores unknown keys and falls back to the default field by field, so one bad field never stops the page.
  - `policyProblems(raw)` lists every problem, for the validator's report.
- **Card rules.** `rulesOf(policy)` gives `{questions, closeOnMiss, passAt}` (`questions` the number of questions), the card-closing rules that `kanji-words.mjs` takes. Its default, `DEFAULT_RULES`, is `{questions: 3, closeOnMiss: true, passAt: 3}`; frozen and cached per policy.
  - A card is closed at its first `false` when `closeOnMiss`, otherwise after `questions` answers; it is passed when it is closed with at least `passAt` answers right. `cardClosed`, `cardPassed`, `answerCard`, `roundResult`, `finishRound` and `readKanjiCards` take the rules as an argument; the saved-round reader treats an answer after a `false` as a fault only when `closeOnMiss` is true.
  - `recordTrial(kw, id, passed, now, {knownAt})` takes the threshold (default `KNOWN_POINTS`).
  - `cardRound(kw, index, vocabulary, {now, size, usable, rank, reviewKnown})`: `rank`, a course rank, puts the ranked words first in rank order, and `reviewKnown: false` leaves known words out.
- **The page's rules.** In `kanji-cards.mjs` every rule takes a `policy` (default `DEFAULT_POLICY`): `roundOpen(met, policy)`, `roundOpened(before, after, policy)`, `needsRound(kc, today, policy)`, `cardState`, `roundTally`, `todayGrid(kc, met, policy)` (`roundSize` cells, `need` from `opensAt`), `trialQuestions(id, {bank, kw, day, index, policy})`, `trialOf` (which also returns the trial's `rules`), `trialOutcome` (closes on a miss only when `closeOnMiss`; passed when it closes with `passAt` right) and `roundSummary`. `ROUND_SIZE`, `ROUND_OPENS_AT`, `TRIAL_ASKS` and `TRIAL_OPTIONS` stay exported, derived from `DEFAULT_POLICY`. The seeds are unchanged, so under the default policy every round and trial is identical to before.

#### The course list

`content.kanji.course` is `{version: 1, entries: [...]}`, one entry per kanji word in the course's teaching order:

```js
kanji: {
  policy: { version: 1, order: 'course' },
  course: { version: 1, entries: [
    { word: 'water', kanji: '水', level: 'N5', unit: 'Lesson 1', requires: ['lesson:f01'] },
    // …one entry per kanji word, in textbook order
  ] }
}
```

- `word`: a kanji word ID, a vocabulary entry with `{written|reading}` markup ([Engine](#engine)). Required, and unique in the list.
- `kanji`: its written form. Optional and informative only; nothing reads it.
- `level`: the course level, such as `N5`. Optional.
- `unit`: the textbook's chapter or unit label. Optional.
- `requires`: optional learning-loop references (`lesson:<id>`, `lesson:<id>/<part>`, `practice:<id>`, `book:<id>`, as in [Runtime rules](#runtime-rules)), all of which must be met before the word gets a card.
- **Reading.** `readKanjiCourse(content, {strict, wordIds})` gives `{entries, rank}`, `rank` a Map of word to its position; an absent course gives no entries. `courseProblems(raw, {wordIds})` lists every problem, and `courseRank(course, ids)` ranks a list of word IDs.
- **The gate.** `eligibleWords(met, {course, refMet})` gives the met words whose course `requires` are all met (`refMet` from `loop.mjs`, against the learner's progress). A word with no course entry is always eligible. A word still has to be met, by its lesson's or practice's drill, before it can be a card: the course orders and gates, and never introduces a word.

#### Names and places

`content.kanji.properNouns` is `{names: [...], places: [...]}`, written forms (`青木`, `上野`) that always stay in kana, as `PROPER_NOUNS` in `kanji-words.mjs` does for the shipped content ([Stages on screen](#stages-on-screen)). Both lists are optional.

- **Reading.** `readProperNouns(content, {strict})` gives the profile's lists, empty when absent; strict throws on the first problem, and lenient drops bad entries. `properNounProblems(raw)` lists every problem.
- **Merged, not replaced.** The app and the validator merge the lists over `PROPER_NOUNS` when they build the kanji word index, so each profile adds its own names and places and keeps the engine's.

#### Question kinds

`QUESTION_KINDS` in `kanji-cards.mjs` is a frozen registry of the three word kinds `meaning`, `reading` and `sentence`, each `{prompt, render, build}`. Every question built carries `prompt`, the text the app shows (*What does this word mean?*), and `render`, how the app draws its options: `text` (glosses), `kana` (readings) or `sentence` (sentences with their blank). `build` makes the question's options from the bank ([The trial](#the-trial)), with the number of options the policy gives its kind. The character kinds of the first course build (`kanjiMeaning`, `readingHere`, `chooseKanji`, and `CHARACTER_KIND_NAMES`) were removed with the set model, 2026-10-29; the set practice has its own kinds in `kanji-practice.mjs` ([Kanji sets](#kanji-characters)).

To add a kind: an entry in `QUESTION_KINDS`, its name in `QUESTION_KIND_NAMES` in `kanji-policy.mjs`, and, only if it renders its options in a way the three renderers do not, a renderer in `app.js`. A policy can then list it in `questions`.

#### In the app

`app.js` reads the policy, the course and the names leniently at start, from `lessons.json`, and builds the kanji word index with the merged names.

- `kanjiRound` draws `roundSize` cards from the eligible met words (the course gates checked with `refMet` against current progress), ranked by the course when `order` is `course`.
- The grid keeps its 3-column paper layout ([Kanji paper](#kanji-paper)), so a round of 6 is two rows and a round of 12 four.
- The trial slip shows each question's text from its `prompt` and its options by its `render`; its eyebrow counts the policy's questions.
- The done line, the *n of N trials* pills and lines (N is `knownAt`), the unlock note (`opensAt`) and backup restore (the rules) all follow the policy.

#### Validator checks

`tools/validate.mjs` fails on:

- a `kanji` key other than `policy`, `course`, `properNouns` and `characters`;
- any `policyProblems` or `courseProblems`, an unknown key in either included, and any `properNounProblems` (a malformed list);
- any `characterProblems`, and a track character missing from the brush font's cut ([Kanji characters](#kanji-characters));
- a course `requires` naming a lesson, part, practice or book that does not exist;
- `order: 'course'` with an empty course.

Its report prints the effective policy and the course's size. The other content minimums a profile may change (kanji words per lesson, unseen texts, stages per stone, kanji check sentences) are `curriculum.json` `rules` ([Content rules](#content-rules)). It builds the kanji word index with the merged names, so the `kanjiTokens` report counts a profile's own names and places as names and places.

#### Profiles and state

- **Profiles.** Each profile's build writes its own `lessons.json` ([builds.md](builds.md)). The personal profile carries no `kanji` block, so `DEFAULT_POLICY`; the course profile will carry `kanji.policy`, `kanji.course` and any `kanji.properNouns` its textbook needs.
- **State is per word.** A word's points, `known` and met day ([Engine](#engine)) belong to the word and survive a change of policy. A saved round that no longer fits the rules is reread leniently and redrawn ([Kanji cards](#kanji-cards), State). Lowering `knownAt` below a word's points makes it known on its next pass, the existing rule for stored points at or above the threshold. Raising it leaves a known word known: `known` is never cleared.
- No state version bump: `state.kanjiWords` and `state.kanjiCards` keep their shapes.

### Kanji sets

Rebuilt 2026-10-29 (engine; the first build of the same day, a daily round of character cards with three-pass mastery, is replaced). The course profile's Kanji page follows にほんごチャレンジ N4・N5 かんじ: 310 kanji in 31 book lessons of ten (Part 1, N5, kanji 1–110 in 11 lessons; Part 2, N4, kanji 111–310 in 20). Each book lesson is a **set**. The learner opens a set, reads its kanji one at a time (the kanji and its meaning, the sounds, the example words and special readings with this kanji's reading in red, the sentences), then answers the set's practice, five questions a kanji of five kinds, and the first finished practice opens the next set. Tap only: no typing, stroke order, writing, audio or parts question. The personal profile has no `kanji.characters`, so its track is empty and its Kanji page keeps the daily word-card round exactly as before; every branch tests `hasTrack(kanjiTrack)`, never the profile id.

- **Decisions (owner, 2026-10-29).** Mastery is undefined for now: nothing records points or known, and a practice finished with wrong answers still counts as done (no pass mark). The plain-kanji reward stays wired through `knownChars` (always empty), so mastery can be defined later without re-plumbing. A later cumulative test mode is left possible (questions are built from a list of kanji, not a set id) and not built. Course version only.

#### The content block

`lessons.json` `kanji.characters` is `{version: 1, lessons: [...]}`, no other keys; absent means no track. Authoring: `course/tools/kanji/` (one file per book lesson, its README and checker).

```js
characters: {version: 1, lessons: [
  {id: 'p1l01', part: 1, number: 1, title: '絵からできた漢字1', en: 'Pictographs 1', kanji: [
    {char: '山', number: 1, meanings: ['mountain'],
     on: [{kana: 'サン', romaji: 'san'}], kun: [{kana: 'やま', romaji: 'yama'}],
     story: 'Three peaks side by side: the outline of a mountain range.',
     words: [{written: 'ふじ山', reading: 'ふじさん', markup: 'ふじ{山|さん}', romaji: 'fujisan', en: 'Mt. Fuji', core: true}],
     special: [],
     sentences: [{ja: '{日|にち}よう{日|び}、ふじ{山|さん}へ いきます。', mark: 'ふじ{山|さん}', en: 'On Sunday I’m going to Mt. Fuji.', distractors: ['ふじやま', 'ふじざん', 'ふじせん']}],
     lookAlikes: ['出', '仙', '止']}
  ]}
]}
```

- **Lesson (a set).** `{id, part, number, title, en, kanji}`: `id` a unique slug (`p1l01` … `p2l20`); `part` 1 or 2, lessons listed in part order; `number` from 1, contiguous within its part; `title` (the book's Japanese) and `en` non-empty; `kanji` one or more characters.
- **Character.** `{char, number, meanings, on, kun, story, words, special?, sentences, lookAlikes}`:
  - `char` exactly one Han character, unique in the track; `number` the book's serial number, running 1, 2, 3 … across the track in lesson and list order. A lesson's `last` is the number of its last kanji.
  - `meanings` one or more English strings, the first the main one; a meaning option shows `meanings.join(', ')`.
  - `on` katakana (ァ–ヺ, ー) and `kun` hiragana with at most one `-` before the okurigana, each `{kana, romaji, variants?}`; at least one of the two lists non-empty. `romaji` lower-case letters, `-` and `'`. `variants` the book's sound-changed forms, `{kana, romaji?}`, `kana` with its leading or trailing dash (`-ガツ`, `イッ-`).
  - `story` the origin or mnemonic in the app's own words. `words` one or more `{written, reading, markup, romaji, en, core}`, `written` holding `char`, `reading` kana only, the core (★) words first. `special` optional `{written, reading, markup, romaji, en}` (the book's ＊ readings).
  - `sentences` two or more `{ja, mark, en, distractors}`. `ja` is markup with every kanji inside a `{written|reading}` token (kana readings) and every kanji a track character numbered at most this lesson's `last` (later or outside kanji are written in kana). `mark` occurs in `ja` exactly once, holds a token written with `char`, and reads as kana only. `distractors` three or more distinct kana strings, none the mark's reading.
  - `lookAlikes` three or more distinct single kanji other than `char`, in the track or not.
- **`markup`** (words and special readings), required on every entry: one without it is a `characterProblems` problem (the validator fails; a lenient read drops the character). The word as app markup, so the app can colour this kanji's own reading red and leave a neighbouring kanji's reading black: `{山|やま}{田|だ}さん`, `ふじ{山|さん}`, `{1|いち}{月|がつ}`, `{日|にち}{曜|よう}{日|び}`, `{分|わ}かります`, `{別|べつ}{々|べつ}に`, and one token where the reading does not split (`{今日|きょう}`, `{八百屋|やおや}`). Rules (`characterProblems` and `course/tools/kanji/check.mjs`):
  - it reads back exactly: `plainWritten(markup) === written` and `plainReading(markup) === reading`;
  - every kanji (々 included) and every digit is inside a token, and only kana outside; a token's written form is one run of kanji (one kanji, or a run whose reading does not split) or one run of digits (`{21|にじゅういっ}{世|せい}{紀|き}`), never kana; its reading is non-empty kana (katakana where the word's is: `インダス{川|がわ}`);
  - one token's written form holds `char` (words and special readings alike);
  - one kanji a token wherever the reading splits cleanly (the author's rule; the checker reports it, the engine does not test it).

#### The module (`dist/kanji-characters.mjs`)

Pure (no DOM, no clock) and imports only `kanji-words.mjs`.

- **Reading.** `characterProblems(raw)` lists every problem (empty for absent or valid). `readKanjiCharacters(content, {strict})` gives the frozen track `{version, lessons, chars, order, count}`: `lessons` with their kanji as entries, `chars` a Map of each character to its entry (its fields, `markup` included, plus `{lesson, part, lessonNumber, last}`), `order` the entries in track order, `count`. Absent is the empty track (`count` 0); `hasTrack(track)` is `count > 0`. Strict throws *This content has an invalid kanji character track: …*; lenient drops a bad character or lesson, keeps the listed order when only the numbering is broken, and gives the empty track for another version. `KANJI_CHARS_VERSION` (1) is the content block's version, `KANJI_STATE_VERSION` (2) the learner state's.
- **Markup.** `kanjiOf(written)` (the one in `kanji-words.mjs`): the distinct Han characters, 々 left out. `plainWritten(markup)` and `plainReading(markup)`: each token as its written form, or its reading. `wordTokens(markup)` → `[{text, reading}]` in order, a token's written form with its reading and the plain text between tokens with `reading: null`.
- **State.** `state.kanjiChars`, version 2:

  ```js
  {version: 2,
   chars: {[char]: {met: 'YYYY-MM-DD'}},              // the day its reading pages were finished
   sets:  {[lessonId]: {done: 'YYYY-MM-DD'}},         // the day the set's practice was first finished
   run:   null | {set: lessonId, seed: string, answers: [true|false, …]}}   // the set practice under way
  ```

  `emptyKanjiChars()` → `{version: 2, chars: {}, sets: {}, run: null}`. `readKanjiChars(value, {strict, chars, sets})` → `{kanjiChars}`, `chars` a Set or Map of the track's characters and `sets` its lesson ids (an array, Set or Map), each checked only when given. A **version 1** store (`{version: 1, chars: {[char]: {met, points, pointDay, known}}}`, the first course build) migrates, strict and lenient alike, keeping each entry's `met` (its entries checked by the old rules; `sets` {}, `run` null), so a backup from that build restores. Strict refuses another version, extra keys, a malformed entry (a version 2 entry is exactly `{met}`), a character or set not on the track, a malformed `sets` entry (exactly `{done}`, a real day) or a malformed `run` (`set` not a track set, `seed` not a non-empty string, `answers` not a list of booleans, or other keys): *This backup contains invalid kanji character records.* Lenient drops what it cannot use (a bad run is null). `sets` and `run` may be absent. `charStage(kc, ch)` → `'unmet' | 'met'` (`'known'` is kept in the union for when mastery is defined; nothing gives it). `markCharMet(kc, ch, now)`: a character already met keeps its day (the same store). `metChars(kc)` the met Set; `knownChars(kc)` always an empty Set. There is no daily trial: no points, no rounds and no cards for characters (the first course build's `recordCharTrial`, `nextUnmet`, `characterRound` and `withNewCard` were removed, 2026-10-29). Every function takes whatever store the caller holds: a store not in the version 2 shape is read leniently first.
- **Sets.** All take the frozen track and the store:
  - `setStatus(track, kc, id)` → `'locked' | 'reading' | 'ready' | 'done'`: `done` once `sets[id].done`; else `locked` while the set before it in track order is not done (the first set never is); else `ready` when every kanji of the set is met (the practice may start); else `reading`. null for an unknown id.
  - `setList(track, kc)` → `[{id, part, number, title, en, status, read, total}]` in track order (`read` the met count); `currentSet(track, kc)` → the first set not done, or null.
  - `boardCells(track, kc, id)` → `[{char, state}]` in the set's order: a met character `read`, the first unmet one `next`, every later unmet one `hidden`; empty for an unknown id.
  - `startRun(kc, id, seed)` → the store with `run: {set: id, seed, answers: []}` (replacing any run; the same store for a bad id or empty seed); `answerRun(kc, correct)` appends `correct === true` (the same store with no run); `finishRun(kc, now)` sets `sets[run.set].done` to the local day unless already set (a replay keeps the first day) and clears the run (the same store with no run); `runOf(kc)` → the run or null.
  - `setsOpened(track, before, after)` → the ids of sets locked in `before` and not in `after` (the finish screen's unlock reveal and notice): with sets done in order, the one after the set just finished.

#### The practice (`dist/kanji-practice.mjs`)

Pure (no DOM, no clock, no learner state); imports `seeded` and `shuffled` from `kanji-words.mjs` and the markup helpers from `kanji-characters.mjs`.

- `PER_KANJI = 5`; `KINDS = ['meaning', 'readingHere', 'chooseKanji', 'wordReading', 'soundMatch']`.
- `practiceQuestions(track, chars, seed)` → the ordered questions for `chars` (an array of track character entries, normally a set's ten; any list works, which is the door for a later cumulative mode), or null when the list is empty or any question cannot be built. Five rounds; round r asks every kanji once in a seeded order, kanji k (its index in `chars`) getting `KINDS[(r + k) % 5]`, so each kanji meets each kind once and a round mixes kinds. When a round would open with the kanji the round before ended on, its first two questions swap (never one kanji twice in a row, unless the list has one). Every draw is seeded by `seeded('kanji-practice|<seed>|<char>|<part>')` (the rounds' order with char empty), so the same seed rebuilds the same list and a reload resumes a run at `answers.length`. The questions are frozen.
- A question: `{kind, char, prompt, render, options: [{text, …}], answer, meaning}` (`answer` the right option's index; `[right, …wrong]` shuffled by the kanji's `<kind>|order` draw; `meaning` the kanji's `meanings.join(', ')`), plus the kind's fields. "The kanji met by now" below is the track's kanji up to the list's last one in track order: with sets opened in order those are exactly the met ones, and the questions stay independent of the learner's state.
  - `meaning`, *What does this kanji mean?*, render `text`: options `{char, text}`, four meaning texts distinct as text; the wrong ones from the other kanji of the list, then the kanji met by now, then any track kanji.
  - `readingHere`, *How is the red word read here?*, render `kana`, field `sentence: {ja, mark, en}`: `sentences[pick]`, pick a seeded index; options the mark's plain reading and three of its `distractors` (seeded). The app shows every token as kanji with furigana except the mark's, bare and red.
  - `chooseKanji`, *Which kanji is the red word written with?*, render `kanji`, field `sentence`: `sentences[(pick + 1) % n]` (a different sentence); options the mark's plain written form and three forms with `char` replaced (`written.replaceAll(char, x)`), distinct as text: `lookAlikes` in seeded order, then the list's other kanji, then any track kanji. The app shows the rest in furigana and the mark as its kana, red.
  - `wordReading`, *How is this word read?*, render `kana`, field `word: {written, reading, markup, romaji, en}`: a seeded core word whose written form is not the `readingHere` mark's (else any core word, else any word), shown as bare kanji with the tokens holding `char` red. Its options are the word's reading and three wrong readings: the first three distinct candidates that are kana only, differ from the answer and pass the possible-reading rule, taken tier by tier, each tier in its seeded order. Every candidate changes exactly one token's reading of the word's `markup` (kana outside tokens stays). The tiers (`wordReadingTiers`):
    1. the target token (the first holding `char`) with the kanji's other readings: its on readings in hiragana, its kun stems, and its variants without their dash, each followed by its rendaku form when the token is not the word's first; a token of several kanji (`{今年|ことし}`) takes every mix of its kanji's readings instead;
    2. one other token with one of its own readings: a track kanji by the same rules; a digit token from the numeral list いち/いっ/ひと, に/ふた, さん/みっ, し/よん/よ, ご/いつ, ろく/ろっ/むっ, しち/なな, はち/はっ/やっ, く/きゅう/ここの, じゅう/じゅっ/じっ/とお, ひゃく/びゃく/ぴゃく, せん/ぜん, まん;
    3. a voicing change of the first mora of the target token, then of another token's, then of a later mora of the target (みず → みす);
    4. slips on the target, then on the other kanji tokens: a long vowel dropped or added (う after an お-row mora, い after an え-row mora; never after a bare vowel, nor before ん, っ, ー or a vowel), a small っ removed, or one put before a か-, さ-, た- or ぱ-row mora (never first, nor after ん, っ, ー or a long vowel);
    5. other words' readings: the list's other words within one mora of the answer's length, then the list's other words, then any track word, ordered by how close their mora count is, then by the kana they share with the answer.

    The possible-reading rule: kana only; never opening with っ, ん, ー or a small kana; a small っ only before a か-, さ-, た- or ぱ-row kana; no ん twice in a row; no ぢゃ, ぢゅ or ぢょ. Short of three: null.
  - `soundMatch`, *Which kanji is read like this?*, render `kanji`, field `readings`: the on kana and the kun kana (dash shown as ・) joined by 、 (`サン、やま`); options `{char, text}` (text the kanji), four kanji: the list's other kanji, then `lookAlikes`, then the kanji met by now, then any; a track kanji sharing no reading with the target comes before one off the track, which comes before one sharing a reading; one whose readings equal the target's is never offered.
- `practiceGaps(track, chars, seed)` → `[{char, kind}]`, each question that cannot be built (empty when `practiceQuestions` gives a list): the validator names them.
- `practiceProgress(questions, run)` → `{at: answers.length, total, right, done: at >= total, missed}`, `missed` the kanji of the questions answered wrong, in question order, each once; answers past the last question are ignored.
- `feedbackOf(q)` → `{right, ja, en}`, the right answer whatever was picked: `ja` (markup, or null) first in furigana, then `right` (plain text), then `en` (or null). meaning `山 · mountain`; readingHere the mark, then *is read やま here*, and the English; chooseKanji *やま is written 山* and the English; wordReading the word, then its romaji, and the English; soundMatch *サン、やま is 山 · mountain*.
- `rubyHTML(markup, {hot, show})` → HTML, text escaped by the module: `show` `'furigana'` (the default; `<ruby>山<rp>(</rp><rt>やま</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>`, a token whose written form holds `hot` with `class="kanji-hot"` on its `<rt>`), `'kanji'` (written forms only, a hot token in `<span class="kanji-hot">`) or `'kana'` (readings only, a hot token's reading in the span). Plain text between tokens is escaped as it is. A sentence with a red mark: `ja` split at `mark`, `rubyHTML(before) + rubyHTML(mark, {hot, show: 'kanji' | 'kana'}) + rubyHTML(after)`.

#### The sets in the app

## Content rules

Built 2026-10-28. A few content minimums the validator used to hard-code are data, so a profile whose material differs can lower them without an engine change. The course profile follows a classroom textbook ([course-curriculum.md](course-curriculum.md)): its first lessons are kana-only, it ships no unseen transfer texts, and its stones are textbook lessons with one stage of parts. The engine files stay identical in both builds and never read the profile id ([builds.md](builds.md)); everything that differs is in the content.

`curriculum.json` may carry a top-level `rules` object, written by a profile's `seed-curriculum.mjs` when it needs one. A field left out keeps its default, and a `curriculum.json` without `rules` (the personal profile) gets `DEFAULT_RULES`, which reproduces the earlier limits exactly:

```js
rules: {
  version: 1,
  kanjiWordsPerLesson: { min: 1, max: 3 },   // min 0–3; max from min to 10
  unseenTexts: { min: 4, max: 6 },           // min 0–6; max from min to 6
  stagesPerStone: { min: 3 },                // min 1–10
  kanjiCheckSentences: { required: true },   // true or false
}
```

The course sets `kanjiWordsPerLesson.min: 0`, `unseenTexts.min: 0` and `stagesPerStone.min: 1` in `course/tools/seed-curriculum.mjs`, and from its kanji lessons (Lesson 6 on, up to 19 kanji in a lesson) `kanjiWordsPerLesson.max: 10` and `kanjiCheckSentences.required: false`.

`dist/content-rules.mjs` (pure, no imports, in the style of `kanji-policy.mjs`) exports `DEFAULT_RULES` (frozen), `readContentRules(curriculum, {strict})` and `rulesProblems(raw)`. Strict reading throws on the first problem, an unknown key included; lenient reading keeps the default for each field that fails (a max below the kept min falls back to its default, which is never below the top of the min's range). A field may be a number range (`min`, `max`) or a boolean (`required`). `rulesProblems` lists every problem.

Where each rule is enforced, all in `tools/validate.mjs`:

| Rule | Check | With the lowest values |
|---|---|---|
| `kanjiWordsPerLesson` | Every unit lesson introduces min–max kanji words in its kanji words drill ([Kanji words drill](#kanji-words-drill-phase-3-unit-format)); `checkUnit` takes max as `kanjiWordsMax` (default 3) for the drill itself, in lessons and practices. | min 0: a lesson may have no kanji words drill; one that has a drill still introduces at most max. |
| `unseenTexts` | The content ships min–max unseen transfer texts ([Unseen transfer texts](#unseen-transfer-texts)). | min 0 with no texts: the negative fixtures and the record round-trips, which use the first text, are skipped. |
| `stagesPerStone` | Every topic node lists at least min stages (with its three outcomes and one expansion). | min 1: a stone may be one stage of parts. |
| `kanjiCheckSentences` | `required: true`: every kanji word whose reading can fade (met in a dialogue or passage) has a `kanjiChecks` sentence ([Kanji in new sentences](#kanji-in-new-sentences)); `validateKanjiChecks(checks, content, {coverage})` in `vocabulary.mjs`. | `required: false`: a fadeable word may go without one (the course never fades a reading before mastery). Every sentence present is still validated, and the round-trips still skip when there are none. |

The validator fails on any `rulesProblems` (prefixed *Content rules:*), then runs its checks under the lenient reading, and its report prints the effective rules as `contentRules` with their `source` (`defaults` or `curriculum.json`). `tools/loop-check.mjs`, `tools/coverage.mjs` and `tools/content-inventory.mjs` hold none of these minimums.

The validator's other round-trips no longer need a particular piece of content: with no kanji word in the vocabulary the reading-evidence round-trip is skipped, and with no `kanjiChecks` sentence the new-sentence round-trip is (coverage is still checked while `kanjiCheckSentences.required` is true: every word whose reading can fade needs a sentence). Two needs stay, and fail with a plain problem rather than a crash: the first passage must have recall (immediate questions and harvest phrases), and the Learn path must have at least one checkpoint boundary. The state-migration check uses the personal practices `restaurant-review` and `everyday-dishes` only when both exist; otherwise it round-trips a loop record naming the content's first practice.

A new rule: add it to `DEFAULT_RULES` and its range to `RANGES` in `content-rules.mjs` with the default that reproduces today's check, use it in the validator, and document it here.

## Lesson shape

Required: `id`, `node`, `title`, `track`, `minutes`, `goal`, `context`, `dialogue`, `question`, `choices`, `correct`, `feedback`, `kanji`, and `editorialStatus`. A lesson with a unit also has `focus`, `words` and `sections`; every lesson has one at present.

- `title` appears exactly once in its node's outline, and `track` names the outline stage that lists it.
- `minutes` is set by the seed from the unit's counted items: `minutesFor(n)`, about 3 + n ÷ 3. It stays internal: lesson rows, lesson headers, Library cards and reading headers show it only as a length label, *Short* up to 5 minutes, *Medium* above 5 up to 15, and *Long* above 15, with a hidden "Length:" for screen readers. A missing or invalid value shows no label. Lessons and passages use the same rule. Review due times and relearning delays stay numeric.
- `goal` and `context` open the lesson's opening page as its goal line and scene; the unit's `focus` follows them as the Focus panel.
- `dialogue` has at least two `{speaker, ja, en}` lines. The unit's dialogue section shows them with a *Meaning* toggle on each line.
- `question`, `choices`, `correct` and `feedback` are the lesson's original single-choice question: `correct` indexes the choices and `feedback` explains every choice. Each lesson also needs a transfer question under its ID in `checks.mjs`. A unit's まとめ review reuses them where they still fit; a lesson without a unit shows the two as one drill.
- `kanji` records have `word`, `reading`, `meaning`, and `note`.
- `editorialStatus` stays a draft label; nothing is marked as fluent-speaker reviewed.

A lesson with a unit no longer ships `why`, `contrast`, `production`, `model`, `rubric` or `productionChecks`, and the validator rejects them. A lesson without a unit needs `why`, `contrast` and `rubric` (at least two entries each), `production`, `model` and `productionChecks`; its two questions become items `q0` and `q1` (`fallbackItems`). No lesson is in that state now.

Use `{written|reading}` for Japanese words requiring support, for example `{水|みず}`. The renderer escapes content before turning this notation into ruby. Do not embed arbitrary HTML in content. Word readings belong to the particular context, not to a universal character replacement table.

### Units

A unit is `{focus, words, sections}`. The teaching rules, skeleton and counts are in [content-standard.md](content-standard.md#a-complete-lesson).

`focus` is one or two sentences of English with Japanese in `{written|reading}` markup: what the unit covers and what to practise ([content-standard.md](content-standard.md#focus)). The seed copies it to the lesson as `focus`, and the lesson page shows it as a Focus panel under the scene, inside the lesson body (hidden while a due review is answered first). `checkUnit` checks it when present (text, readings, at most two sentences and 45 words); `tools/validate.mjs` requires it on every shipped unit.

`words` lists 6–12 entries in display order, each a vocabulary ID or `{id, frame?, recognize?}`. `frame` is the particle frame the word is used in, in `{written|reading}` markup (`［N］を ください`); `recognize: true` marks a word to understand rather than use. Every ID exists in the vocabulary and appears once. The lesson page renders the list as the Words table: Japanese, meaning, word type (`pos`) and frame (a column shown when any word has one), with a small *recognize* tag. Each word opens its word card and can be dragged to a deck.

`sections` run in order. Each is one of:

- `{kind: 'point', id, title, pattern, patternId?, explain, examples, note?}`. `patternId` says the point teaches a catalog pattern (see [Grammar patterns](#grammar-patterns)). `pattern` marks slots with full-width brackets ［ ］ and renders as the pattern box. `explain` keeps to three sentences and under 60 words. `examples` are 3–4 `{ja, en}`. `note` is one caution line.
- `{kind: 'dialogue'}`: the lesson's `dialogue` at this position. A lesson with conversation lines has exactly one.
- `{kind: 'practice', id, format, instruction, stem?, example?, items, final?, patternId?, reasons?, kanjiWords?, intro?}`: one numbered drill in one format. `kanjiWords` and `intro` make it a kanji words drill ([Kanji words drill](#kanji-words-drill-phase-3-unit-format)). `reasons: false` keeps the drill out of reason checks ([Reason checks](#reason-checks)); a `choice` or `blank` item may carry `reasons: false` too (never a 例). `patternId` on the set, or on one of its items (which wins), says those items test that pattern; only such items take part in reason checks. Drills are labelled Practice A, B, C… in order. `instruction` is one English line; above it the app shows a default Japanese line per format (`STEMS`, for example グループに わけましょう。), which `stem` overrides. `example` is an optional legacy field, validated when present but never rendered, counted or reviewed. New drills should omit it: question screens show no worked example and no Example toggle. Only the last practice set may have `final: true`: it renders as the まとめ Review, every item names its own `format`, and formats may mix. An item of the final set may carry its own `stem` (text), which replaces its format's default line for that item. In other sets an item's `format`, when given, matches its set's.

Section and item IDs use letters, digits and hyphens (up to 60 characters) and are unique within the lesson. Item IDs may not be `q0`, `q1`… or the reserved answer keys `review-round`, `review-shown`, `review-focus`, `reason-v` and `reason-attempt`, nor the retired pretest keys (`pretest-v`, `pretest-items`, `pretest-phase`, `pretest-fast`), which load as dropped.

### Item formats

| `format` | Fields | The learner | Counted units |
|---|---|---|---|
| `choice` | `prompt`; optional `ja` (a Japanese line shown large), `mark` (part of `ja` to underline: it appears exactly once and never splits markup), `card` (2–8 short lines shown as a menu, sign or receipt), `kanjiWord` and `ask` (a kanji words item: [Kanji words drill](#kanji-words-drill-phase-3-unit-format)); `choices` (2–4, all different), `correct`, `feedback` (one per choice) | Taps a choice | 1 |
| `blank` | `ja` with exactly one `（　）`; optional `en`; `choices`, `correct`, `feedback` | Taps a chip, which fills the gap | 1 |
| `order` | `en` (what to build); `tiles` (2–7, in the correct order); optional `given` (leading tiles placed already, leaving at least two to place), `extra` (1–2 tiles that do not belong and never repeat a real tile), `accept` (other correct orders as arrays of tile indexes, never moving a given tile); `explain` | Taps tiles into the line (tap again to send one back, drag along the line to reorder), then **Check** | 1 |
| `star` | `tiles` (exactly 4, all different, in the correct order), `star` (the slot 0–3 holding ★); optional `before`, `after`, `en`; `explain` | Taps the tile that belongs in ★; the whole sentence is then shown | 1 |
| `match` | `pairs` (3–8 `[left, right]`, both sides all different); optional `explain` | Taps a left item, then its partner | 1 per pair |
| `truefalse` | `statement`, `answer` (true or false), `explain` | Taps ○ or × | 1 |
| `sort` | `groups` (2–3 different labels), `cards` (4–10 `[text, groupIndex]`, all different); optional `explain` | Taps a card, then its group | 1 per card |
| `cloze` | `lines` (`{speaker?, ja}`, with gaps `（1）`, `（2）`… numbered in reading order), `bank` (different words: the answers plus 1–2 that fit no gap), `answers` (one bank index per gap); optional `en`; `explain` | Taps a gap (the next empty one is active), then a word from the box | 1 per gap |

Choices, chips, order and ★ tiles (with `extra`) and bank words have no fixed display: each `{kanji|reading}` token shows in the learner's kanji stage, so `checkUnit` (`lookAlikeProblems`) rejects two entries that read the same, ignoring spaces, once reduced to their written form (`{来|く}れば` and `{来|き}れば` both show as 来れば once 来 is known) or to their reading (`{今|いま}` and `いま`).

A lesson has 1–50 counted units (`TOTAL_RANGE`), usually 35–50; the minimum rejects empty content rather than requiring padding. `checkUnit` checks coverage structure separately as editorial warnings; `countUnits` and `unitKeys` give the count and the keys: the item ID, or `<itemId>.<n>` for each pair, card and gap. See the [generated inventory](content-inventory.md#lesson-sequence-and-companions) for current lesson counts and [lesson-audit.md](lesson-audit.md) for the dated trim history.

Grading (`gradeChoice`, `gradeTrueFalse`, `gradeOrder`, `gradeStar`, `gradeMatch`, `gradeSort`, `gradeGap`): choice and blank compare the index and return that choice's feedback; true/false compares the answer; an order is right when it uses every real tile and no extra tile and reads as the authored order or an accepted one (compared as joined text, so identical tiles may swap); a ★ answer is right when the chosen tile equals the ★ tile; match, sort and cloze grade each pair, card and gap. An answer that names no listed choice, tile, card or gap throws and records nothing.

Display orders are seeded by item and lesson ID, so a reload shows the same order, and never start in answer order: choices (`choiceOrder`), the tile bank, which never spells an accepted answer left to right (`tileBank`), ★ tiles (`starOrder`), the right-hand match column, where no meaning sits beside its own word (`pairOrder`), sort cards, never listed group by group (`cardOrder`), and the cloze box, never in gap order (`bankOrder`). The validator checks every shipped item.

### Results, completion and lesson reviews

Exercise results are plain values in `state.answers[lessonId]`:

- `<unitKey>` is `right` (first answer correct), `wrong` (first answer wrong, not yet corrected) or `fixed` (correct after a miss). The first result is kept: a later miss never turns `right` into `wrong`. A wrong choice is locked and the learner tries another; a wrong tile line can be cleared and built again; a wrong pair, card or gap flashes and waits for another try. Legacy numeric choices under `q0` and `q1` still read as right or wrong.
- `progress` counts solved units (`right` or `fixed`) of the total, and the first-try score (`right` of those answered). The lesson's finish page shows “*x* of *y* correct · first try *a* of *b*”.
- **Finish lesson** is enabled when every counted unit is solved (`lessonComplete`). The first finish adds the lesson to `completed` and silently enters it in the review queue at 1 day (`enterReview`); finishing again changes no schedule. The finish screen reveals what the completion unlocked ([Lesson access](#course-progression)) and says nothing about reviews. **Do the exercises again** clears the item results (`resetItems`), keeps review progress and opens the first practice page. Since 2026-10-29 a finished lesson never shows **Finish lesson** again, on a revisit or after *again*: only the progress line, the status (*Lesson finished. Every item is correct.*, or *… · n to go.*) and, while every item is solved, **Do the exercises again**.
- The optional delayed lesson and book reviews (Practice's and Reading's *Recall after a delay* lists, `#review/<id>` and `dist/delayed-review.mjs`) were removed on 2026-10-29 at the owner's direction; reviews stay parked ([requirements](requirements.md#reviews-are-parked)).

### Paged lessons and runs

Lessons and runs show one page at a time ([requirements.md](requirements.md#phone-interaction-requirements)). `dist/pages.mjs` is the page model: dependency-free, with no DOM, unit-tested against every lesson in `tests/pages.test.mjs`. `unitPages(unit, {words, opening = true, finish = true, chunk = WORDS_PER_PAGE, title})` takes any unit-shaped set (`{sections}` as in `lessons.json`; a run's own section may carry a `label`, and kind `page` is a plain reading page) and returns `{pages, sections}`:

- **Pages**, in order: the opening page; the Words table at most eight rows a page (`words` is the number of rows the table shows); one page per point; one per practice item, in set order, with the まとめ's returned items already appended to its items; one for the conversation; the finish page. A page is `{kind, section}`, plus `{from, to, part, parts}` for words (rows `from` to `to - 1`) or `{item, k, of}` for an item.
- **Sections**, in page order, each `{key, kind, label, first, last}` (its page range), plus `{final, items}` for a practice set: the opening (labelled with `title`, the lesson's or practice's own title, else Opening; its key stays `opening`, so saved places resolve), Words, Point *n*, Practice A…, Conversation, まとめ Review, Finish. `sectionKeys` gives each section's key, its own ID or else `section-<i>`, so no two share one.
- `pageLabel` (“Practice B · 3 of 6”, “Words · 1 of 2”; a set of one item has no counter), `pageKey` (`item:<id>`, `words:<part>` or `<kind>:<section>`, stable while pages are added) and `findPage`.
- `sectionStatus(model, state)` and `setsLeft`: with `state(id)` saying `'solved'` (right in a lesson, answered in a closed run), `'answered'` or `null`, each set's items left and the page of the first.
- `resumePage(model, state)`: the first page while nothing is answered; the finish page (else the last page) once every item is solved; otherwise the furthest answered item's page, or the page after it when that item is solved.

`unitPager(root, {model, state, place, hold})` in `app.js` shows the pages. The view renders everything and the pager only toggles `.off-page`: on blocks marked `data-page-block="<section key>"`, on items marked `data-page-item="<id>"` and on the rows of a `data-page-rows` container, so drill bindings, results, returned items and word references work as before. It fills its slots: `data-pager-progress` (the track), `data-pager-contents` (a `<details>` whose summary is the position, “15 / 47”), `data-pager-dock` (Back and Next, which waits, disabled, on an item until `state(id)` is `'solved'` and on a Words page until its words are tapped (below), and a swipe with it; a deck tray placed there shows only on pages with words to drag, once there is a deck), `data-page-count` (a set's or the Words table's counter), `data-page-first` (shown on a section's first page only, such as a run's intro) and `data-page-remaining` (the finish page's sets with items left). It sets `--dock-h`, the dock's height, on its root, so a word-bank item's word box can stay just above the dock. It keeps the page per route (`place`) for the session and persists lesson/practice page keys as answers[id][unit-page]. Valid keys resume on reload; retired keys fall back to resumePage, so a lesson can be left on any page (the pause note on Point and Conversation pages was removed on 2026-10-28). `hold()` keeps the dock and contents back while a reading check is open, until `release()`; `sync(model)` takes added pages and keeps the page on screen; `reveal(el)` opens the page that holds an element; `refresh()` repaints after an answer. Lessons, checkpoint runs and practice sets use it.

Words pages wait too (2026-10-29). Lessons and practice sets pass `wordsSeen(wordId)`, read from boolean `seen:<wordId>` keys in `state.answers[<unitId>]` beside `unit-page`. Tapping a Words row's word (`bindUnitWords(unitId, onSeen)`) saves its key before the card opens, then refreshes the pager. Next and the swipe then wait on a Words page until every word in its rows `from`…`to - 1` is seen. A word still to tap has a small muted dot (`.unit-word.to-tap`), and a word known from elsewhere still needs a tap in this unit. While the learner is on or before the first Words page with a word left, every contents section after that page is disabled; Back and earlier sections stay open. The keys persist, so a revisit is not gated again: `resetItems` keeps them, a practice's *again* keeps them, and backups accept them as booleans. `resumePage` is unchanged. Checkpoint and review pagers pass nothing.

A practice set (`practiceRun` in `app.js`, built in 2a) renders inside `pagedHTML`:
- its opening block: title, its home lesson's topic and length, and its Focus;
- `wordsTableHTML(set, 'practice:<id>')`, whose word cards record the practice as where a word was met;
- `unitHTML` and its finish block.

It binds its drills with its own drill context (`practiceCtx`: results in `state.answers[<id>]`, no reason checks), and calls `unitPager` with `unitPages(set, {words})` and a `state` read from its own results. Its completion, results and unlocks stay in its view ([Completion](#completion)). A locked item never reaches this code: its view returns before rendering a pager.

A practice in parts (2026-10-29, [Practice parts](#practice-parts)) has no single run: `#practice/<id>` is its part list (`partList`), rows in the topic page's style with no pager. Each part, `#practice/<id>/<part>` (`partRun`), is paged exactly as a set is, from a part-shaped unit (`partUnit`: the part's words and sections, its word occurrences re-pathed into them), with the same `practiceCtx` and its saved page under `unit-page:<part>`.

### Retired pretest and prequestion

Units used to open on three guesses with a fast-track, and readings on an unscored prequestion ([content-standard.md](content-standard.md#focus)); Focus replaced both. Records saved then still load:

- `readReasons` drops the retired unit keys, `pretest-v`, `pretest-items`, `pretest-phase`, `pretest-fast` and every `pretest:`, `pretest-ok:`, `again:` and `fast:` key, with the `why:` checks of those occurrences (`isRetiredKey`). It does so on load and on restore alike: a backup is never refused for them. Item results, `return:` keys, review keys and reason checks of the unit itself are kept, and an interrupted pretest opens the ordinary unit.
- `completed` and `reviews` are untouched, so a unit finished by fast-track stays finished and keeps its review schedule; no *Fast-tracked* label is shown any more.
- A passage draft saved with `guess` (the prequestion pick) loads without it, its answers and support marking kept.

The cleaned records are held in memory at load and written back with the next save.

### Reason checks

After a right first answer on a sampled `choice` or `blank` item ([content-standard.md](content-standard.md#reason-checks)), `reasonCheck(unit, item, seed)` returns `{options, answer, source}` or `null`. The options are the item's own correct-choice feedback and the correct-choice feedback of two other items in its drill (one per distinct line, compared without reading markup, a leading "Correct." or case), in a seeded order. `reasonSeed(lessonId, mode, round, occurrenceId)` names the occurrence: mode `learn` with `reason-attempt` or `review` with `review-round`. A `return:` copy uses its source item's drill. Only pattern-tagged items take part (2026-10-29): an item without a `patternId`, its own or its drill's (as `unitItems` resolves it), is never asked and never offers its line, so vocabulary, meaning and comprehension items ask nothing; a drill with fewer than three different lines among its tagged items asks nothing either. Items marked `reasons: false`, items in a drill marked `reasons: false`, and every other format take no part. The record is plain values in `state.answers[lessonId]`:

- `reason-v` (`1`) and `reason-attempt` (a count raised by **Do the exercises again**, so a new attempt draws a new sample).
- `why:<answer key>`: `pending` (saved before the check is shown), then `right` or `wrong`, the first answer kept. The answer key is the occurrence's own ID, or `review:<itemId>` in a lesson review. A pending check keeps the unit from completing (`progress().pending`) and leaves a review answer unscored; once the reason is picked, the review answer is recorded as right only if the reason was right too.
- `return:<itemId>`: the まとめ reattempt of a drill item whose reason was wrong (`returnItems`), a unit key like any item's, added at the end of the まとめ; an item in the まとめ gets none.

`finishReview` drops `why:review:` keys; `resetItems` clears the `why:` keys of the items it resets. `readReasons(record, unit, {strict})` checks a saved record against the unit as it is now: the lenient read on load drops a check with an unknown version or value or an unknown item, and a backup with one is refused. A well-formed check on an existing item that no longer takes part (such as one saved before 2026-10-29 on an untagged item, which then no longer keeps the unit waiting) is dropped on restore too; a backup is refused only for malformed checks (2026-10-29); retired pretest keys are dropped in both reads ([Retired pretest and prequestion](#retired-pretest-and-prequestion)). Older records without these keys need nothing, and items answered before reason checks existed are never asked. A soft miss is recorded through `recordMiss(…, {reason: true})`, which counts and schedules like a miss and adds one to the pattern record's optional `reasons` tally.

### Unit checks

`checkUnit(unit, {lessonId, vocabulary, dialogue, patterns, kanjiWords, practice})` returns `{problems, warnings, count}` as readable sentences naming the lesson, section and item. `kanjiWords` is the vocabulary's `kanjiWordIndex`, for kanji words drills ([As built](#as-built-phase-3a)). Problems fail `tools/validate.mjs`: the field rules above, unknown or repeated word IDs, repeated section or item IDs, a dialogue section missing or repeated, a final set that is not the last, kanji without a reading or broken `{written|reading}` markup anywhere in the sections or frames, the kanji words drill problems of `kanjiWordProblems`, a correct choice whose feedback only says “Correct”, an order item in which every arrangement is accepted, a `patternId` that is not a lowercase ID or (when `patterns` is given) not in the catalog, a `patternId` on a solved 例, a `focus` that is not text or has kanji without readings, a `reasons` field that is not `false` (or one on a 例), and an empty lesson or a count above 50. Warnings are the softer targets: a `focus` of at most two sentences and 45 words; 6–12 words; 2–3 points; meaning, building and application checks with about 8–14 items after each point; a まとめ review of 4–10 items; 2–4 examples; explanations of at most three sentences and under 60 words; feedback of at most 20 words; item explanations of one or two sentences; one or two bank words that fit no gap; and unexpected fields.

### Unit files and the seed

`tools/units/<lessonId>.mjs` exports `unit` (`{focus, words, sections}`) and, optionally, `newWords`: vocabulary records the unit needs (`id`, `word`, `reading`, `meaning`, `example`, `pos`, `definition`, optional `pack`, which defaults to `reading`, `senses` and `forms`). `tools/seed-lessons.mjs` imports every file in the folder; the file name is the lesson ID, and a file without a matching lesson in the seed is an error. It attaches `focus`, `words` and `sections` to that lesson, sets `minutes` from the count, removes the fields a unit replaces (and word references into `contrast`), and adds new words with the seed's provenance. A new word whose ID already exists is accepted only when the record is identical. Title, track, goal, context, dialogue, question, kanji and word references stay in the seed. The seed adds reading support to bare kanji in shipped text; every other section field, kanji words drills included, passes through unchanged.

## Passage and vocabulary shapes

Passages contain `id`, `title`, `level`, `minutes`, `context`, paragraph strings, glossary pairs, optional-support translation, open prompts under `questions` (`type`, `prompt`, `answer`; shown unscored as *Take it further* after the reveal), and a `recall` object, described next.

### Passage recall

`recall` carries everything the read → hide → answer → reveal → recall-later loop needs (the protocol is in [content-standard.md](content-standard.md#passage-recall-protocol)):

- `purpose`: the reading's Focus, one or two sentences saying why the learner is reading and what to follow and remember, without the answers. The reading opens on it, above the text, with no step before; it is hidden with the text for the questions. The retired `prequestion` is no longer shipped and fails validation.
- `ideaUnits`: `[{id, text, critical, evidence}]`. `id` is `<passageId>-u<n>` and never reused. `text` is the English proposition. `critical` marks units essential to the reading purpose. `evidence` is a non-empty list of spans `{path, start, end, surface}`: `path` is `paragraphs/<n>`, offsets index the authored string including ruby markup, and `surface` repeats that slice, so an edit that shifts the text is caught. A span never splits a `{written|reading}` token or a word occurrence.
- `immediate` and `delayed`: question sets in the single-choice shape plus `id` (`<passageId>-i<n>` or `<passageId>-d<n>`), `kind` and `units`. `kind` is `gist`, `idea`, `detail`, `sequence` or `inference`; `units` lists the idea units whose evidence answers the question, and the reveal highlights their spans. An `idea` check uses exactly the scale *The passage says this.* / *The passage says the opposite.* / *The passage does not say.*, in that order. Each set needs at least four questions: one gist, two idea checks, one detail or sequence item and one inference item (including "what can't we conclude?" items). No delayed prompt repeats an immediate one, and the delayed set samples every critical unit. Choices other than the idea scale are shown in a seeded order that changes with each attempt; after a lapse, the delayed set also rotates which critical item comes first.
- `harvest`: two or three `{id, phrase, meaning, context}`. `id` is `<passageId>-h<n>`. `phrase` is authored markup that appears whole in `context`, a span covering its passage sentence. Each phrase becomes a review item: the sentence returns with the phrase blanked, its meaning as the cue, and the passage's other harvested phrases as the choices. `recovery` is `[{id, ja, en}]`, at least two (three are authored) new sentences holding the same phrase, id `<harvestId>-r<n>`, authored in `tools/recovery-sentences.mjs` and attached by the seed. They are asked only in recovery (see [Recovery](#recovery)). `recoveryProblems` in `recovery.mjs` rejects a sentence that lacks the phrase or holds it twice, does not end a sentence, leaves a kanji without its reading, uses a `{kanji|reading}` token found nowhere else in the course, repeats a course line, or runs past three sentences or 48 characters; it also rejects a passage none of whose phrases sits in an idea unit's evidence.

In `tools/seed-lessons.mjs`, idea-unit evidence and harvest contexts are written as `[paragraph, sentence]` pairs (sentences end at 。); the seed computes the offsets on the final text. `tools/validate.mjs` (through `recallProblems` in `recall.mjs`) rejects missing purposes, purposes over two sentences, a `prequestion`, fewer than four idea units or two critical ones, malformed IDs, spans that do not match the text or split markup or references, sets without the required functions, unknown unit IDs, idea checks off the scale, repeated delayed prompts, critical units the delayed set never samples, and harvest phrases outside their sentence or without two distinct alternatives.

Each passage also carries `node`, its course topic, and its loop fields `home`, `requires`, `introduces` and, for an optional book, `optional` ([Learning loop](#learning-loop)). Since Phase 3 (2026-10-23) the Library shelves a book under its home lesson and opens it on its `requires`, not on its topic; `node` still decides which passages an unseen text's source lesson may name.

**Repeated attempts and completion (Phase 3, 2026-10-23).** The recall store has always allowed any number of immediate attempts: `startAttempt` starts a new draft whenever none is in progress, and the store keeps the last 40 attempts (`ATTEMPTS_MAX`). An immediate attempt from memory with every first response correct completes the passage (`completesPassage`), once, in `state.loop.books`, so the completion survives that trimming. Until then the result offers **Try again** in place: the page renders again with a fresh supported read (the delayed set is skipped even if it is due, `passageView(id, {again: true})`), then a new immediate attempt. Each attempt's choices are ordered by a seed from its own start time (the draft's `started`), so a new attempt has its own order. A delayed attempt never completes a passage. The reading's status line says what completes the book until it is completed.

### Unseen transfer texts

`lessons.json` `transfer` is `{version: 1, texts: [...]}`: short texts read once and answered from memory, set in a venue no lesson teaches (`transfer.mjs`; authored in `tools/transfer-passages.mjs`). They are a collection of their own, never listed as passages, so passage lists and review queues never include them. Each text has:

- `id` (`t01` …), `genre` (`notice`, `message`, `receipt`, `menu` or `sign`), `title`, `venue` (English only, so the venue adds no Japanese), `level`, `minutes`, `context`, `focus` (the Focus line shown above the first reading), `translation` and `editorialStatus`.
- `sources`: `[{lesson, passages}]`. Every word, form, function word and pattern in the text comes from these lessons. Each lesson names the passages that come before the text: its own topic's passages, or, for a topic without passages (Basic sentences), a passage that reuses a word from its Words table. `sources` is the language contract `transferProblems` checks; since Phase 3 it no longer decides when a text opens. The text's `requires` does, and its chain must hold every source lesson and passage (`tools/loop-check.mjs`).
- `home`, `requires`, `introduces` and, if optional, `optional`: the text's loop fields ([Learning loop](#learning-loop)). A text opens once every reference in `requires` is met, with no delayed recall (learning-loop.md rule 8).
- `lines`: the text, one authored string per line, with ruby markup. `occurrences` are word references on `lines/<n>` in the usual shape; `grammar` lists every function-word token `{path, start, end, surface}`. Together they cover every character except punctuation, spaces and numerals (`NOTATION`).
- `patterns`: `[{line, pattern}]`, sentence-level references to catalog patterns.
- `ideaUnits`: as for passages, with evidence spans on `lines/<n>` (whole lines from the seed).
- `questions.immediate` (first reading) and `questions.delayed` (the one delayed check), each recall items first, then at least one evidence-line item. Recall items are `gist`, `detail` or `idea` (the fixed scale *True* / *False* / *Cannot conclude*) in the single-choice shape plus `units` and `targets: {words, patterns}`, the words and patterns the item actually tests. A gist item may test none; every other item names at least one, each a word in its evidence lines or a pattern declared on them. An evidence-line item (`kind: 'line'`) names one idea unit and offers some of the text's lines (`lines`, indexes); the right one is evidence for that unit and no wrong one is. Every set needs a gist, two idea checks, a detail and an evidence line; the delayed set asks new questions and samples every critical unit.

`transferProblems` (run by the seed, which stops on a problem, and by `tools/validate.mjs`) resolves what each source lesson teaches from its unit, not from the vocabulary pool: a word must be in a source lesson's Words table or word references, in a form those lessons show (or the record's own form); a function word must come from the closed list `FUNCTION_WORDS` or be a clock time, and be attested as a whole piece of a source lesson's tiles, choices, banks, point patterns, examples or dialogue; a vocabulary word is never accepted as a function word. It rejects an uncovered or overlapping span, a word, form, function word or pattern outside the sources, an unknown pattern, a source without a real prerequisite passage, a broken evidence span, an evidence line that is not evidence, a question target outside its evidence lines, and Japanese in a question, choice, feedback, idea unit or note that is not a quote of a line on token boundaries. String checks cannot show that a pattern annotation is grammatically right or that a distractor is fair; those need educator review.

### Vocabulary

Vocabulary contains `id`, `word`, `reading`, `meaning`, `example`, `pack`, `pos`, `definition`, and `provenance`, plus optional `senses` and `forms`. There is no `audio` field, and the validator rejects one.

- `id` is a stable lowercase slug. Never reuse or rename one: learner backups store discovered word IDs.
- `word` is the citation form in `{written|reading}` markup. `reading` is kana only and must equal the markup's reading, for example `お{水|みず}` → `おみず`.
- `meaning` is the short English gloss shown in lists. `definition` is a fuller explanation of use; mark Japanese inside it with ruby like any other text. `pos` is the word type shown in the Words table and on the word card.
- `pack`: `core` and `plus1` are the course word lists, shown in Library → Dictionary as *Core words* and *Restaurant extra* (below); `reading` words are met in context and only reach the learner through discovery. Every `reading` word must be referenced at least once, by an occurrence or a lesson's Words table (`wordSources`).
- `senses`: `[{id, gloss, definition?}]` for words whose meaning depends on context (すみません: attention or apology; できる: possible or newly opened). When a word has more than one sense, every occurrence must name one.
- `forms`: `[{id, form, label}]`, authored surface forms such as `{辛|から}かった` · “plain past: was spicy”. They are editorial records, not generated conjugations.
- `provenance`: who authored the entry and its review status.

**Library → Dictionary** (`#library/dictionary`, `dictionarySection` and `bindDictionary` in `app.js`) is one column: the label *Search by kanji, kana, or English*; a toolbar of the search field and two icon buttons inline to its right, a funnel (*Filter words*) and sliders (*Change what entries show*); the open panel, if any, as a card directly under the toolbar; the count line; and the list. Each button toggles its own panel (`aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`). Opening one closes the other, Escape inside an open panel closes it and returns focus to its button, and both start closed. The layout panel holds the field list (`dictionary.fields`: tick to show, arrows to reorder, the first shown largest) and a preview; it stays open while the learner adjusts it, and the entries follow at once. The Filter panel holds one filter group, *Words shown*. *Your words*, the default, lists the learner's discovered words. *Kanji words* joins it once a kanji word is met ([Kanji cards](#kanji-cards)). The course word lists follow, *Core words · 12* and *Restaurant extra · 24* (`WORD_PACKS`, counted from `pack`). Choosing a filter applies it, closes the panel and returns focus to the Filter button. For any filter but Your words, the button shows a dot and names it (*Filter words, showing Core words*), and the count line names the list. A word list shows every word of its pack in vocabulary order, met or not, as dictionary entries with **Details** and **Add to deck**. Details opens the word card without recording a discovery (only opening a word in a text does); adding a word to a deck records one, as for any word added outside a text ([Flashcard decks](#flashcard-decks)). Its count line reads *12 words in Core words.*, or *2 of 12 words in Core words match “noodles”.* while searching. With no query, **Practice these 12 words** sits above the entries, after *You can study these words now.* on Restaurant extra while Everyday dishes is not open. It starts a meaning quiz in place of the list, which records nothing: the pack's words in random order, *Which word means “water”?* with three choices from the pack, **Next word** once right, *WORD n OF N*, and a finished card, *All 12 words done*. While it runs, the label, the toolbar, both panels (an open one closes) and the count line are hidden. **← Back to the list** or the finished card's button brings them back and returns to the list, with focus on the Practice button. The heading's count stays the discovered words. The packs had a Library section of their own, Word lists, until 2026-10-23: an old `#library/words` link replaces itself with `#library/dictionary`, leaving no history entry, and the Dictionary opens on Core words; a later visit opens on Your words.

`kanjiReadings` (top level of `lessons.json`, authored in `tools/seed-lessons.mjs`) lists common readings per character, on'yomi in katakana and kun'yomi in hiragana, for example `文: ["ブン", "モン", "ふみ"]`. It is used only to build plausible wrong answers in reading checks (ちゅうもん → ちゅうぶん). The correct answer always comes from the reference's own markup. Leave out readings that would also be acceptable in context. Every character in a referenced kanji word needs an entry.

### Word occurrences

Lessons and passages carry an `occurrences` array. Each entry is `{path, start, end, surface, word, sense?, form?}`:

- `path` is a slash-separated location inside the same object: `paragraphs/0`, `dialogue/2/ja`, `question`, `choices/0`. A lesson's transfer check in `checks.mjs` is addressed through the named bank `transfer/…`. Passage recall text is addressed inside the passage: `recall/immediate/<n>/…`, `recall/delayed/<n>/…` and `recall/harvest/<n>/phrase`.
- `start`/`end` are offsets into the authored string, including ruby markup. `surface` repeats that exact slice, so any edit that shifts the text is caught.
- References never split a `{written|reading}` token and never overlap on the same path. Inflected surfaces keep their authored form and link to one stable `word`; `form` and `sense` name the variant used here.

Do not hand-write offsets. In `tools/seed-lessons.mjs`, list each reference under `refs` as `[surface, wordId, {sense?, form?}]` for its path; repeated surfaces are matched in order. The seed computes offsets after reading support is added. `tools/validate.mjs` rejects unknown word, sense or form IDs, unresolvable paths, mismatched surfaces, split ruby, overlaps, unreferenced context words, and paragraphs without references. For kanji words it also requires a kana reading for every kanji in each reference, each `{written|reading}` token to match the word or one of its forms, and at least two distinct wrong readings for every reference that can appear in a reading check.

Passage paragraphs and lesson dialogue lines render references as buttons that open the word card. A unit's words are reached through its Words table, and a unit section can also carry references in two places: a point example (`sections/<i>/examples/<n>/ja`) and the Japanese line of a choice item (`sections/<i>/items/<k>/ja`). Legacy worked-example references (`sections/<i>/example/ja`) remain valid data but are not rendered. They are listed under `unitRefs` in `tools/seed-lessons.mjs` by item, worked-example or `<point>/<n>` ID, so a moved item keeps them, and the seed turns them into these paths after the lesson's other references, whose indices stay as they were (f01, card 0044). These buttons open the word card but are not dragged to a deck (the card's deck chooser adds the word), so the deck tray stays on the Words and conversation pages. A referenced marked word keeps the drill's underline. A reading target in a kanji words item stays plain text until the item is solved, so neither a button nor a card shows its reading; then it becomes its word button with the reading shown. Answer choices, tiles and gaps never carry references, and an item brought back to the まとめ after a wrong reason shows its line without them. References in a lesson's question and its transfer check are not shown while the lesson has a unit; references in passage recall questions are listed beside the question, never inside an answer button.

Two dictionary records can share a headword. Existential いる (`iru-exist`, h02, card 0048) and need (`need`, s05) are both written いる, and their polite forms います／いません and いります／いりません also occur as endings of other verbs (ございます, 会います, 住んでいます). The word ID in an explicit reference, never the headword or a surface match, says which record an underlined word opens: every います, いますか and いません in h02, `people-and-pets` and ph02 names `iru-exist` with its form, and s05's and ps02's references stay on `need`. The lesson-drill check in `tools/loop-check.mjs` looks for words by surface, and it skips kana-only words of one or two characters that are not nouns, so it never reports いる, in h02 or in an earlier lesson's suffix tiles (`tests/loop-check.test.mjs`). That limit is deliberate; explicit references cover these words instead.

### Word card

Tapping a word in the Words table, a dialogue line or a passage opens a compact card (`wordDetails` in `app.js`; the standard is in [content-standard.md](content-standard.md#word-card)). In order, it shows: the word type, with a *recognize* tag when the lesson lists the word that way; the headword with furigana, labelled *dictionary form* when the tapped text uses another form, and its kana reading only when that differs from the markup; the gloss of the sense used here, or `meaning`; *here:* the tapped form and its label, when it differs or names a form; the lesson's frame; the sense or word definition, only when it says more than the gloss; one example; the word's forms on one line; one kanji line, *Kanji* and a met kanji word's trial status (*Met today* for a word met today with no pass yet, *2 of 3 trials passed*, *Mastered*, or *Mastered · its reading shows again until you read it right* after a miss; `kanjiStatus`, from `KNOWN_POINTS`), or for a kanji word not met, its reading-evidence status when it has evidence; and **Add to deck**. Nothing empty is shown, and there is no audio row, *In this text* list or *Also appears in* list. Opening a word from a text records its discovery.

Saves and backups from before the removal of typed answers and listening load cleanly: `production`, `listening`, `replies` and `conversations` are dropped, review keys starting `listen:`, `reply:` or `talk:` are dropped (`LEGACY_REVIEW` in `recall.mjs`), and Today history drops units of the removed `reply` type.

Saves and backups from before kanji Phase 3c (2026-10-23) load cleanly too, and a backup is never refused for them: `kanjiParts`, `kanjiMeanings` and `kanjiReview` are dropped (`RETIRED_STATE` in `app.js`), and so is the removed editor's `draft`, ledger entries of type `component` or `meaning` are dropped (`RETIRED_TYPES` in `schedule.mjs`), and Today drops runs and history units of those types, as it does `reply` units (`LEGACY_TYPES` in `session.mjs`).

### State versions 2 and 3

The current state `version` is `3` (since 2a, 2026-10-22); the storage key stays `niwa-v1`. Saves and backups migrate step by step on load or restore (`migrateState` in `recall.mjs`), and any other version is not read.

- **Version 3** adds `loop`, the learning loop's finishes ([State](#state)). The restaurant review's and Everyday dishes' flags, `coreCheck` and `plusDone`, become their practices' finishes, and they and the `core` and `plus1` answer records are dropped. Passages and unseen texts already completed by a clean attempt get their finish from history. `loop` is read strictly on restore (`readLoopState`); in browser storage, what cannot be used is dropped.
- **Version 2** widens `reviews` and adds `recall`. A version 1 save or backup keeps its lesson reviews unchanged, and `recall` starts empty.

- `reviews`: `{[key]: {count, due, lastPractice?, lapses?}}`. The key is a lesson ID (as in version 1), `passage:<passageId>`, or `phrase:<harvestId>`. `count` indexes the 1, 3, 7, 14, 30-day intervals; `due` and `lastPractice` are timestamps; `lapses` counts returns to 1 day. A lesson enters the queue at 1 day when it is first finished; a passage and its harvested phrases enter it when the immediate set is finished. A correct delayed answer on a due item moves it to the next interval (30 days repeats); a wrong one returns it to 1 day. A lesson review counts as correct when it has at most one miss. An item that is not due keeps its schedule, so same-day repeats never advance it. A passage advances only when every delayed answer is correct and given with the text hidden. The Today badge that counted every due review unit is gone with Today.
- `recall`: `{version: 1, passages: {[passageId]: {attempts, draft?}}}`. `attempts` holds at most 40 records, oldest first: `{at, set, mode, items, interval?, supportFrom?, corrections?}`. `set` is `immediate` or `delayed`; `items` is `[{q, choice, correct}]` with question IDs from that set, the **first responses**, which never change; `interval` is the review interval, in days, that a delayed attempt checked.
  - `mode` is `closed` (the text stayed hidden) or `supported` (the text was open at some point). Once supported, an attempt never returns to closed. `supportFrom` (supported attempts only) counts the first responses given before the text was opened; older supported records without it read as 0.
  - `corrections` (supported attempts only) is `[{q, choice, correct}]`: at most one second try per missed first response, with a different choice, made with the text open after the miss. Corrections are kept apart from `items` and never change the score, the schedule or the stage.
  - An attempt is **from memory** when every first response came before the text was opened (`closed`, or `supportFrom` equal to the number of items). Only first responses count: a delayed attempt advances its passage only when it is from memory and every first response is correct.
  - `draft` is the attempt in progress: `{set, started, mode, answers, corrections, supportFrom?, interval?}`, with `answers` in the item shape. It is saved at every step, so a reload, a visit to another page or a visit from a word review resumes it at the first unanswered question with its first responses and support marking unchanged. Rereading before a delayed recall starts a supported draft before the text appears. A draft ends only by finishing it (`finishAttempt`), which appends it to `attempts`; there is no way to discard a supported draft and start again closed. The next attempt starts closed. A draft saved with the retired `guess` loads without it.
- Passage stages shown to the learner come from the attempts: *not read yet*, *read with the text open*, *answered from memory* (an attempt from memory), *recalled after a delay*, and *retained* (a delayed attempt from memory at the 7-day check or later with no critical idea unit missed and at least 70% of first responses correct, as in content-standard.md). They describe evidence and are not mastery claims. Flashcard ratings, completion and reading evidence never feed them, and passage attempts never feed those.

Restore rejects review keys that name no lesson, passage or harvested phrase (keys from removed features are dropped instead), review records with other fields or non-numeric dates, a recall store with another version, attempts for unknown passages or questions, more than 40 attempts, items whose `correct` does not match the answer key, `supportFrom` on a closed record or beyond its first responses, corrections on a closed record, on a correct first response, repeating the first choice or repeating a question, and malformed drafts (a supported draft without `supportFrom`, an `interval` on an immediate draft, or duplicate answers). In browser storage, a malformed draft is dropped.

In passage assessment the text, glossary, translation, word references and word details are not in the document while the text is hidden; only the question, its choices and the words listed beside it (without details until the answer) are. *Look back at the text* asks for confirmation before it marks the attempt supported. After a missed first response the learner can look back and choose once more, or see the answer.

Version 1 had added further optional fields, which version 2 keeps. Older backups without them restore with the defaults:

- `discovered`: `{[wordId]: {at, source?}}`. Opening a word in context adds it once. Later openings change nothing. `source` is `passage:<id>`, `lesson:<id>` or `practice:<id>` (a practice set's Words table or conversation; since 2b a lesson that became a practice has its `lesson:` sources rewritten, [State](#state)). Discovery never changes `completed`, `reviews`, answers or unlock flags, and the interface does not present it as mastery.
- `dictionary.fields`: the ordered list of visible dictionary fields. The options are `reading`, `gloss`, `definition`, `written`, `example` and `forms`. The default is `reading`, `gloss`, `definition`, and at least one field always stays visible.

- `decks`: an ordered array of personal flashcard decks, described below.
- `readingEvidence`: per-word reading evidence for kanji words, described below.
- `wordReviews`: due dates for flashcard prompts and the current recall session, described below.
- `daily`: Today runs, described below; kept and validated, no longer read by the UI.
- **Superseded (kanji Phase 2, 2026-10-23):** `reading` is no longer saved. A save or backup that has it migrates to `showAllKanji` ([Stages on screen](#stages-on-screen)). It was `kana` (Kana first), `kana-kanji` (Kana + kanji above), `ruby` (Kanji + readings: readings fade only for retained words) or `full` (Kanji + all readings). The toggle cycles them in that order. All four restyle the same `{written|reading}` ruby, so content needs nothing per mode: in `kana-kanji`, `{遅|おく}れます` shows おくれます with 遅 above おく, and a compound such as `{映画館|えいがかん}` keeps 映画館 as one note over えいがかん. Written-form fields (the word card headword, word tables, a flashcard's written field) keep kanji with furigana in every mode, and reading-check targets stay plain kanji without a reading. Deck labels for a word in a question follow the kana-led modes (`kana`, `kana-kanji`) by showing its reading.

On load, invalid or unknown entries in browser storage are dropped. On restore, they reject the whole backup with a message, and the current state is left untouched. If browser storage is unavailable, the passage and dictionary views say so and suggest exporting a backup, and the app footer shows that warning in place of “Progress is saved in this browser.”

Local previews and hosted pages have different origins and therefore different browser storage. Use export and restore to move progress. No cross-device sync, microphone use, or remote personal-data storage is implemented.

### Flashcard decks

Each deck is `{id, name, front, back, cards, created}`:

- `id` is `deck-` followed by lowercase letters, digits and hyphens. `name` is 1–60 characters with whitespace collapsed, and unique per learner ignoring case. Names are learner text, and the interface always escapes them.
- `front` and `back` are ordered lists of the dictionary field IDs (`reading`, `gloss`, `definition`, `written`, `example`, `forms`). Each face needs at least one field. A field may appear on only one face, so a prompt never shows its own answer. The presets are *Kana → definition* (`[reading]` → `[definition]`) and *Kana → written form + definition* (`[reading]` → `[written, definition]`). The deck format is independent of `dictionary.fields`.
- `cards` is `[{word, at, source?, occurrence?, rating?, rated?}]`. `word` is a vocabulary ID. `source` (`passage:<id>`, `lesson:<id>` or `practice:<id>`) and `occurrence` (an index into that text’s `occurrences`) record where a word was collected in context; `occurrence` never appears without `source`. Cards never copy dictionary text, so a format change or a corrected entry shows on every card at once. A word appears at most once per deck but may sit in several decks. `rating` and `rated` are a legacy per-deck self-rating (`again`, `hard` or `good`) kept for older state and backups. Recall no longer writes them, and they earn no review stage: scheduling lives in `wordReviews`, described below.

On a prompt face, written forms appear without readings so the reading can be recalled. On the answer face they appear with readings. A word without kanji shows its kana as the written form, labelled *kana only*. A face whose fields are all empty for a word shows the kana reading instead. During practice, the answer face is not in the document until the learner reveals it.

Adding a word that has not been discovered records its discovery with the card’s `source`, or `source: "deck:<id>"` when it was added outside a text. Adding a word that is already in the deck changes nothing, so the first source is kept. Removing a card or deleting a deck leaves `discovered`, dictionary settings and lesson progress untouched. A deletion can be undone from the Library until the learner leaves it or creates or renames a deck. On load and restore, any deck word missing from `discovered` is added, so every deck word is in the dictionary. Restore rejects unknown deck or word IDs, duplicate decks, names or cards, invalid formats, malformed ratings and malformed card sources, and, given the content's practice IDs (`readDeckState(…, {practices})`, as the app passes them), a `practice:<id>` source naming no practice.

### Word review scheduling

`wordReviews` is `{version: 1, limit, items, session}`. It is separate from lesson `reviews`, `completed`, unlock flags and reading evidence, and nothing in it changes them.

- **Prompt identity.** A record is keyed `<wordId>|<front fields>><back fields>`, each side sorted, for example `water|reading>definition,written`. Reordering fields within a face keeps the key. Moving a field to the other face, or adding or removing one, makes a new prompt with its own schedule. The earlier record is kept and resumes if that format returns. The same word and format in several decks is one record and one place in a session. Removing a card, deleting a deck and undoing a deletion leave records untouched.
- **Record.** `items[key]` is `{word, front, back, stage, due, last, rating, count, lapses, supported, extra}`. `stage` 0 is unearned. Stages 1–5 mean intervals of 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days. `count` is the number of ratings applied. Legacy `card.rating` values earn nothing, so every prompt starts unseen.
- **Rules** (`nextReview`, with an injected clock). A prompt is due when `due ≤ now`, and an unseen prompt is always due. When due, an unaided Good advances one stage (the first Good gives 1 day) and holds at 30 days. Hard, or Good with support, keeps the current interval, which is 10 minutes at stage 0. Again resets to stage 0, due in 10 minutes, even before the due time. Any other rating before the due time is extra practice: it is counted in `extra` and leaves the schedule unchanged. These intervals are an initial policy, not a demonstrated optimal schedule.
- **Support and exposure.** A hint (`hintFor`: the first character of a Japanese answer field, or the initials of an English one) can be shown only before the reveal. It is stored on the session item and counted in `supported`. `answerExposure` flags a prompt when a front fallback shows a back value (for example a kana-only written form while the reading is tested), or when every back value already appears on the front. Exposed attempts are practice only. In both cases Good cannot lengthen the interval.
- **Sessions.** `limit` is 1–50 prompts, 5 by default. `buildSession` takes overdue prompts first (earliest due first), then unseen prompts in deck and card order, up to the limit, within one deck or `scope: "all"`. With nothing due or unseen, *Practise early* takes the soonest scheduled prompts as extra practice. Otherwise the view says nothing is due and shows the next due time. `session` is `{id, scope, created, limit, early, items}`. Each item is `{key, word, front, back, base, exposed, revealed, support, rating?, outcome?, retry?}`, and is saved at each step, so a reload resumes before the reveal (answer still hidden, hints kept), after it (answer shown) or after the rating (next prompt).
- **Applying once.** `submitRating` needs the current, revealed item, and applies only when the record's `count` still equals the item's `base`. A repeated click, a reload or an older session for the same prompt therefore adds no credit. A stale item closes with outcome `stale`. Again adds a `retry` item, at most twice per prompt, to show the answer again later in the session. Retries happen before the new due time, so they are practice.
- **Restore.** A missing `wordReviews`, `limit` or counter takes its default. Restore rejects an unknown version, an out-of-range limit, a key that does not match its record, unknown words, invalid or overlapping faces, stages outside 0–5, malformed times, ratings or counts, and malformed sessions. In browser storage, invalid records and sessions are dropped.

### Contextual collection

One operation adds a word to a deck from its context, used by **Add to deck** on the word card, by the words listed beside passage recall questions, and by the deck tray. Exercise words are the occurrences whose path is a question bank’s `question` or `choices/N` (`exerciseOccurrences(container, prefix)`). They are listed below the question, never inside an answer button. Passage recall questions use prefix `recall/immediate/<n>/` or `recall/delayed/<n>/` and the deferral key `passage:<passageId>|<questionId>`, which reads its answer from the recall draft. Before the first response, their words show as written, without details, and **Add to deck** queues them; the queue applies when the feedback for that response appears, and leaving the page discards it.

### Returning to a word's source

After the reveal, the answer face of a word review shows where the word was met (`wordContext` in `flashcards.mjs`): the `source` recorded on the card (the deck under review first, then any deck), then the dictionary entry's first `source`, then the first passage, lesson or practice set that references the word or lists it in its Words table (`wordSources`, in that order). The line shown is the sentence around an occurrence in the text body (a passage's paragraphs, or a lesson's or practice set's conversation); a recorded occurrence in a question or choice gives way to the word's first body occurrence in the same text, so the answer face never quotes an assessment item. A word listed only in a Words table links to its open lesson or practice set without a line. A `practice:<id>` source links to `#practice/<id>` while the set is open, started or finished (since 2b; a word met only in the Dating practices, such as 趣味, has one as its source). When the source lesson or practice set is not open, the source title is plain text and its sentence stays hidden. A passage with a recall due or a draft in progress keeps its sentence back. A link opens the full source. The review session is saved, so returning resumes the revealed prompt, focuses the source link again, and the rating still applies once; the lesson or passage page offers *Back to your review*. Looking up the source changes no state, visiting it is an ordinary visit to that text, and flashcard ratings never count as comprehension, completion or reading evidence.

Before a question is answered, its words show only as written in the question. They do not open details, and **Add to deck** queues the word instead of adding it. The queue (`queueCollection`, `dequeueCollection`, `commitCollections`) holds one entry per deck and word and applies when that question’s feedback appears; a deck deleted in the meantime is reported and skipped. Leaving the page discards queued entries, so nothing is added without feedback.

On a lesson or reading, the deck tray lists decks as drop targets. Holding a marked word (in the Words table or the text) for 450 ms and dragging it onto a deck performs the same operation. `dragStep` defines the states: *pending* (pointer down), *dragging* (held without moving more than 10 px), *dropped* (released over a deck) and *cancelled*. Movement before the hold, pointer cancellation, lost pointer capture, Escape, release away from the tray, leaving the window and navigation all cancel without changing any deck. Dragging is never required: every drop has a click, tap or keyboard equivalent in **Add to deck**.

### Reading evidence and furigana fading

**Fading superseded (kanji Phase 2, 2026-10-23).** Reading evidence no longer hides readings: each word's display comes from its kanji word stage ([Stages on screen](#stages-on-screen)). The evidence is still recorded as below and the reading check still runs before texts; the word card and dictionary entries show its status line only for a kanji word not met, since a met word's line is its trial status ([Word card](#word-card)). Since 2026-10-23 the check asks only met kanji words; its answers were progress points until the Kanji card trial took points over the same day, and now only mark a word met or missed ([Build phases](#build-phases), follow-ons). *Fades* now only marks the *retained* stage, and the check's notes say *retained* where they said the reading would hide. A later phase merges the two records.

`readingEvidence` is `{version: 1, words: {[wordId]: {met?, log, transfer?}}}`. `met` is when a text first showed the word with its reading. `log` holds at most 60 events, oldest first: `{at, kind: "check", source, occurrence?, correct}` for a reading check, or `{at, kind: "reveal", source, occurrence?}` for a tap that showed a hidden reading. `source` is `passage:<id>`, `lesson:<id>`, `practice:<id>` (a practice set's conversation, since 2b), `context:<sentenceId>` (a kanji check sentence, below) or `transfer:<textId>`, and `occurrence` indexes that text's `occurrences`.

- A check is **delayed** when it falls on a later calendar day and at least 12 hours after the previous sighting (first meeting, check or reveal). Same-day repeats never count.
- A word's reading **fades** (`readingState` returns `faded`) after correct delayed checks in at least two different sources since the last wrong answer. A wrong answer clears that evidence, so the reading is shown again. A reveal is a sighting, never a failure.
- Stages shown to the learner: not read in a text yet, seen, practised, retrieved (one delayed context) and retained (two). They describe evidence and are not mastery claims.
- Opening a passage or lesson records first meetings for the kanji words in its reading text (a passage's paragraphs, a lesson's dialogue), then offers up to six due words. Both count only words the learner has met in a kanji words drill (since 2026-10-23). A due word was met before and last seen on an earlier day; words missed last time come first. Retained words return for a maintenance check after 14 days. Each item shows the word in its own sentence without a reading, and other words in the same check lose their readings there too. The learner chooses from up to four readings (`readingChoices`). Skipping records nothing.
- Superseded in Phase 2, kept for the record: fading applied only in Kanji + readings mode and only to referenced words. The ruby markup stays in the page: the reading is hidden and a ring marks the word. The first selection shows the reading; the next opens the word card. Kana first, Kana + kanji above and Kanji + all readings always show readings, and the first selection there opens the word card.

Restore rejects an unknown version, unknown word IDs, logs over 60 events, events with an unknown kind, a missing or extra `correct`, or a source that is not a passage, lesson, practice set, context sentence or unseen text (given the content's practice IDs, `readReadingEvidence(…, {practices})`, a practice set the content has), and malformed `transfer` records. On load, a malformed `transfer` record is dropped and the rest of the word's evidence is kept.

### Kanji in new sentences

Fading shows that a word was read in the texts it was learned from. The **Kanji in new sentences** review asks for the reading somewhere the learner has never read it, next to words that look alike. It ran at `#readings` and as kanji reading units in Today's run; both are parked since 2026-10-22 (`#readings` replaces itself with `#field`). The content, scheduling and evidence rules below stay, validated, for the later review design.

`curriculum.json` holds `kanjiChecks: {sentences, lookAlikes}`, authored in `tools/seed-curriculum.mjs`:

- A sentence is `{id, word, text, target, en}`. `text` is one short line in `{written|reading}` markup, with a reading for every kanji, ending in 。, ？ or ！. `target` is the exact marked span to read; it appears once, is written with kanji, and each of its tokens matches the word record or one of its forms. The rest of the line never contains a token of the same word, which would give the reading away. `en` is shown only after the answer.
- A sentence must be new to the learner. It may not repeat, sit inside, or contain a familiar line around the same word from any lesson, passage, question bank or dictionary entry (`familiarLine`). A line hardly longer than the word itself, such as お願いします, does not count. The app filters again at run time (`sentencesFor`), so a check never shows the sentence the word was learned from.
- Every kanji word that can fade (met with kanji in the reading text of a lesson or passage, `fadeableWords`) needs at least one sentence, and every target needs at least two distinct wrong readings to choose from.
- A look-alike pair is `{id, words: [a, b], kanji: [ka, kb], contrast}`: two different kanji words, the character compared in each (it must occur in that word, and may be the same character read differently, as in 定食 and 食べる), and a one-line contrast of at most 160 characters. Each pair is stored once. `lookAlikeIndex` lists it under both words with the characters in that word's order, and validation checks that the index is symmetric and that no pair is duplicated in either order. Both words need a sentence.
- Generated look-alikes from the kanji component catalog, and their exclusions (`lookAlikeExclusions`), were removed with the catalog (kanji Phase 3c, 2026-10-23). Only authored pairs remain.

Scheduling and evidence:

- A word enters the schedule when its reading has faded. It is due when it was last seen on an earlier day, at least 12 hours ago, and its `transfer.due` has passed. A word missed last time comes first, then words already in the schedule, then newly faded words.
- A session holds up to four due words. Each is followed by up to two look-alikes that the learner has met (`met` is set), up to eight items in total, with no word twice. Look-alikes are shared out one per word at a time, starting with the least recently seen. A look-alike the learner has never met is never tested. It appears only in feedback.
- Each item shows its sentence with the target marked and no reading. Every token belonging to a word in the session also loses its reading, and no meaning or translation is in the page until the answer. The learner chooses the reading from up to four (`readingChoices`): the reading in this sentence and plausible misreadings. A wrong choice that is a near miss (voicing, small kana, small っ or vowel length) is named in feedback (`readingSlip`) and is still wrong.
- Feedback gives the reading, then one line with the look-alike, its own reading and the contrast, then the translation and meaning.
- `recordTransferCheck` logs a check event with source `context:<sentenceId>` and updates `transfer = {step, due, at, ok, seen, fresh, passed}`. A miss clears the delayed evidence, so the reading is shown again, and sets the next check for 1 day later. A correct answer on a due check after a delay moves one step along 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, the same rhythm as the review queue. The first answer starts at 1 day. Answers before the due date change nothing. Correct delayed readings in two different sentences can fade a word, like two texts.
- `seen` lists answered sentence IDs (at most 20), least recently answered first. A word gets its first unanswered sentence. When all have been answered, the one answered longest ago returns. `fresh` counts delayed answers in a sentence answered for the first time, and `passed` counts the correct ones. This is the roadmap's new-context measure. The review's summary and empty state show it as “New-sentence readings so far: *passed* of *fresh* correct”, against the course aim of about 80%, and the Library's dictionary entries show the count for each word. It is evidence, not a mastery score.

### Grammar patterns

Some structures are tested in more than one lesson: `［N］を ください` in a café, a noodle shop and a bakery. `patterns.mjs` links those drills, so a miss in one lesson and another in a second lesson count as the same gap, and the structure is re-tested in a different lesson rather than with the prompt that failed.

- **Catalog.** `lessons.json` `patterns` is `[{id, title, contrast: {ja, en, why}}]`, authored in `tools/patterns.mjs` and written by the seed. IDs are lowercase with hyphens, such as `request-wo`, `counter-tsu`, `honorific-others` or `polite-past`; Bridge A (ba01, ba02) adds the rest of the verb family, `polite-nonpast`, `polite-past-negative`, `polite-negative` and `offer-mashouka`. The frame, the explanation and the lessons that teach a pattern are not repeated there: they come from the unit points whose `patternId` names it (`patternIndex`), the lessons' first, then those of unit practice sets (`{practice, point}`, since 2b: a lesson that became a practice kept its points, such as `de-option` in *Without onions, please*). Re-test items come from lessons only. Tagged drills come from `unitItems`, which gives each item its `patternId` (the item's own, else its set's). A point's tag does not carry to its drills: tag only the drills that test the structure.
- **Checks.** `tools/validate.mjs` fails on an unknown or malformed `patternId`, a catalog entry without a teaching point, a missing title or contrast, and kanji without readings; a pattern drilled in fewer than two lessons is a warning, because no re-test can come from another lesson. `patternCoverage` lists, for each pattern, the topics (lesson `node`s) whose drills test it. A pattern drilled in one topic only, a unit with no tagged drill, and a unit whose patterns are all drilled in its own topic only are warnings too, because the re-test could not change venue. It also checks that misses in two lessons queue a re-test from another prompt and that a record survives a strict restore.
- **Misses.** `state.patterns` is `{version: 1, records: {[patternId]: {misses, total, due, retests, transferred, seen, reasons?}}}`. A miss is a first-try miss of a tagged item in a lesson's drills (`drill`, the moment the item's result first becomes `wrong`), in a closed-book lesson review (`review`) in a re-test (`retest`) or in a cumulative checkpoint (`checkpoint`, with the checkpoint item’s ID; see [Checkpoints](#checkpoints)), or a right drill or review answer with a wrong reason ([Reason checks](#reason-checks)), which also adds one to `reasons` (kept only once there is one; never more than `total`): `{lesson, item, source, at}`, at most 20 open. The same item from the same source counts once while the record is open, so repeated taps and reloads add nothing. `total` counts every miss ever recorded.
- **Trigger.** When the open misses come from two different lessons (`TRIGGER_LESSONS`), `due` is set to one day after that miss (`RETEST_DELAY`). Two misses in one lesson never trigger, and later misses never postpone a due date. A checkpoint miss sets the due date at once: it is already a cross-venue retrieval after other lessons. Both values are product defaults to evaluate with learners.
- **Re-test (parked 2026-10-22).** Misses are still recorded and `due` still set, but with Today parked no page runs a re-test and no message promises one. The design: a due pattern is one Today unit, `pattern:<patternId>`, interleaved and capped with the other units. `pickRetest` chooses the drill when the run is planned (`item: {lesson, item}` in the unit): a tagged item from a lesson the learner has started, never the lesson of the latest miss, never an item missed in this round, never an item that today's due lesson reviews will show, and never a prompt already shown twice (`PROMPT_MAX`: the lesson drill, a closed-book review and earlier re-tests each count once). Lessons the pattern was not missed in come first, then prompts not yet shown, then another topic, then course order. With no eligible drill the pattern waits and the progress view says why; an identical prompt is never substituted. The drill is shown closed book with one try and without the pattern's name; its lesson progress is untouched. Correct clears `misses` and `due` and sets `transferred`; `total` and `retests` (`[{lesson, item, at, correct}]`, at most 20) stay as history. A miss is added to `misses` and the pattern is due again a day later, never retried at once. The unit's `revision` changes only when the re-test is queued, answered or rescheduled.
- **Note.** After a miss on a tagged drill, its feedback offers **Pattern note** (in a closed-book review, once the review is finished). The note is one screen: the frame and explanation of the first point that teaches it, the catalog contrast, and links to every lesson that teaches it. It is reached only from a mistake, is never scored, and records `seen`. Closing it returns focus to the button that opened it.
- **Progress.** The Library's progress section lists each pattern with the strongest evidence state from [content-standard.md](content-standard.md#assessment-and-progression-evidence): Seen (note or a teaching lesson opened), Practised (a tagged drill answered), Retrieved (right at the first try), Retained (right in a closed-book review), Transferred (a re-test right). Independently demonstrated needs an unfamiliar task with a rubric and is never reached by these drills. Open misses and the re-test date are shown apart from the state.
- **Restore.** `readPatternState` rejects, in a backup, unknown patterns, lessons or items, malformed events, more than 20 misses or re-tests, a due date without open misses and a total below the open misses; local state drops what it cannot use.
- **Unseen texts.** A question that tests the pattern in an unseen text and was answered right from memory, on the first reading or at a qualifying delayed check, is Transferred evidence too, shown on its own line (*Unseen texts: …*). It never clears open misses; only a re-test does.

### Unseen texts

`state.transfer` is `{version: 1, texts: {[textId]: {read?, attempts, draft?, delayed?, rereads?}}}` (`transfer.mjs`), kept apart from lesson scores and passage recall.

- **Opening (Phase 3, 2026-10-23).** A text opens on its own `requires`, like any book (`itemState` in `loop.mjs`): with its lessons finished and its books completed, and no delayed recall. It is a blank cover once every prerequisite is started, and a text with any record stays open. A text that is not open has no page: its `#unseen/<id>` address returns to Reading. The delayed-recall gate (`transferAccess`, `passageReached`) is gone.
- **First reading.** `read` is the time the text was first shown, its one unseen exposure; a reload during the reading keeps it. *Done reading* starts the draft (`{phase, started, mode, answers, lines, supportFrom?}`), and from then on the text is not in the page. Recall items get one first response each, with no feedback until the attempt is finished. Looking back opens the text and marks the rest of the attempt `supported` for good (`supportFrom`: the answers given before). Evidence-line picks come only after every recall answer, with the lines in view, and are scored apart.
- **Attempts.** `[{phase, started, at, mode, items, lines, supportFrom?, qualified?}]`: `initial`, `delayed` or `retry`; `items` are the recall answers `{q, choice, correct, words, patterns}`, each with the words and patterns its question tests, copied when recorded; `lines` are the evidence picks. Finishing records once; a repeated finish or a reload adds nothing. Rereading after the first attempt adds to `rereads` and is labelled a revisit, never an unseen reading. At most 10 attempts are kept (`ATTEMPTS_MAX`): the oldest retries are dropped first, never the `initial` or `delayed` attempt.
- **Retries (Phase 3, 2026-10-23).** After the first attempt, the result offers **Try again** until the text is completed, with no limit and no wait. (While the delayed check is due, the text's page opens on that check, and its result offers Try again after it.) It records a fresh read in `rereads` and shows the text again, then asks the first-reading (immediate) set from memory as phase `retry`, with its choices in a new order (seeded with the try's start). A retry leaves the delayed re-ask as it is, never counts as transfer evidence (`transferEvidence` and `transferSummary` skip it), and does not move the Today revision. A closed retry with everything right completes the text (`completesText`), as a closed first reading does; the delayed check never does. A read for a retry is a sighting, so a delayed check less than a day after it counts as practice, as after any reread.
- **Delayed check.** Finishing the first attempt sets `delayed.due` once: at least a day later (the scheduler's first interval) and on a later local calendar date. With Today parked (2026-10-22) it is taken on the text's own page. It was also one Today unit, `transfer:<textId>`, while due, with the run's cap, interleaving and resume; its revision moves only when it is answered, so an answer given on the text's page skips a snapshot unit as resolved. It asks the delayed set with the text hidden. Finishing it sets `delayed.done` whatever the score: misses stay as evidence and nothing is queued again. `qualified` is true only for a closed attempt at least a day, and on a later calendar date, after the learner last saw the text (the first reading, a reveal of results or a reread). Unseen texts are not in the scheduler's outcome ledger.
- **Evidence.** `transferEvidence` gives, per word and pattern, answers right from memory on a first reading (a new context), at a qualifying delayed check (recall of the now-familiar text), and practice (the text open, or a check that did not qualify), counting only the words and patterns each question tests. Other words in a text count as exposure only. The progress page shows a summary row, each pattern its line, and a word's card its line, marked as understanding in context: none of it is a kanji reading check.
- **Restore.** `readTransferState` rejects, in a backup, an unknown text, extra fields, answers that do not match the key, unknown words or patterns in targets, more than one first or delayed attempt, a delayed or retry attempt without a first one, more than 10 attempts, a missing or impossible re-ask, and a draft for a phase that cannot start (a retry draft needs the first attempt); local state drops what it cannot use. Retry attempts and drafts round-trip.

### Recovery

**Parked (2026-10-22).** No page shows recovery: the recovery callouts (“comes back in Today”) and recovery units are gone with Today. The field is still migrated, exported and validated, and the rules below stay for the later review design.

`state.recovery` (`schedule.mjs`) is `{version: 1, since, items}`, keyed like Today units (`word:<prompt>`, `reading:<word>`, `phrase:<harvestId>`, `passage:<id>`, `lesson:<id>`). Each entry is `{lapses, done, episode?}`: eligible lapses since the item entered the schedule or last recovered, and completed episodes. Lifetime lapse totals stay in each source record.

- **Entry.** Every applied answer is logged in the outcome ledger; on each commit the app counts the new entries whose effect is `lapsed` and that are `eligible` (due, not an in-session repeat), except those with venue `recovery`. The third opens `episode = {since, venue, origin: 'lapse', route, last, streak: 0, misses: 0, seen: []}`. A lapse during an episode changes nothing.
- **Delivery.** While an episode is open the item is out of every normal venue: Today's own unit, deck sessions (`reviewQueue` lists it under `recovering`; `dropRecovering` removes it from a session in progress), the lesson page's review, the passage page's delayed set, the phrase page and the kanji page (`transferSession` `skip`). From `last + 1 day` it is due in Today with prompts from `recovery.mjs`: a word card as the word marked in a sentence from an opened lesson or reading (or its dictionary example when the card never shows it), asked for its meaning; a kanji reading in a check sentence it was never asked in, or a sentence from an opened text; a phrase in one of its recovery sentences; a passage as its idea-linked phrases in recovery sentences, one each; a lesson review as one drill per missed grammar pattern (its last review's wrong items, then open pattern misses from that lesson) from another started unit. A prompt the episode used is never reused.
- **Completion.** `answerRecovery` counts only an unassisted, non-repeat answer on unused prompts at least a day after the preceding qualifying attempt. A miss resets `streak` and the wait. The second success closes the episode (`{lapses: 0, done: n + 1}`) and that one answer is credited to the item's own schedule as a correct, due review; the first success and every prompt's own source (another unit's drill, another phrase) are never credited.
- **Blocked.** An item with no usable prompt (`no-content`, `no-evidence`, `not-started`, `exhausted`) is listed on Today as needing recovery content, with the reason, and is not review debt.
- **Migration.** A save or backup without `recovery` is seeded once (`migrateLeeches`): each scheduled item's known lifetime lapses become its count; three or more open an episode with `origin: 'migrated'`, `venue: null` and `last` set to the record's own last attempt, or the migration time when it keeps none. `since` records when tracking began, so the seeding never repeats.
- **Restore.** `readRecovery` rejects unknown keys, malformed episodes, a route that does not match the type, repeated prompts and a count of three without an episode.

### Today runs

**Parked (2026-10-22).** There is no Today page: `#today` replaces itself with `#field`, the sidebar has no Today entry and Practice has no Today koi. No page builds or shows a run, and the review-debt gate holds no lesson (lessons lock only by prerequisites and due checkpoints; [Course progression](#course-progression)). `session.mjs`, its tests and the `daily` field (still read and validated on load and restore) stay as described below.

`session.mjs` gathers every due review into one run (`#today`). Each source keeps its own scheduler, evidence and assessment rules; Today only reads the due queues, orders a snapshot, tracks the run and records it as its own evidence line.

- **Units.** One unit is one review task, keyed `<type>:<ref>`: `lesson` (a lesson review: about six closed-book items), `word` (a flashcard prompt), `reading` (a kanji word, in a new check sentence or in a sentence from a text), `passage` (the whole delayed recall set), `phrase` (a harvested phrase), `pattern` (a re-test drill from another lesson; see [Grammar patterns](#grammar-patterns)) and `transfer` (an unseen text's one delayed check; see [Unseen texts](#unseen-texts)). `dueUnits` lists every due unit; only familiar, scheduled items count, never unseen words, early practice or retries. Each unit carries a `revision` that changes whenever its source records anything.
- **Order and cap.** `interleave` takes each source oldest first and, at every step, the oldest head from a source other than the previous one. `planRun` keeps the first `limit` units (1–50, 20 by default); the rest is carry-over and stays due.
- **The run.** `daily` is `{version: 1, limit, started, active, history}`. `active` is `{id, started, limit, cursor, units, carry}`; each unit is `pending`, `done` or `skipped`, and the unit on screen may hold a `step` (a word card's reveal and hint, a passage's mode and answers) so a reload resumes it. A lesson review keeps its answers with the lesson in `state.answers`, so it needs no step. `recordOutcome` applies once, with `result` `correct`, `missed` or `assisted` and an `outcome` per type: `passed` or `failed` with a score for lessons, the rating for words, `correct` or `incorrect` for readings, phrases and pattern re-tests, `closed` or `supported` with a score for passages and unseen texts (whose answers live in the text's own draft, so their step only marks the unit started). Ending early (`abandonRun`) leaves unfinished units due. On resume, `revalidate` skips a pending unit answered elsewhere (`resolved`) or no longer in the course or decks (`removed`) and records nothing for it. `history` keeps at most 100 finished or abandoned runs with counts per type.
- **Review-debt gate.** New lessons wait while 20 or more review units are due (`DEBT_LIMIT`); the gate says how many to complete. A lesson already started (opened while allowed, answered or finished; `started` records the first opening) and every review stay open.
- **Recovery units.** An item in a recovery episode keeps its own key and type, carries `item: {recovery: [promptId…]}` (one to three prompts) and keeps its answers in `step.answers` (`[{p, choice}]`, or `[{p, ok}]` for drills), in prompt order. Its revision follows the episode, so an answer given elsewhere skips it on resume; a normal unit of an item that has since entered recovery is skipped the same way. Word recovery outcomes are `correct`/`incorrect`; a passage round is `closed` and a lesson round `passed`/`failed`, both with a score. History units keep `recovery: true`. The ledger entry uses venue `recovery`.
- **Restore.** `readDailyState` rejects malformed runs, counts that do not match their units, an out-of-range cursor and unknown lessons. Older saves keep their typed-answer lesson outcomes readable in history and drop units of the removed `reply`, `component` and `meaning` types.

### Retention report and forecast

**Parked (2026-10-22).** The Retention page and Today's forecast strip are gone with Today, and `#retention` replaces itself with `#field`. `retention.mjs`, `outcomeReport`, `workloadForecast` and their tests stay for the later review design.

`#retention` (linked from Today) read saved state only; nothing on it wrote or rescheduled a record. Three parts stay apart:

- **Measured (`schedule.mjs` `outcomeReport`).** The rolling 30-day window ending now, both ends included, from the outcome ledger alone; nothing is inferred from per-source counters. `sources` has one row per ledger type: `eligible` (denominator: due, first, unassisted answers), `recalled` (numerator) and `rate` (null with no denominator). A lesson review counts as one set that passes with at most one miss (`unit: 'batch'`), never as per-question accuracy; word cards are `basis: 'self-rated'`. Assisted answers (`supported`), repeats and early answers (`practice`), lesson lapses routed from a checkpoint (`routed`) and answers with venue `recovery` (`recovery`, also totalled) stay outside the rate. A repeated submission has the same ledger id and is logged once. `coverage` says when the ledger began after the window opened (a save or backup from before the ledger starts it at load or restore) or dropped entries inside it.
- **Estimated (`session.mjs` `workloadForecast`, `retention.mjs` `recallRisk`).** Days are the device's local calendar days (`localDays`; 23 or 25 hours across a clock change). `scheduled` reads `dueUnits` at each day's end, so it counts what Today counts (same keys; a lesson review is one unit; blocked recovery left out) and overdue work stays on day 0. `simulated` adds returns inside the week if each unit were answered correctly on its day, through `review()` and the recovery rule; pattern re-tests and unseen texts do not return; kanji words re-checked in texts without a scheduler memory are `unmodelled`. `runOutlook` spreads the forecast over one run a day at the learner's cap. `recallRisk` counts model-scheduled items, not in recovery, estimated below the 0.90 target now or before the week ends. All are labeled estimates.
- **Recovery (`retention.mjs` `recoveryRows`).** One row per open episode: due, waiting (next check) or blocked (reason), with where it kept failing, lifetime lapses and the next step.

## Notices

Since 2026-10-28 every unlock is also announced in a small popup, a notice (`notices.mjs`, styled by `notices.css`). Notices add to the finish screens' unlock lists and the lesson-part line; they replace neither, and the `toast()` lines stay as they were.

A notice is text only: what opened and where it lives. It holds no link and no button but its close button, and never says *Go to*, *Open* or *Next*. Tapping anywhere on it only dismisses it.

```js
{id:'practice:everyday-dishes', // dedupe key `${kind}:${ref}`; a kanji notice uses its sorted word ids joined by ','
 kind:'practice',               // stone | lesson | checkpoint | practice | book | kanji-round | kanji
 title:'New practice',          // small-caps eyebrow
 name:'Everyday dishes',        // what opened
 where:'Visit the koi pond.',   // where it lives, one calm sentence
 ja:false}                      // true when name is Japanese (kanji notices), rendered with lang="ja"
```

| kind | glyph | title | name | where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stone | ◈ | New stone | topic title | In the garden. |
| lesson | ◈ | New lesson | lesson title | In Learn · *topic*. |
| checkpoint | ◈ | Checkpoint *n* | A short closed-book check | In Learn · *topic of the lesson it sits before* (In Learn. when unknown) |
| practice | ▤ | New practice | practice title | Visit the koi pond. |
| book | ▧ | New book | book title | On the reading table. |
| kanji-round | 字 | Kanji cards open | Your first round of nine | In Kanji. |
| kanji | 字 | New kanji | each word as *written reading*, joined by ` · ` (`お水 おみず · お茶 おちゃ`) | Added to your kanji words. |

Plain words: no exclamation marks, emoji, congratulations, counts of what is left or *Next:*.

- **Model.** `unlockNotices(u,names)` turns an `unlockedSince` result into notices in the order stones, lessons, checkpoint, practices, books, Kanji round; `names` resolves ids to titles and a null skips the item. `kanjiNotice(words)` makes one notice for a batch of met words (none for an empty batch). `noticeQueue` is the pure queue.
- **Renderer.** `createNotices(root)` over `#notices` (index.html, right after `#toast`, `role="status"` and `aria-live="polite"`; a fixed overlay outside `#main`, so notices survive route changes; nothing is added to the sidebar). At most 3 show at once (`NOTICE_MAX`), the rest wait in order; a batch enters 350 ms apart (`NOTICE_GAP`); each stays 7 s (`NOTICE_MS`), paused while hovered or focused; tap or Escape inside dismisses it. An id already shown is ignored for the page's lifetime. Strings are escaped. With reduced motion the notices fade only. A null root makes every method a no-op.
- **Where app.js fires them.** Each place that computes `unlockedSince(before)` announces the same result (`announceUnlocks`): a lesson's first finish (`#mark-practiced`), a checkpoint's finish, a book's first completion, an unseen text's first completion and a practice set's first finish. The answer that completes a lesson part announces the practices and books it opened (`partsOpened`), and the Kanji page's first round comes with `unlockedSince` (`kanji`). New kanji words: at each `kanjiWordsAnswer` caller (lesson and practice drills, the reading check), before the store commits, the ids in the new `kanjiWords.words` that the saved store lacks, kept to words in the vocabulary, are named in one New kanji notice (`announceMet`, written and reading by `plainWritten`/`plainReading`).
- **Tests.** `tests/notices.test.mjs` (the model and queue), `tests/notices-ui.browser.test.mjs` (the renderer alone) and `tests/notices.browser.test.mjs` (in the app: one notice per finish-screen unlock item with its kind and place, no link, tap dismisses; a first kanji drill answer names the words met).
