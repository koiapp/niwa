# Niwa: a Japanese curriculum that grows with you

Working design, updated 25 October 2026. Focus: everyday life in Japan. Pace: 20–30 minutes most days. Starting point: hiragana and katakana, with no assumed kanji.

## The central decision

Organize the visible curriculum by what you want to do. Inside each situation, deliberately teach the language systems that make it work. A restaurant is a small course, with a core visit, complications, connected conversation, reading, and later vocabulary rounds. The learner should be able to explain a choice and adapt it to a new turn.

There are two distinct kinds of growth:

1. **Greater conversational depth:** more independent understanding of replies, longer turns, reasons, stories, inference, tact, and repair.
2. **Greater vocabulary breadth:** more foods, ingredients, places, descriptions, and personal interests, reused in meaningful tasks.

The map shows topic nodes and their entry levels. It should not imply that every part of a node belongs to that entry level. A restaurant can begin early and remain useful years later. The stones stand alone, with no connecting lines. A new learner sees Basic sentences only. Starting prerequisite lessons reveals the next stones as unnamed shapes; their titles and kanji appear when a lesson inside opens. Topics without playable lessons stay hidden.

## What this kit contains

- A topic catalogue, personal levels and garden rows; the [current inventory](content-inventory.md) separates implemented content from unwritten titles.
- A 48-lesson restaurant outline across four layers, plus four vocabulary expansions.
- Playable textbook lessons with general practices, optional practice branches, connected readings, unseen transfer texts and contextual vocabulary. [Current counts and pacing tables](content-inventory.md) are generated from the app data.
- A working field explorer with sliding topic panels, tap and drag drills with one-line feedback, lessons and runs shown one page at a time with a Back and Next dock, lessons and checkpoints that unlock in place as earlier ones are finished, practice sets and books that open on their own prerequisites, and a Kanji page with a daily round of nine cards from the kanji words the learner has met.
- Requirements, content standards, assessment rules, and reusable authoring templates.

Catalogue titles include written lessons, titles converted to optional practices and unwritten plans; they are not a count of finished lessons. Each lesson introduces words and patterns, checks their meaning and use with a few varied exercises, then closes with a conversation and a short mixed review. Further repetition belongs mainly in Practice. It is shown like turning the pages of that workbook, one page at a time, never as one long scroll. Every exercise is answered by tapping or dragging; there is no typing or speaking practice, and audio is not implemented (the owner may later add it to a few selected exercises). Independent assessment is a requirement for the future product. The prototype checks authored answers and saves progress in this browser. Its teaching samples remain drafts pending a fluent Japanese educator’s review.

## Five rules worth preserving

1. **A dense node must not become a bottleneck.** Completing its core stage lets you enter other topics. Return for deeper stages when the relevant shared language is ready.
2. **Lessons check understanding.** Each pattern gets a short explanation and a few distinct checks; Practice supplies repeated retrieval. Avoid noun swaps and repeated formats that test the same decision. Every lesson addresses intention, structure, context, alternatives, and transfer.
3. **Results describe what was tested.** Tap and drag exercises show reading, recognition, and sentence building in written Japanese. They cannot establish that you can follow natural speech or sustain a spoken conversation.
4. **Kanji is learned as words you can read.** Each lesson introduces one to three written words, by meaning and then reading, in a sentence. Text shows a word in kana until you have met it, then with the kanji small above the kana, then in kanji once you have passed its daily trial three times on the Kanji page. Readings are always a tap away. Nothing is split into parts, and nothing is handwritten. The course profile, the classroom-support build ([builds.md](builds.md)), will follow its textbook's kanji order with its own trial rules.
5. **Progress represents evidence.** “Seen,” “practiced,” “retrieved later,” and “used independently” are different states.

## Where to begin

Start with **Basic sentences**, then **Restaurants**. Finish Ask for something, Ask someone to repeat, and Numbers, prices, and quantities. Starting the third lesson reveals the Restaurants outline; finishing it opens Restaurants at A table for two. Meeting people and Shopping follow that first restaurant lesson in the current draft. Follow the lesson rows' prerequisites and checkpoints: topic lists follow teaching order, and unwritten titles are kept under Planned lessons.

The course runs as one **learning loop** ([learning loop](learning-loop.md)). Lessons are the start point and the focus. They introduce a topic's core words and grammar. Practices provide repeated retrieval and expand the lessons with more vocabulary, more use of the grammar and more everyday situations. Books in Library → Reading test what you retain. A practice or a book can require any lessons, practices or books, so the two kinds build on each other until a branch needs a new lesson to go further.

The loop is built. Starting prerequisites reveals unnamed koi and books, all requirements must be met before they open, and a book completes only after a mistake-free attempt without reopening the text. Every lesson is the home of at least one general practice and one general book. Optional content is welcome: some activities are meant to be more optional than others, for niche, less general situations. Optional content is always an optional practice, which can unlock optional books, never an optional lesson. Dating and relationships is optional practices on that basis, under *Invite someone*, and has no stone. In the garden an outline has no name or kanji and cannot be clicked, and opens when any of its lessons opens. Already-started work remains accessible.

The app works by **progressive unlock, with no redirects**. Learn, Practice and Library (usually in that order, though any order works) lay out what you can do now, in place. Finishing a lesson, checkpoint or practice set unlocks the next thing, and its finish screen shows what opened, with one way back to the topic. A locked lesson shows where it sits and what opens it; no page sends you elsewhere with “Go to” or “Next” buttons, and there is no Today page. The optional due lesson and book reviews were removed on 2026-10-29 at the owner's direction; reviews, Today and review-debt gating stay parked ([requirements](requirements.md#progressive-unlock-no-redirects)).

In a typical 25-minute session, spend five minutes on passage recall on a book's page in Reading, fifteen to seventeen on a lesson or its next part, and the rest checking one item again and noting gaps. A lesson can be left on any page: the page and answers are saved on the device. These are adjustable design defaults, not measured study times or a forecast of fluency.

## A realistic definition of topic confidence

“Fairly fluent within a realm” becomes a set of observable performances: follow familiar and unfamiliar replies, produce connected turns, ask relevant follow-ups, handle changes, clarify unknown words, and read realistic material. Repeated successful performance across partners and situations is the target. One completion badge is insufficient evidence.

“All foods” and “all small talk” are open-ended. Cover high-frequency categories systematically, teach ways to ask about unfamiliar things, and let personal vocabulary packs grow without a fixed ceiling. This keeps the original ambition while making the curriculum authorable.

## Reference points

The [Japan Foundation Standard](https://www.jfstandard.jpf.go.jp/summaryen/ja/render.do) organizes objectives around communicative tasks. That supports the can-do structure here. Niwa’s six levels and progression rules are original design proposals, with no official CEFR or JLPT equivalence.

[Irodori](https://www.irodori.jpf.go.jp/en/about.html) is a useful reference for everyday-life situations and the integration of practical tasks, audio, reading, and language knowledge. Niwa’s prose, dialogues, passages, and exercises are newly authored; external teaching materials are linked rather than reproduced.
