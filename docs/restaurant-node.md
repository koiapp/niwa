# Restaurant node: a small course with a long life

## Destination

The learner can complete a meal out, follow service questions, clarify unfamiliar food, discuss preferences and experiences, and interpret written restaurant information. They can recover when something changes. Confidence grows through many varied conversations and restaurant texts, not through memorizing a closed list of menu nouns.

Entry: basic requests, questions, prices, and repair. The node begins at personal level 1 and extends into levels 4–5. Its first stage can open other topics before the deeper stages are finished.

## Four layers, 48 planned lessons

Each lesson is a textbook unit ([content-standard.md](content-standard.md#a-complete-lesson)), and every exit task and the capstone use the same tap and drag formats: the learner chooses each reply, builds lines from tiles, and completes exchanges from a word bank. Nothing is spoken or typed.

### 1. Core visit: a complete supported exchange

1. A table for two
2. Ask for the menu
3. Which dish is that?
4. Build an order
5. One, two, and another one
6. Is this a set?
7. How much is it?
8. Listen for the follow-up
9. Ask for another try
10. Ask for the bill
11. Can I pay by card?
12. A complete simple visit

Exit task: arrive, give party size, request a menu, order two items, answer a simple follow-up, check payment, and close the exchange. Use unfamiliar item/price details in the assessment. A cooperative partner and full reading support are acceptable at this stage.

### 2. Changes and surprises: reasons, options, and repair

1. What is in the dish?
2. Preferences and restrictions
3. Without onions, please
4. Small, regular, or large?
5. Something is sold out
6. Which would you recommend?
7. Is the drink included?
8. The order is different
9. How long is the wait?
10. Making a reservation
11. Changing a reservation
12. A visit with a complication

Exit task: make a request with a constraint, understand a limitation, and agree on an alternative. Teach preferences, avoidance, intolerances, and allergies as distinct communication situations. A “without X” request does not establish ingredient absence or cross-contact safety. Any safety-critical material needs qualified review before release.

### 3. Conversation around food: connected personal meaning

1. Say what you enjoy
2. Explain your preference
3. Compare two dishes
4. Ask how it is made
5. Local and seasonal food
6. What do you usually eat?
7. Invite someone to try it
8. Accept or decline gently
9. My first time eating this
10. Tell a food story
11. Notice when to stop chatting
12. A meal with a friend

Exit task: sustain a connected conversation about a meal, with reasons, follow-ups, and an experience. Choose an appropriate partner: a friend over a meal and busy staff at a counter afford different conversations.

### 4. Independent dining: discourse and unfamiliar situations

1. Read a restaurant message
2. Read a detailed review
3. Fact, opinion, and implication
4. Understand a course menu
5. Ask about an unfamiliar dish
6. A careful dietary clarification
7. Separate bills and group payment
8. Resolve a billing question
9. Recognize formal service language
10. Explain a special request
11. Write a useful restaurant review
12. An unfamiliar restaurant capstone

Exit task: an unfamiliar 8–12 turn exchange with two changes, completed by choosing each reply, plus interpretation of a connected text. Repeat later with a different venue, partner, or menu. Do not grade the learner on building staff honorific formulas merely because they can recognize them.

## The + rounds

| Round | Breadth | Application |
|---|---|---|
| +1 Everyday dishes | Rice, noodles, soup, set meals, common proteins, vegetables, drinks, and useful dish names | Recombine known ordering and availability frames; learn the small bridge for “included” |
| +2 Ingredients and preparation | Broth, sauces, cooking processes, texture, temperature, portion, and doneness | Ask how something is made, compare, and explain a preference |
| +3 Places and specialties | Cafés, bakeries, noodle shops, family restaurants, izakaya menus, regional and seasonal foods | Interpret a less familiar menu and ask about an unknown dish |
| +4 Food as conversation | Memories, hospitality, evaluations, regional vocabulary, and personal interests | A longer discussion, an evidence-based review, and a personal account |

+1 is implemented as the *Everyday dishes* practice set. Later expansions are curriculum plans, though some of the restaurant practices written for the learning loop already reach into them (tastes and ingredients in *Sweet, salty, and what’s in it*, places to eat in *Soba or a restaurant? Choosing with friends*). A vocabulary round should mainly extend breadth; if it needs substantially new grammar or discourse, add a clearly named bridge or stage first.

## A coverage system instead of a finite “all foods” list

Track vocabulary through a matrix:

- **Food families:** grains and starches; noodles; vegetables; mushrooms; beans; eggs; meat; fish and seafood; dairy; fruit; sweets; drinks.
- **Dish types:** soups; bowls; set meals; grilled, steamed, simmered, raw, fried, and baked dishes.
- **Ingredients and components:** stock, sauces, seasonings, toppings, fillings, sides, and garnishes.
- **Descriptions:** flavor, texture, temperature, degree, portion, and comparison.
- **Venue and service:** table service, counters, ticket machines, tablets, takeaway, reservations, courses, and shared meals.
- **Personal interests:** regional foods, cuisines encountered locally, favorite ingredients, and the learner’s own dietary vocabulary.

Each entry needs a reading, meaning in context, example, collocation, category, and source/review status. Add labels for recognition-only versus active production. Start a session with approximately 6–8 new lexical chunks; that is a tunable planning constraint, not an evidence-based limit for every learner.

Unknown food should trigger strategies: ask what it is, what it contains, how it is cooked, what it resembles, and whether a pictured dish is the one being discussed. These strategies make a finite starting vocabulary useful in an open-ended world.

## Small talk coverage

Teach conversational functions rather than scripts for every possible topic:

| Function | Example intention | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| React | Show interest in a recommended dish | Brief response |
| Elaborate | Explain why a flavor appeals to you | Reason + detail |
| Follow up | Ask about a preparation method just mentioned | Contingent question |
| Compare | Contrast today’s meal with another experience | Two linked ideas |
| Narrate | Describe the first time you tried a dish | Sequence + evaluation |
| Negotiate | Balance two diners’ preferences | Alternatives + agreement |
| Reflect | Discuss hospitality or personal habits | Viewpoint + qualification |
| Close | Thank a host or end a staff exchange gracefully | Context-sensitive ending |

Availability, price, payment, opening hours, ingredients, recommendations, atmosphere, travel to the venue, and meal plans can overlap with other nodes. Link to shared lessons rather than pretending each topic is sealed off.

## Reading progression

Begin with a menu plus a short exchange. Move to a short account of a changed order, then a reservation message with conditions, then a multi-paragraph review whose interpretation depends on context. Ask for gist, details, reference, inference, evidence, and a response. Translation remains optional support.

The ten original restaurant passages in the prototype (p01–p10) demonstrate this progression, from a lunch menu card (p05) and a sign by the register (p07) to a reservation message with conditions (p02) and a review of a restaurant worth returning to (p03). Since the learning loop's Phase 4 refit (2026-10-23) each is built from words its chain teaches, introduces at most one glossed word, and opens on its own prerequisites, so the reservation message waits for pl04 *Running late* and the review for r08 *Tell a food story* ([learning-loop.md](learning-loop.md#books)).

## Capstone rubric

Score each dimension 0–3: not yet achieved, heavy support, occasional support, independent in this task. Observe task completion, comprehension, interaction, repair, and appropriateness. A critical misunderstanding about an order or restriction requires repair, regardless of the total score. Store the evidence and conditions; do not compress everything into a single fluency percentage.
